## Questions 1-6: Passage about Sunscreen Effectiveness

**Study 1: SPF Protection Duration**

Dermatologists at the University of Miami conducted a study on sunscreen effectiveness. Twenty participants applied SPF 50 sunscreen and were exposed to simulated sunlight. Skin damage was measured under UV photography at intervals.

**Results:** Participants showed no visible skin damage for the first 2 hours. After 4 hours, 60% showed mild damage. After 6 hours, 95% showed significant damage.

C) Skin damage level  
D) Number of participants  
**[ ]** → **B**

## Questions 7-12: Passage about Exercise and Memory

**Study 2: Acute Exercise and Cognitive Function**

Researchers at the University of Georgia examined how acute aerobic exercise affects memory consolidation. Thirty college students were divided into three groups:

- Group A: 30 minutes of moderate cycling immediately before learning
- Group B: 30 minutes of moderate cycling 4 hours after learning  
- Group C: No exercise (control)

Memory was tested 48 hours later.

**Results:**

| Group | Recall Accuracy |
|-------|-----------------|
| A     | 78%             |
| B     | 65%             |
| C     | 58%             |

---

## Question 7 — D5

D) Groups A and B equal  
**[ ]** → **A**

## Questions 13-18: Passage about Honey Bee Navigation

**Study 3: Honey Bee Foraging Distance**

Biologists at Cornell University investigated how pesticide exposure affects honey bee foraging behavior. Two groups of bees were tagged and released 2 km from their hive.

- Exposed group: Bees previously exposed to neonicotinoid pesticides  
- Control group: Untreated bees

The number of bees returning within 24 hours was recorded.

**Results:**

| Group     | Return Rate | Average Return Time |
|-----------|-------------|---------------------|
| Exposed   | 45%         | 18 hours            |
| Control   | 82%         | 6 hours             |

## Questions 19-20: Conflicting Viewpoints on Climate Models

**Viewpoint 1: Climate Model Reliability**

Dr. James Hansen argues that current climate models are reliable predictors of future temperature changes. He points to successful retrodictions—models that accurately predicted past climate behavior—as evidence. "When models successfully hindcast known climate events," Hansen states, "their projections for the future gain credibility."

**Viewpoint 2: Climate Model Limitations**

Dr. Judith Curry counters that climate models have significant limitations. She notes that models disagree substantially on key variables like cloud feedback and aerosol effects. "Model intercomparison studies show a wide range of outcomes," Curry explains. "This uncertainty means policy decisions based on model projections should be made cautiously."

---

## Question 19 — D8
Dr. Hansen and Dr. Curry would most disagree about whether:

A) Climate models exist  
B) Climate models are sufficiently accurate for predictions  
C) Past climate events occurred  
D) The future can be predicted  
**[ ]** → **B**

---

## Question 20 — D9
To resolve the disagreement between the two viewpoints, scientists should:

A) Accept one scientist's opinion over the other  
B) Conduct additional studies on cloud feedback and aerosol effects  
C) Abandon climate models entirely  
D) Only use models that agree with past observations  
**[ ]** → **B**
