## Questions 7-12: Passage about the Louisiana Purchase

**Document Analysis: Jefferson's Justification**

When Thomas Jefferson considered acquiring the Louisiana Territory from France in 1803, he faced a constitutional dilemma. The Constitution contained no provision for purchasing territory from foreign nations. Nevertheless, Jefferson believed the acquisition served the nation's long-term interests.

Jefferson wrote to John Breckenridge: "The Constitution does not contain an express power to acquire territory, but the power may be derived...The good thus resulting...is of greater weight than any hair-brain scruple."

## Questions 13-18: Passage about the Dred Scott Decision

**Supreme Court Opinion, Dred Scott v. Sandford (1857)**

Chief Justice Roger Taney delivered the majority opinion: "The question is simply this: Can a negro, whose ancestors were imported into this country and sold as slaves, be a citizen of the United States?...We think they are not...They are not included in the political community formed by the Constitution."
