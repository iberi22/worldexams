## Questions 1-6: Passage about Chicago Architecture

Chicago's architectural heritage reflect the city's resilience. In 1871, the Great Chicago Fire destroyed much of the downtown area, leaving architects with an opportunity to rebuild from scratch. Rather than simply recreating what existed before, many chose to experiment with new materials like steel-frame construction. This decision ultimately shaped the skylines of cities worldwide.

The Home Insurance Building, completed in 1885, often cited as the world's first skyscraper. Its innovative steel skeleton allowed for greater height than traditional masonry buildings permitted. Architect William Le Baron Jenney is credited with this breakthrough, earning him the nickname "Father of the American Skyscraper."

[1] Modern Chicago continues to lead in sustainable design. [2] Buildings like the Bullitt Center produce more energy than they consume. [3] The result has been a new generation of eco-friendly architecture that honors the city's innovative spirit.
