## Questions 7-12: Passage about the Industrial Revolution

**Economic Analysis: The Gilded Age**

The period from 1870 to 1900, known as the Gilded Age, saw unprecedented industrial growth in America. Railroads expanded from 30,000 to nearly 200,000 miles. Steel production increased from 380,000 tons to over 10 million tons annually. Industrialists like Andrew Carnegie, John D. Rockefeller, and J.P. Morgan accumulated fortunes unprecedented in American history.

However, this growth came at significant social cost. Factory workers, including many children, labored 10-14 hour days in dangerous conditions. Urban slums housed millions of immigrants in cramped, unsanitary housing. By 1890, the top 1% of Americans controlled more wealth than the bottom 50%.

## Questions 13-18: Passage about World War I

**Historical Analysis: Causes of World War I**

The assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand in Sarajevo on June 28, 1914, triggered a cascade of events leading to World War I. However, historians argue that the assassination alone did not cause the war. Long-term factors, including militarism, alliances, imperialism, and nationalism (the "MAIAN" framework), had created a fragile international system.

Alliance systems divided Europe into two opposing camps: the Triple Entente (France, Britain, Russia) and the Central Powers (Germany, Austria-Hungary, Ottoman Empire). When Austria-Hungary declared war on Serbia, these alliances activated like "dominoes," pulling nation after nation into conflict. By August 1914, most of Europe was at war.
