**Bloom:** Remember
**EJE:** Gramatica
**Expected_Success:** 0.80
**Contexto:** Last Saturday our class visited the market in Otavalo and we bought fruit for the school fair.

### Enunciado
Choose the correct form of the verb 'visit' in the past simple.

### Opciones
- [x] A) Last Saturday our class visited the market in Otavalo.
  <!-- feedback: Correct! The regular past of 'visit' takes -ed: visit becomes visited, with no change to the pronunciation of the -ed. -->
- [ ] B) Last Saturday our class visit the market in Otavalo.
  <!-- feedback: 'Visit' is the base form and belongs to the present, so it cannot describe an action that happened last Saturday. -->
- [ ] C) Last Saturday our class visiteds the market in Otavalo.
  <!-- feedback: 'Visiteds' adds -ed to a base form that already ends in a consonant, which is not how English forms the past tense. -->
- [ ] D) Last Saturday our class visiting the market in Otavalo.
  <!-- feedback: 'Visiting' is an -ing form of the present continuous or the gerund, not the finite past form of the verb. -->

### Explicacion Pedagogica
A regular verb in the past simple takes -ed after the base form: work becomes worked, play becomes played, visit becomes visited. Only verbs ending in -e drop the final letter before -ed, and verbs ending in a consonant plus y change the -y for -ied: study becomes studied.

### Explicacion Pedagogica
Some verbs end in a consonant plus -ed and take no -ed at all, such as call called, like liked, want wanted and need needed. Their past form looks identical to the present simple, which makes them easier to learn but easy to misread when a sentence mixes tenses.
