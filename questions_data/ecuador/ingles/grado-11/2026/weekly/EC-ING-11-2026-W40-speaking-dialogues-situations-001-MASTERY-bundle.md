## Question 1 [D3]
**ID:** EC-ING-11-2026-W40-speaking-dialogues-situations-001-MASTERY-bundle-v1
**Bloom:** Apply
**EJE:** Speaking
**Expected_Success:** 0.80
**Contexto:** A student has entered a shop in Quito and wants to know the price of a jacket.

### Enunciado
Which question is the most natural way to open that conversation?

### Opciones
- [x] A) Excuse me, could you tell me how much this jacket costs?
  <!-- feedback: Correct! A polite opener followed by a direct request is exactly how a shop assistant is approached. -->
- [ ] B) How much is this jacket and why is it so expensive?
  <!-- feedback: No. It opens with a demand and then criticises the price, which a customer has no reason to do at the start. -->
- [ ] C) I want to buy this jacket in a different colour.
  <!-- feedback: No. It assumes a purchase and changes the subject before any information has been obtained. -->
- [ ] D) Is this the jacket you sold me last week?
  <!-- feedback: No. It refers to a previous visit that the situation does not include. -->

### Explicacion Pedagogica
A functional dialogue has conventions. Greeting or apologising first, then the request, is the pattern that speakers of a language expect, and it costs three or four words. Following it makes the second turn of the conversation possible, because the other person now knows what is being asked.

### Enunciado
Which utterance is the most natural way to offer somebody a piece of cake?

### Opciones
- [ ] A) Would you like to eat some cake?
  <!-- feedback: No. It asks whether the person would like to eat cake, which comments on their appetite rather than offering a piece. -->
- [x] B) Would you like a piece of cake?
  <!-- feedback: Correct! This is the standard offer, with the countable noun in its singular form after 'a piece of'. -->
- [ ] C) Do you like a piece of cake?
  <!-- feedback: No. It asks about a general preference, not about the piece being offered at that moment. -->
