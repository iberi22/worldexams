## Question 1 [D3]
**ID:** EC-ING-11-2026-W40-speaking-dialogues-situations-001-MASTERY-bundle-v1
**Bloom:** Apply
**EJE:** Expresion oral
**Expected_Success:** 0.85
**Contexto:** Situation: A classmate asks you for your notes from the lesson you missed yesterday.

### Enunciado
You cannot send the notes today. Which reply is the most appropriate?

### Opciones
- [ ] A) No.
  <!-- feedback: No. A single word refusal closes the conversation and leaves the classmate without an alternative, so the situation becomes impolite. -->
- [ ] B) You could just copy them from the board like everybody else does.
  <!-- feedback: No. The reply shifts the problem back to the classmate and says nothing about whether the notes can be shared at all. -->
- [x] C) I'm sorry, I haven't finished rewriting them yet. I can bring them tomorrow morning.
  <!-- feedback: Correct! It apologises, gives a reason and offers a clear alternative, which is what a friendly refusal with a solution sounds like. -->
- [ ] D) It is not that the notes are important, but that they are mine and I do not lend things.
  <!-- feedback: No. The sentence defends a position nobody raised and sounds confrontational in an ordinary classroom exchange. -->

### Explicacion Pedagogica
In a spoken exchange a refusal is expected to carry three parts: a brief apology, a reason and an offer. Dropping any one of them leaves the listener without a social route forward, which is what makes a reply sound cold.

### Explicacion Pedagogica
In a time pressure situation the effective line names the obstacle and asks for one specific action with a deadline attached. A complaint without a request gives the listener nothing to do, however true the complaint is.
