**Expected_Success:** 0.85
**Contexto:** Only one of the students in the group has won a national award.

### Enunciado
The student ___ won a national award will present the results.

### Opciones
- [ ] A) which
  <!-- feedback: No. 'Which' refers to things or animals in defining clauses, so it cannot stand in for a person such as the student here. -->
- [ ] B) whose
  <!-- feedback: No. 'Whose' expresses possession, and it would mean the award belongs to the student rather than that the student won it. -->
- [ ] C) where
  <!-- feedback: No. 'Where' introduces a clause about a place, and there is no place named in the sentence for it to refer to. -->
- [x] D) who
  <!-- feedback: Correct! 'Who' introduces a defining relative clause referring to a person, and the clause is essential to identify which student is meant. -->

### Explicacion Pedagogica
Defining relative clauses use who for people, which for things, and whose for possession. The clause is not optional: remove it and the sentence no longer identifies anyone, which is exactly what makes it defining rather than non-defining.
