## Question 1 [D3]
**ID:** EC-ING-11-2026-W39-writing-opinion-paragraph-001-MASTERY-bundle-v1
**Bloom:** Apply
**EJE:** Writing
**Expected_Success:** 0.80
**Contexto:** A student must write a paragraph of 80 to 100 words giving an opinion about public transport in the city.

### Enunciado
Which sentence opens a paragraph of opinion in the most suitable way?

### Opciones
- [x] A) In my opinion, the public transport in this city should be modernised.
  <!-- feedback: Correct! It states the writer's position clearly and directly, which is what a paragraph of opinion requires at the start. -->
- [ ] B) Public transport is a topic that people discuss a great deal.
  <!-- feedback: No. It only announces the subject and leaves the reader waiting for an opinion that never arrives. -->
- [ ] C) This paragraph is about modernising the buses of the city.
  <!-- feedback: No. It describes what the paragraph will do instead of taking up a position. -->
- [ ] D) Some people think that the buses here are old.
  <!-- feedback: No. It attributes a view to other people rather than presenting the writer's own argument. -->

### Explicacion Pedagogica
The first sentence of an opinion paragraph should name the writer's position. Formulae such as 'In my opinion', 'I firmly believe' or 'From my point of view' do that job in one line, and they save the reader from a paragraph that describes the issue without ever taking a side.

## Question 2 [D3]
**ID:** EC-ING-11-2026-W39-writing-opinion-paragraph-001-MASTERY-bundle-v2
**Bloom:** Apply
**EJE:** Writing
**Expected_Success:** 0.80
**Contexto:** The same student now has to support that opinion with reasons and finish the paragraph.

### Enunciado
Which connector best introduces the FIRST reason that supports the opinion?

### Opciones
- [ ] A) However,
  <!-- feedback: No. That connector signals a contrast, so it would be wrong before a reason that agrees with the opinion. -->
- [ ] B) For instance, although this is true,
  <!-- feedback: No. The expression carries a contrast as well, which contradicts the function of introducing support. -->
- [x] C) First of all,
  <!-- feedback: Correct! It opens a list of arguments and marks the first one clearly, which is the job required at that point. -->
- [ ] D) In conclusion,
  <!-- feedback: No. That connector belongs at the end of the paragraph, where the writer draws the argument together. -->

### Explicacion Pedagogica
Connectors are chosen by function, not by familiarity. Adders such as 'first of all', 'another reason' and 'moreover' open support, whereas 'however' and 'although' introduce contrast and 'in conclusion' closes. Reading the role before the wording prevents most connector mistakes.

Which connector introduces an example that illustrates the reason just given?

### Opciones
- [ ] A) Therefore,
  <!-- feedback: No. That connector draws a conclusion from what has already been said and does not introduce an illustration. -->
- [x] B) For example,
  <!-- feedback: Correct! It announces a concrete case that makes the general statement easier to understand. -->
- [ ] C) Nevertheless,
  <!-- feedback: No. That connector would reverse the direction of the argument and weaken the point. -->

**EJE:** Writing
**Expected_Success:** 0.80
**Contexto:** A student is writing about the value of learning a second language and has just presented two arguments in favour.

### Enunciado
Which connector should introduce the final sentence that restates the position?

### Opciones

  <!-- feedback: No. That connector opens a list of reasons, and by this point the list has already been presented. -->

  <!-- feedback: No. It rejects the previous statement, which is the opposite of what a closing sentence must do. -->

## Question 5 [D4]
**ID:** EC-ING-11-2026-W39-writing-opinion-paragraph-001-MASTERY-bundle-v5
**Bloom:** Analyze
**EJE:** Writing
**Expected_Success:** 0.80
**Contexto:** A student wrote: 'The city has many buses, however, the service is slow and uncomfortable.'

### Enunciado
What is the problem with this sentence?

### Opciones
- [ ] A) It expresses two points of view and should be split into two paragraphs.
  <!-- feedback: No. A contrast inside one sentence is perfectly normal, so splitting the sentence would not solve anything. -->
- [ ] B) It uses the wrong tense for a paragraph about the present.
  <!-- feedback: No. Every verb here is in the present tense, which is correct for this kind of text. -->
- [x] C) A comma cannot be placed before 'however' when it joins two independent clauses.
  <!-- feedback: No. The comma is required here, and the real problem lies elsewhere. -->
- [ ] D) The second clause is a reason against the first, not a contrast with it.
  <!-- feedback: Correct! The service is not the opposite of having many buses but a disadvantage of it, so the contrastive connector misrepresents the relationship. -->

### Explicacion Pedagogica
Contrast and cause are not interchangeable. A number of buses and slow service are not opposites; the second fact qualifies the first and shows a disadvantage of it, which is exactly what a connector such as 'although' or 'yet' can express without inventing an opposition.

## Question 6 [D4]
**ID:** EC-ING-11-2026-W39-writing-opinion-paragraph-001-MASTERY-bundle-v6
**Bloom:** Analyze
**EJE:** Writing
**Expected_Success:** 0.80
**Contexto:** A student wrote: 'Cycling to school is cheaper than taking the bus. Nevertheless, it takes longer and the weather is not always good.'

### Enunciado
Is the connector 'nevertheless' used correctly here?

### Opciones
- [x] A) Yes, because the second sentence introduces a disadvantage that contrasts with the advantage of the first.
  <!-- feedback: Correct! The connector signals a concession, and that is exactly the relationship between the two ideas. -->
- [ ] B) No, because the second sentence adds support to the opinion stated in the first.
  <!-- feedback: No. The second sentence introduces drawbacks and does not strengthen the argument. -->
- [ ] C) No, because a contrastive connector may only join two sentences with different subjects.
  <!-- feedback: No. A contrastive connector works between any two related ideas, whatever their subjects. -->
- [ ] D) No, because 'nevertheless' cannot be used in a paragraph written in the present tense.
  <!-- feedback: No. The connector is neutral with respect to tense and appears freely in present tense writing. -->

### Explicacion Pedagogica
A concessive connector states that a real disadvantage exists in spite of the argument. That is precisely the situation described here, since the saving is acknowledged and the costs are then set against it, which strengthens the paragraph without weakening the opinion.

**EJE:** Writing
**Expected_Success:** 0.80
**Contexto:** A student must argue that homework should be reduced and give two reasons plus one example.

### Enunciado
Which sentence supplies a reason for the opinion rather than an illustration of it?

### Opciones
- [ ] A) For example, a pupil in Quito finishes her tasks at midnight.
  <!-- feedback: No. It reports a particular case, which makes the preceding claim concrete instead of adding a new argument. -->
- [x] B) Long hours of homework reduce the time available for sport and rest.
  <!-- feedback: Correct! It states a general advantage of the proposal, which is what a reason does. -->
- [ ] C) In my opinion, homework should be reduced this year.
  <!-- feedback: No. It restates the position itself and contributes no support at all. -->

### Explicacion Pedagogica
A reason answers the question of why the opinion is held, and it must be general enough to apply beyond one case. An illustration answers a different question: it shows a concrete situation in which the reason operates, which is why the two are not interchangeable inside the same paragraph.

What is the main weakness of this paragraph as an answer to an opinion question?
