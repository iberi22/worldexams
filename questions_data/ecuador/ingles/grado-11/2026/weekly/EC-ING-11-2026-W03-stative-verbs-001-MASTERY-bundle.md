## Question 1 [D3]
**ID:** EC-ING-11-2026-W03-stative-verbs-001-MASTERY-v1
**Bloom:** Remember
**EJE:** Gramatica
**Expected_Success:** 0.80
**Contexto:** Your Ecuadorian pen friend describes the weather in the highlands: the mornings are cold and the afternoons are sunny.

### Enunciado
Which sentence uses 'to be' correctly with an adjective describing a state?

### Opciones
- [x] A) The mornings are cold and the afternoons are sunny.
  <!-- feedback: Correct! 'Are' agrees with the plural subjects 'the mornings' and 'the afternoons', which is what a plural subject needs. -->
- [ ] B) The mornings is cold and the afternoons are sunny.
  <!-- feedback: 'Is' agrees with a singular subject, but 'the mornings' is plural, so the verb should be 'are cold'. -->
- [ ] C) The mornings be cold and the afternoons be sunny.
  <!-- feedback: 'Be' is an infinitive and cannot open the clause; the present simple of 'to be' after a plural subject is 'are'. -->
- [ ] D) The mornings are cold and the afternoons is sunny.
  <!-- feedback: The first 'are' is right, but 'the afternoons' is also plural, so its verb should be 'are sunny' and not 'is sunny'. -->

### Explicacion Pedagogica
The verb 'to be' has three present forms: am with I, is with he, she, it and one singular noun, and are with you, we, they and every plural subject. Spanish speakers often over-apply 'is' because every other verb takes -s in the singular, but 'are' is the verb for any plural subject.

### Explicacion Pedagogica
Uncountable nouns take 'there is' and are quantified with 'much' or 'a lot of'; countable plural nouns take 'there are' and are quantified with 'many' or 'a few'. Pairing the quantifier with the right noun is what keeps the two clauses consistent.
