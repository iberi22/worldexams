## Question 1 [D3]

**ID:** `CO-ING-10-2026-P1-P4-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v1`
**Bloom:** Remember
**ICFES:** Reading comprehension (literal)

**Context:** A student in Bogotá is reading a text about environmental issues in Colombia.

**Text:** "Colombia is one of the most biodiverse countries in the world. It has many different ecosystems, from the Amazon rainforest to the páramos in the mountains."

### Enunciado
According to the text, what makes Colombia special?

### Options
- [ ] A) It has only one ecosystem.
- [ ] B) It is a very large country.
- [x] C) It is one of the most biodiverse countries.
- [ ] D) It has no rainforests.

### Explicación Pedagógica
This question tests literal reading comprehension. The text explicitly states that Colombia "is one of the most biodiverse countries in the world." Students need to identify the main idea directly stated in the text.

- [ ] A) have

## Question 14 [D8]

**ID:** `CO-ING-10-2026-P1-P4-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v14`
**Bloom:** Evaluate
**ICFES:** Reading comprehension (critical)

**Context:** Two students are discussing a news article:

**Text:** "A study from the Universidad de los Andes found that students who participate in extracurricular activities have higher academic performance. The study followed 500 students over three years."

Student A: "This proves that extracurricular activities make students smarter."
Student B: "Not necessarily. Maybe students who are already good students tend to participate in more activities."

### Enunciado
Whose interpretation is more critical and justified?

### Options
- [ ] A) Student A, because the study clearly shows a correlation.
- [x] B) Student B, because correlation does not imply causation.
- [ ] C) Both are equally valid.
- [ ] D) Neither, because the study is not reliable.

### Explicación Pedagógica
Student B demonstrates critical thinking by recognizing that correlation does not mean causation. The study shows a relationship between extracurricular activities and academic performance, but it doesn't prove that activities cause better grades.
