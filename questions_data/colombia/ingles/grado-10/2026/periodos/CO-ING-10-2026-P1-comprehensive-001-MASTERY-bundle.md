## Question 1 (Difficulty D3)

**ID:** `CO-ING-10-2026-P1-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v1`
**Bloom:** Remember
**ICFES:** Gramatica B1

**Context:** In English class in Bogota, the teacher reviews reported speech rules with the students.

### Enunciado
Which is the correct structure for reported speech when the reporting verb is in the past tense?

### Options
- [x] A) Subject + said/told + (that) + subject + past tense verb <!-- feedback: Correcto. When the reporting verb is past tense, the verb in the reported clause shifts back one tense. -->
- [ ] B) Subject + said + that + subject + present tense verb <!-- feedback: Incorrecto. Present tense does not backshift; past tense reporting verb requires past tense in the reported clause. -->
- [ ] C) Subject + told + that + subject + will + infinitive <!-- feedback: Incorrecto. 'Will' changes to 'would' in reported speech when the reporting verb is past. -->
- [ ] D) Subject + say + that + subject + past tense verb <!-- feedback: Incorrecto. 'Say' is present tense; the structure given has present reporting verb but past reported clause, which is inconsistent. -->

### Explicacion Pedagogica
In reported speech, the tense of the reported clause typically shifts back one tense relative to the direct speech. If the reporting verb is in the past tense ('said', 'told'), present simple becomes past simple, present perfect becomes past perfect, and 'will' becomes 'would'. Example: Direct: 'I am tired.' Reported: 'He said that he was tired.'

## Question 2 (Difficulty D3)

**ID:** `CO-ING-10-2026-P1-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v2`
**Bloom:** Remember
**ICFES:** Gramatica B1

**Context:** A student in Cali is studying conditional sentences. The teacher asks about the third conditional.

### Enunciado
The third conditional describes:

### Options
- [ ] A) A general truth or scientific fact <!-- feedback: Incorrecto. General truths are expressed with the zero conditional (if + present, present). -->
- [ ] B) A possible situation in the present or future <!-- feedback: Incorrecto. Possible present/future situations are expressed with the first conditional (if + present, will). -->
- [x] C) An unreal situation in the past and its imagined consequence <!-- feedback: Correcto. The third conditional uses 'if + past perfect, would have + past participle' for hypothetical past scenarios. -->
- [ ] D) A real possibility for the future <!-- feedback: Incorrecto. Real future possibilities use the first conditional, not the third. -->

### Explicacion Pedagogica
The third conditional is formed with 'if + past perfect (had + past participle), would/could/might have + past participle.' It describes imaginary situations in the past that did not actually happen. Example: 'If I had studied harder, I would have passed the exam.' This means I did NOT study harder and I did NOT pass.

## Question 3 (Difficulty D4)

**ID:** `CO-ING-10-2026-P1-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v3`
**Bloom:** Understand
**ICFES:** Gramatica B1

**Context:** Marcela tells her friend: 'My mom said: "I will pick you up after school."' She needs to report this to her teacher.

### Enunciado
What is the correct reported speech version?

### Options
- [ ] A) My mom says that she will pick me up after school. <!-- feedback: Incorrecto. The reporting verb should be in past tense ('said'), not present ('says'). -->
- [ ] B) My mom said that she will pick me up after school. <!-- feedback: Incorrecto. 'Will' must backshift to 'would' in reported speech when the reporting verb is past. -->
- [x] C) My mom said that she would pick me up after school. <!-- feedback: Correcto. The reporting verb 'said' is past, so 'will' becomes 'would' and the pronouns adjust appropriately. -->
- [ ] D) My mom told that she would pick me up after school. <!-- feedback: Incorrecto. 'Told' requires an indirect object (told me / told us / told her). Without it, the structure is ungrammatical. -->

### Explicacion Pedagogica
When reporting speech in the past tense, we apply backshift: 'will' changes to 'would,' and pronouns change to match the perspective of the reporter ('you' becomes 'me'). Also, 'said' is used without an indirect object (we say 'said that' or 'said to someone'), while 'told' always requires an indirect object.

**Context:** During a class presentation in Medellin, Andres says: 'I have visited the Museum of Antioquia three times.' His friend needs to report this to an absent classmate.

### Enunciado
How should Andres' statement be reported correctly?

### Options
- [ ] A) Andres said he has visited the Museum of Antioquia three times. <!-- feedback: Incorrecto. Present perfect ('has visited') must backshift to past perfect ('had visited') after a past tense reporting verb. -->

## Question 7 (Difficulty D5)

**ID:** `CO-ING-10-2026-P1-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v7`
**Bloom:** Apply
**ICFES:** Gramatica B1

**Context:** In an English test in Barranquilla, students must correctly transform commands into reported speech: 'The teacher said: "Open your books to page 20."'

### Enunciado
How do you report this command correctly?

### Options
- [x] A) The teacher told us to open our books to page 20. <!-- feedback: Correcto. Commands in reported speech use: 'told + person + to + infinitive.' -->
- [ ] B) The teacher said open your books to page 20. <!-- feedback: Incorrecto. 'Said' cannot be directly followed by a bare infinitive for reported commands. -->
