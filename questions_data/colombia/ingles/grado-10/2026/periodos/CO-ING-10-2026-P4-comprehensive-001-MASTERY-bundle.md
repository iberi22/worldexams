## Question 1 (Difficulty D3)
**ID:** `CO-ING-10-2026-P4-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v1`
**Bloom:** Remember
**ICFES:** Gramatica B1
**Context:** In English class in Cali, the teacher introduces passive voice.
### Enunciado
What is the correct structure for passive voice in the present simple?
### Options
- [x] A) Subject + am/is/are + past participle <!-- feedback: Correcto. Passive voice in present simple: am/is/are + past participle. Example: 'Coffee is grown in Colombia.' -->
- [ ] B) Subject + verb + object <!-- feedback: Incorrecto. That is active voice structure, not passive. -->
- [ ] C) Subject + have/has + past participle <!-- feedback: Incorrecto. That is present perfect active, not passive. -->
- [ ] D) Subject + was/were + past participle <!-- feedback: Incorrecto. That is passive voice in past simple, not present simple. -->

### Explicacion Pedagogica
The passive voice structure is: subject + form of 'be' + past participle + (by + agent). In present simple: am/is/are + past participle. Example: 'Coffee is grown in Colombia' (active: 'Farmers grow coffee in Colombia'). The focus shifts from the doer to the action.

## Question 2 (Difficulty D3)
**ID:** `CO-ING-10-2026-P4-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v2`
**Bloom:** Remember
**ICFES:** Gramatica B1
**Context:** The teacher explains mixed conditionals, combining second and third conditional patterns.
### Enunciado
A mixed conditional (e.g., third conditional if-clause + second conditional result) describes:
### Options
- [x] A) A past condition with a present result <!-- feedback: Correcto. Mixed conditionals often combine an unreal past condition (if + past perfect) with an unreal present result (would + infinitive). -->
- [ ] B) A present condition with a future result <!-- feedback: Incorrecto. That is the first conditional. -->
- [ ] C) Only past situations <!-- feedback: Incorrecto. A regular third conditional is entirely past; a mixed conditional combines past condition with present result. -->
- [ ] D) Impossible future situations <!-- feedback: Incorrecto. The second conditional expresses unlikely future situations; mixed conditionals combine past and present. -->

### Explicacion Pedagogica
Mixed conditionals combine different time references. The most common type: if-clause refers to an unreal past (had + past participle), and the result clause refers to an unreal present (would + infinitive). Example: 'If I had studied medicine, I would be a doctor now.' (Past condition, present result.)

## Question 5 (Difficulty D5)
**ID:** `CO-ING-10-2026-P4-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v5`
**Bloom:** Apply
**ICFES:** Gramatica B1
**Context:** A student needs to change this active sentence to passive: 'Farmers in the Coffee Triangle grow high-quality Arabica coffee.'
### Enunciado
What is the correct passive form?
### Options
- [x] A) High-quality Arabica coffee is grown by farmers in the Coffee Triangle. <!-- feedback: Correcto. Present simple active -> present simple passive: 'is grown' (is + past participle). The object becomes the subject. -->
- [ ] B) High-quality Arabica coffee was grown by farmers in the Coffee Triangle. <!-- feedback: Incorrecto. The original is present simple ('grow'), so passive should be present ('is grown'), not past ('was grown'). -->
- [ ] C) High-quality Arabica coffee is being grown by farmers in the Coffee Triangle. <!-- feedback: Incorrecto. The original is present simple, not present continuous. The passive should match the original tense. -->
- [ ] D) Farmers in the Coffee Triangle are grown by high-quality Arabica coffee. <!-- feedback: Incorrecto. The subject and object are reversed; this changes the meaning completely. -->

### Explicacion Pedagogica
To convert active to passive: (1) Move the object to subject position, (2) Change verb to 'be + past participle' matching the original tense, (3) Move the subject to object position after 'by' (optional if the agent is obvious). Present simple active 'grow' becomes present simple passive 'is grown.'
