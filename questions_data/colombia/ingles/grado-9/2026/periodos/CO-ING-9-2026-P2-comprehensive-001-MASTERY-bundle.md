# Bundle MASTERY: English Period 2 – Passive Voice, Phrasal Verbs & Opinion Writing

This bundle covers the second period of Grade 9 English at CEFR B1 level: passive voice across all tenses, essential phrasal verbs, and opinion writing structure.

---

## Question 1 [D3]

**ID:** `CO-ING-9-2026-P2-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v1`
**Bloom:** Remember
**ICFES:** Identificación de contenidos locales
**Context:** The teacher explains the passive voice formation.

### Enunciado
What is the correct structure of the passive voice in English?

### Options
- [ ] A) Subject + main verb + object <!-- feedback: Incorrect. That's the active voice structure. -->
- [x] B) Subject + to be (conjugated) + past participle (+ by agent) <!-- feedback: Correct. The passive voice always uses a form of "to be" plus the past participle of the main verb. The agent (who does the action) is optional and introduced by "by." Example: "The book was written (by the author)." -->
- [ ] C) Subject + have + past participle <!-- feedback: Incorrect. That's present perfect active. -->
- [ ] D) Subject + to be + gerund <!-- feedback: Incorrect. That's a continuous tense construction. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
To form the passive: identify the tense you need, conjugate "to be" in that tense, then add the past participle of the main verb. Only transitive verbs (verbs that take an object) can be passive.

## Question 2 [D3]

**ID:** `CO-ING-9-2026-P2-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v2`
**Bloom:** Remember
**ICFES:** Identificación de contenidos locales
**Context:** Students learn about common phrasal verbs.

### Enunciado
What is a phrasal verb?

### Options
- [ ] A) A verb with a silent letter <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Phrasal verbs are not about spelling. -->
- [x] B) A verb combined with a preposition or adverb that creates a new meaning different from the original verb <!-- feedback: Correct. A phrasal verb = verb + particle (preposition or adverb). The new meaning often cannot be guessed from the individual words. Example: "give up" (stop trying), "look after" (take care of), "run out of" (exhaust the supply). -->
- [ ] C) A verb in the infinitive form <!-- feedback: Incorrect. That's just the base form of a verb. -->
- [ ] D) A verb that doesn't change form in past tense <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Those are irregular verbs, not phrasal verbs. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
Phrasal verbs are extremely common in English and are a key feature of natural, fluent speech. They can be separable (the object can go between verb and particle: "turn the light off") or inseparable ("look after the children").

## Question 3 [D4]

**ID:** `CO-ING-9-2026-P2-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v3`
**Bloom:** Understand
**ICFES:** Identificación de contenidos locales
**Context:** Students examine why passive voice is used.

### Enunciado
When is it appropriate to use the passive voice?

### Options
- [ ] A) Always, because it sounds more formal <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Overusing passive voice can make writing unclear. -->
- [ ] B) When you want to emphasize the action or when the agent is unknown, unimportant, or obvious <!-- feedback: Correct. Use passive when: 1) the doer is unknown ("My phone was stolen"), 2) the doer is obvious ("He was arrested"), 3) the action is more important than the doer ("A new hospital was built"), or 4) in scientific/formal writing to be objective. -->
- [ ] C) Only in negative sentences <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Passive can be used in any type of sentence. -->
- [ ] D) Only in spoken English, never in writing <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Passive is common in both speech and writing. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
Choosing active vs. passive changes the focus of the sentence. Active: "Shakespeare wrote Hamlet" (focus on Shakespeare). Passive: "Hamlet was written by Shakespeare" (focus on Hamlet).

## Question 5 [D5]

**ID:** `CO-ING-9-2026-P2-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v5`
**Bloom:** Apply
**ICFES:** Identificación de estructuras
**Context:** Students transform active sentences to passive voice.

### Enunciado
Transform to passive: "The students are reading the book."

### Options
- [ ] A) The book is read by the students <!-- feedback: Incorrect. This is present simple passive, but the original is present continuous. -->
- [x] B) The book is being read by the students <!-- feedback: Correct. Present continuous (are reading) → present continuous passive (is being read). The structure is: subject + am/is/are + being + past participle. -->
- [ ] C) The book was read by the students <!-- feedback: Incorrect. This is past simple passive. -->
- [ ] D) The book is been read by the students <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Been" without "being" is incorrect grammar. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
Each tense has its own passive structure. Present continuous passive: am/is/are + being + past participle. Past continuous passive: was/were + being + past participle.

## Question 9 [D6]

**ID:** `CO-ING-9-2026-P2-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v9`
**Bloom:** Analyze
**ICFES:** Identificación de estructuras
**Context:** Students classify phrasal verbs as separable or inseparable.

### Enunciado
Which of these phrasal verbs can be separated by the object? "She turned off the computer" → "She turned the computer off."

### Options
- [x] A) "Turn off" is separable: the object can go between "turn" and "off" <!-- feedback: Correct. Many phrasal verbs with objects are separable: "She turned off the computer" = "She turned the computer off." If the object is a pronoun, it MUST go in the middle: "She turned it off" (NOT "She turned off it"). -->
- [ ] B) "Turn off" is inseparable <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Turn off" is separable. -->
- [ ] C) All phrasal verbs are inseparable <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Some are separable, some are inseparable. -->
- [ ] D) Phrasal verbs can never be separated <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Many phrasal verbs can be separated by the object. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
The rule for pronoun objects: if the phrasal verb is separable and the object is a pronoun (it, them, him, her), the pronoun MUST go between the verb and the particle. ✓ "Pick it up" ✗ "Pick up it."

**Context:** Students identify the thesis statement in an opinion paragraph.

**Paragraph: "Many people think that homework is essential for learning. However, I disagree. In my opinion, homework causes unnecessary stress, reduces family time, and does not significantly improve academic performance. Instead, schools should focus on quality in-class learning."**

### Enunciado
Which sentence is the thesis statement?

### Options
- [ ] A) "Many people think that homework is essential for learning" <!-- feedback: Incorrect. This is the opposing view (counterargument). -->
- [x] B) "In my opinion, homework causes unnecessary stress, reduces family time, and does not significantly improve academic performance" <!-- feedback: Correct. This is the thesis statement: it states the writer's opinion clearly and previews the arguments (stress, family time, performance). The following sentences will develop these points. -->

A strong thesis statement: 1) expresses a clear opinion, 2) is specific, 3) often previews the main arguments. It usually appears at the end of the introduction paragraph.

## Question 11 [D7]

**ID:** `CO-ING-9-2026-P2-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v11`
**Bloom:** Analyze
**ICFES:** Identificación de estructuras
**Context:** Students transform an active sentence to passive in different tenses.

### Enunciado
"The chef has prepared the meal." What is the correct passive form?

### Options
- [ ] A) The meal is prepared by the chef <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Present simple passive, but the original is present perfect. -->
- [ ] B) The meal was prepared by the chef <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Past simple passive. -->
- [x] C) The meal has been prepared by the chef <!-- feedback: Correct. Present perfect (has prepared) → present perfect passive (has been prepared). Structure: have/has + been + past participle. -->
- [ ] D) The meal is being prepared by the chef <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Present continuous passive. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
Present perfect passive: have/has + been + past participle. Past perfect passive: had + been + past participle. Future passive: will + be + past participle. Modal passive: modal + be + past participle (can be done, should be done).

Which of the following is the BEST first sentence (hook) for an opinion essay about social media?

### Options

**ICFES:** Reflexión crítica
**Context:** A student analyzes an opinion essay and identifies weaknesses.

**Body paragraph: "Social media is bad. It causes problems. People waste time. Students get distracted."**

### Enunciado
What is the main weakness of this body paragraph?

### Options

## Question 15 [D9]

**ID:** `CO-ING-9-2026-P2-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v15`
**Bloom:** Evaluate
**ICFES:** Reflexión crítica
**Context:** A student evaluates and corrects a paragraph with overuse of passive voice.

**Paragraph: "A decision was made by me to visit the museum. A ticket was bought by me online. The museum was visited by me with my family."**

### Enunciado
What is wrong with this paragraph and how should it be improved?

### Options
- [ ] A) Nothing is wrong; passive voice is correct <!-- feedback: Incorrect. The passive is overused and makes the paragraph awkward. -->
- [x] B) The passive voice is overused and makes the paragraph sound unnatural and indirect. It should be rewritten in active voice: "I decided to visit the museum. I bought a ticket online. I visited the museum with my family." <!-- feedback: Correct. Using passive voice when the agent is known and important (I) creates unnecessary wordiness and distances the writer from the action. Active voice is more natural, concise, and direct. -->
- [ ] C) The phrasal verbs are missing <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Phrasal verbs are not the main issue here. -->
- [ ] D) It should use more complex tenses <!-- feedback: Incorrect. The tense choice is not the problem. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
Active voice: subject → action → object (I bought a ticket). Passive voice: object → action → agent (A ticket was bought by me). Overusing passive when the agent is known creates weak, indirect writing. In opinion writing, use active voice for clarity and conviction.
