# Bundle Mastery: Present Perfect, First Conditional and Comparatives (CEFR B1)

This bundle covers the present perfect tense for experiences, the first conditional for future possibilities, and comparative/superlative adjectives at the B1 level, with Colombian contexts.

---

## Question 1 [D3]

**ID:** `CO-ENG-9-2026-P1-comprehensive-002-MASTERY-v1`
**Bloom:** Remember
**ICFES:** Competencia Lingüística
**Context:** Teacher María is explaining the present perfect tense to her 9th grade students at a school in Bogotá.

### Enunciado
Which auxiliary verb is used to form the present perfect tense in English?

### Options
- [ ] A) "do" / "does" <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "do/does" are auxiliary verbs for the present simple in questions and negatives. -->
- [x] B) "have" / "has" <!-- feedback: Correct. The present perfect is formed with the auxiliary "have" (or "has" for third person singular) + past participle. -->
- [ ] C) "will" <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "will" is an auxiliary verb used for the future tense. -->
- [ ] D) "be" <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "be" is used for continuous tenses (present continuous) and passive voice. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
The present perfect tense connects the past with the present. Form: subject + have/has + past participle (+ complement). Example: "I have visited the Gold Museum in Bogotá." It is used for experiences, changes, and actions that started in the past and continue.

## Question 2 [D3]

**ID:** `CO-ENG-9-2026-P1-comprehensive-002-MASTERY-v2`
**Bloom:** Remember
**ICFES:** Competencia Lingüística
**Context:** In a classroom in Medellín, students are learning about conditional sentences.

### Enunciado
What is the correct structure for a first conditional sentence?

### Options
- [x] A) If + present simple, will + base verb <!-- feedback: Correct. First conditional: If + present simple (condition), will + base verb (result). Example: "If it rains, I will stay home." -->
- [ ] B) If + past simple, would + base verb <!-- feedback: Incorrect. That is the structure for the second conditional (unreal present situations). -->
- [ ] C) If + will + base verb, present simple <!-- feedback: Incorrect. The "will" clause cannot be in the "if" clause. -->
- [ ] D) If + have + past participle, will + past simple <!-- feedback: Incorrect. This combination is not a standard conditional structure in English. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
The first conditional expresses a real possibility in the future. The "if" clause (condition) uses the present simple, while the main clause (result) uses "will" + base verb. Example: "If you study hard, you will pass the exam."

## Question 3 [D4]

**ID:** `CO-ENG-9-2026-P1-comprehensive-002-MASTERY-v3`
**Bloom:** Understand
**ICFES:** Competencia Lingüística
**Context:** Students at a school in Cali are learning about comparative adjectives.

### Enunciado
Which sentence correctly uses the comparative form of an adjective?

### Options
- [ ] A) Cartagena is more hot than Bogotá. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. For one-syllable adjectives, "more" is not used. The correct form is "hotter" (double the last consonant). -->
- [ ] B) Bogotá is colder that Medellín. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. The correct conjunction is "than", not "that". -->
- [x] C) The Amazon River is longer than the Magdalena River. <!-- feedback: Correct. "Longer" is the correct comparative form of "long" (short adjective + -er). -->
- [ ] D) The Sierra Nevada is the most high mountain in Colombia. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. This is a superlative form (most + adjective), not comparative. Also, "high" → "highest", not "most high". -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
Comparative adjectives compare two things. Short adjectives (1 syllable) add -er: long → longer, tall → taller. Adjectives ending in consonant-vowel-consonant double the last letter: big → bigger. Long adjectives (2+ syllables) use "more": beautiful → more beautiful.

## Question 4 [D4]

**ID:** `CO-ENG-9-2026-P1-comprehensive-002-MASTERY-v4`
**Bloom:** Understand
**ICFES:** Competencia Lingüística
**Context:** At a language school in Bucaramanga, a teacher explains the difference between present perfect and simple past.

### Enunciado
Which sentence uses the present perfect tense CORRECTLY to talk about an experience?

### Options
- [ ] A) I have go to Cartagena last year. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "last year" is a specific past time, so simple past should be used: "I went to Cartagena last year." -->
- [ ] B) I went to the Tayrona Park. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. This is simple past, not present perfect. -->
- [x] C) I have visited the Museo del Oro in Bogotá. <!-- feedback: Correct. Present perfect is used for experiences without specifying exactly when they happened. -->
- [ ] D) I have visited the Museo del Oro yesterday. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "yesterday" is a specific past time, so simple past is needed: "I visited the Museo del Oro yesterday." -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
Present perfect is used for: 1) life experiences (no specific time), 2) changes over time, 3) actions that started in the past and continue. Simple past is for completed actions at a specific past time. "Have visited" is correct for a general experience.

### Enunciado
Complete the sentence with the correct comparative form: "Medellín is _________ (modern) than it was 20 years ago."

### Options
- [ ] A) moderner <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Modern" has two syllables and does not take -er. The correct form is "more modern". -->
- [x] B) more modern <!-- feedback: Correct. "Modern" has two syllables, so we use "more + adjective" for the comparative. -->

**Bloom:** Apply
**ICFES:** Competencia Lingüística
**Context:** Ana is telling her classmates about her life experiences during an English class in Popayán.

### Enunciado
Complete Ana's sentence: "I _________ (never / try) traditional Colombian sancocho."

### Options
- [ ] A) never tried <!-- feedback: Incorrect. The word order should be "have never tried" with the auxiliary "have" before "never". -->
- [ ] B) have never try <!-- feedback: Incorrect. After "have", the past participle form "tried" is needed, not the base form "try". -->

Adverbs like "never", "ever", "already", and "yet" go between "have/has" and the past participle. "I have never tried sancocho" means Ana hasn't had this experience in her life so far.

**Bloom:** Apply
**ICFES:** Competencia Lingüística
**Context:** Students at a sports competition in Cali are comparing the Colombian athletes.

### Enunciado
Choose the correct superlative: "Mariana Pajón is one of _________ (successful) Colombian athletes in history."

### Options
- [ ] A) the most successful <!-- feedback: Correct. "Successful" is a long adjective; superlative uses "the most + adjective". -->
- [ ] B) the successfullest <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "-est" is only used for short adjectives. "Successful" needs "the most". -->
- [ ] C) most successful <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Superlatives need the definite article "the": "the most successful". -->

Superlative adjectives are used to compare one thing with all others in a group. Long adjectives (2+ syllables): the most + adjective. Short adjectives: the + adjective + -est. Examples: the most beautiful, the tallest, the most important.

## Question 9 [D6]

**ID:** `CO-ENG-9-2026-P1-comprehensive-002-MASTERY-v9`
**Bloom:** Analyze
**ICFES:** Competencia Lingüística
**Context:** In an English exam in Bogotá, students must identify the correct use of present perfect vs. simple past.

### Enunciado
Read the two sentences. Which one is CORRECT?

### Options
- [ ] A) "Yesterday, I have visited the Salt Cathedral of Zipaquirá." <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Yesterday" is a specific past time, so simple past is required: "I visited the Salt Cathedral yesterday." -->
- [x] B) "I have already visited the Salt Cathedral of Zipaquirá twice." <!-- feedback: Correct. "Already" is used with present perfect to talk about completed experiences without a specific time. -->
- [ ] C) "I have visited the Salt Cathedral last month." <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Last month" is a specific past time expression; use simple past: "I visited the Salt Cathedral last month." -->
- [ ] D) "I didn't have visited the Salt Cathedral yet." <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Negation in present perfect: "I haven't visited the Salt Cathedral yet." "Didn't" is for simple past. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
Key difference: present perfect does NOT use specific past time expressions (yesterday, last week, in 2020). It uses unspecific time expressions: already, ever, never, yet, before. Simple past uses specific time expressions.

**ICFES:** Competencia Lingüística

### Enunciado
What is the error in the sentence "If I will have time, I will visit the Cocora Valley"?

### Options
- [ ] A) The sentence is correct. No error. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. There IS an error in the sentence. -->
- [ ] B) "will visit" should be "visited". <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "will visit" is correct in the main clause. The error is in the "if" clause. -->

### Enunciado
Choose the sentence that CORRECTLY uses the superlative form.

### Options

**Context:** A student says: "I have gone to the Galerías mall last Sunday."

### Enunciado
Why is this sentence grammatically incorrect?

### Options

**Bloom:** Evaluate
**ICFES:** Competencia Lingüística
**Context:** In a debate at school, a student claims: "The first conditional and the second conditional have the same meaning."

### Enunciado
Is this statement correct?

### Options
- [ ] A) Yes, both conditionals express the same type of situation. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. They express very different types of situations. -->
- [ ] B) No, the first conditional uses "will" while the second uses "would", but they mean the same thing. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. They differ not only in form but also in meaning. -->
- [x] C) No, the first conditional expresses a real or probable future situation, while the second conditional expresses an unreal or hypothetical present situation. <!-- feedback: Correct. The meaning is fundamentally different between real (1st) and unreal (2nd) conditionals. -->

## Question 15 [D9]

**ID:** `CO-ENG-9-2026-P1-comprehensive-002-MASTERY-v15`
**Bloom:** Evaluate
**ICFES:** Competencia Lingüística
**Context:** A student writes a short paragraph about Colombian culture and makes several mistakes.

### Enunciado
Read the following student paragraph and identify which sentence contains a grammar error:

"Colombia is a beautiful country. I have visited the Coffee Region last year. The Caribbean coast is hotter than the Andes. If you visit Colombia, you will have an amazing experience."

### Options
- [ ] A) "Colombia is a beautiful country." <!-- feedback: Incorrect. This sentence is grammatically correct with correct subject-verb agreement. -->
- [x] B) "I have visited the Coffee Region last year." <!-- feedback: Correct. This sentence incorrectly uses present perfect with a specific past time expression ("last year"). It should be: "I visited the Coffee Region last year." -->
- [ ] C) "The Caribbean coast is hotter than the Andes." <!-- feedback: Incorrect. This sentence correctly uses the comparative form "hotter" (short adjective, double the t). -->
- [ ] D) "If you visit Colombia, you will have an amazing experience." <!-- feedback: Incorrect. This sentence correctly uses the first conditional: if + present simple, will + base verb. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
The error is in sentence B. Present perfect ("have visited") cannot be used with specific past time expressions like "last year", "yesterday", or "in 2020". The correct version uses simple past: "I visited the Coffee Region last year." Present perfect would be correct without the specific time: "I have visited the Coffee Region."
