# Bundle Mastery: Second Conditional and Reported Speech (CEFR B1)

This bundle covers the second conditional (unreal present/future) and basic reported speech at the B1 level.

---

## Question 1 [D3]

**ID:** `CO-ENG-9-2026-P2-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v1`
**Bloom:** Remember
**ICFES:** Comprensión de textos
**Context:** A student in Bogotá is learning about conditionals.

### Enunciado
What is the structure of the Second Conditional?

### Options
- [ ] A) If + present simple, will + verb <!-- feedback: Incorrect. That's the first conditional (real future). -->
- [ ] B) If + past perfect, would have + past participle <!-- feedback: Incorrect. That's the third conditional (unreal past). -->
- [x] C) If + past simple, would + base verb <!-- feedback: Correct. Second conditional: "If I had money, I would travel." -->
- [ ] D) If + present simple, present simple <!-- feedback: Incorrect. That's the zero conditional (general truth). -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
The second conditional is used for unreal or imaginary situations in the present or future. Structure: If + subject + past simple, subject + would + base verb. Example: "If I were rich, I would buy a house in Cartagena."

## Question 2 [D3]

**ID:** `CO-ENG-9-2026-P2-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v2`
**Bloom:** Remember
**ICFES:** Comprensión de textos
**Context:** A teacher explains reported speech in Medellín.

### Enunciado
In reported speech, what happens to the verb tense when the reporting verb is in the past?

### Options
- [ ] A) The verb stays in the same tense <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Usually the tense shifts back. -->
- [x] B) The verb tense usually shifts back (backshift): present becomes past, past becomes past perfect <!-- feedback: Correct. "I am tired" becomes "He said he was tired." -->
- [ ] C) The verb changes to future tense <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Tenses shift back, not forward. -->
- [ ] D) The verb changes to the infinitive <!-- feedback: Incorrect. That's not how reported speech works. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
When reporting what someone said, if the reporting verb (said, told) is in the past, the verb in the reported clause shifts back one tense: present simple -> past simple, present continuous -> past continuous, past simple -> past perfect, will -> would.

**Bloom:** Understand
**ICFES:** Comprensión de textos
**Context:** Read: "If I lived in Santa Marta, I would go to the beach every day."

### Enunciado
Does this person live in Santa Marta?

### Options
- [ ] A) Yes, they live in Santa Marta and go to the beach often <!-- feedback: Incorrect. The second conditional describes an unreal situation. -->
- [x] B) No, they don't live in Santa Marta. The sentence expresses an imaginary situation. <!-- feedback: Correct. Second conditional uses past tense to show unreality. -->
- [ ] C) Maybe, the sentence doesn't say <!-- feedback: Incorrect. The past tense "lived" signals it's unreal. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
The second conditional uses past tense, but the meaning is about the present/future. The past tense signals that the situation is imaginary or unlikely. "If I lived..." means "I don't live there (now), but imagine if I did."

## Question 4 [D4]

**ID:** `CO-ENG-9-2026-P2-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v4`
**Bloom:** Understand
**ICFES:** Comprensión de textos
**Context:** Read: "María said, 'I am studying medicine.'" Now change to reported speech.

### Enunciado
What is the correct reported speech version?

### Options
- [ ] A) María said that I am studying medicine. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. The pronoun "I" doesn't change correctly. -->
- [ ] B) María said that she is studying medicine. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. The tense doesn't backshift. -->
- [x] C) María said that she was studying medicine. <!-- feedback: Correct. "I" becomes "she," present continuous "am studying" becomes past continuous "was studying." -->
- [ ] D) María said that she has been studying medicine. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Present perfect continuous doesn't match "am studying." -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
When reporting speech, three things change: 1) Pronouns (I -> she), 2) Tenses backshift (am -> was), 3) Time/place words if needed (here -> there, now -> then).

**Bloom:** Apply
**ICFES:** Competencia léxica y gramatical
**Context:** A reporter needs to report what a famous Colombian athlete said: "I train every day."

### Enunciado
Report this statement correctly.

### Options
- [ ] A) The athlete said that I train every day. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Pronoun "I" doesn't change. -->
- [ ] B) The athlete said that he trains every day. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. No backshift of the tense. -->
- [x] C) The athlete said that he trained every day. <!-- feedback: Correct. "I" becomes "he," present "train" becomes past "trained." -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
In reported speech with a past reporting verb (said), present simple becomes past simple. "I train" -> "he trained." If the statement is still true (he still trains daily), some speakers keep the present tense, but backshift is always grammatically correct.

**ICFES:** Comprensión de textos

### Enunciado
What does the speaker mean by this sentence?

### Options
- [ ] A) The speaker is you and is applying for the scholarship <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "If I were you" is a fixed expression. -->
- [ ] B) The speaker is giving advice by imagining being in the other person's position <!-- feedback: Correct. "If I were you" is a common phrase for giving advice. -->

"If I were you" is a fixed expression using the second conditional to give advice. Note: we use "were" (not "was") for all persons in this expression. It's a subjunctive form. "If I were you, I would talk to the teacher."

**ID:** `CO-ENG-9-2026-P2-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v9`
**Bloom:** Apply
**ICFES:** Competencia léxica y gramatical
**Context:** A journalist reports an interview with the Mayor of Bogotá: "I will improve public transportation."

### Enunciado
Report this statement correctly.

### Options
- [ ] A) The Mayor said that I will improve public transportation. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Pronouns and tenses not changed. -->
- [ ] B) The Mayor said that he will improve public transportation. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Will" should backshift. -->
- [x] C) The Mayor said that he would improve public transportation. <!-- feedback: Correct. "I" -> "he," "will" -> "would" in reported speech. -->

**Bloom:** Analyze
**ICFES:** Comprensión de textos
**Context:** Read the dialogue: "What would you do if you won the lottery?" "If I won the lottery, I would travel around the world."

### Enunciado
This is a conversation using the second conditional. What does it imply about winning the lottery?

### Options
- [ ] A) The person is likely to win the lottery <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Second conditional implies low probability. -->
- [x] B) Winning the lottery is unlikely or imaginary, so the person is speculating about a hypothetical situation <!-- feedback: Correct. The second conditional describes unreal or improbable situations. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
The second conditional is also called the "unreal conditional." It describes situations that are: 1) Impossible (If I were a bird...), 2) Unlikely (If I won the lottery...), or 3) Imaginary (If I lived in another country...).

## Question 11 [D7]

**ID:** `CO-ENG-9-2026-P2-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v11`
**Bloom:** Analyze
**ICFES:** Comprensión de textos
**Context:** A student reads: "She told me that she had never been to the Amazon rainforest."

### Enunciado
What was the original direct speech?

### Options
- [ ] A) "I have never been to the Amazon rainforest" <!-- feedback: Correct. "Had never been" -> backshift from present perfect "have never been." -->
- [ ] B) "I had never been to the Amazon rainforest" <!-- feedback: Incorrect. If the original was past perfect, it would stay the same or use different context. -->
- [ ] C) "I never went to the Amazon rainforest" <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Simple past would backshift to past perfect, but present perfect is more likely here. -->
- [ ] D) "I will never go to the Amazon rainforest" <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Will" would become "would." -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
Present perfect (have/has + past participle) in direct speech becomes past perfect (had + past participle) in reported speech. Direct: "I have seen that movie." Reported: "She said she had seen that movie."

## Question 12 [D7]

**ID:** `CO-ENG-9-2026-P2-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v12`
**Bloom:** Analyze
**ICFES:** Comprensión de textos
**Context:** A student is confused about when to use "say" vs "tell" in reported speech.

### Enunciado
Which sentence is grammatically correct?

### Options
- [ ] A) He said me that he was tired. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Say" doesn't take an indirect object. -->
- [x] B) He told me that he was tired. <!-- feedback: Correct. "Tell" requires an indirect object (me, him, her). -->
- [ ] C) He told that he was tired. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Tell" needs an object. -->
- [ ] D) He said me that he was tired. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Same as A. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
Important difference: "say" + (that) + clause (no indirect object). "Tell" + indirect object + (that) + clause. Correct: "He said (that) he was tired." "He told ME (that) he was tired."

**Bloom:** Create
**ICFES:** Competencia léxica y gramatical
**Context:** A student must write about environmental change in Colombia using second conditional and reported speech.

### Enunciado
Which paragraph correctly and naturally combines both structures?

### Options
- [ ] A) "If the government does more, the environment will be better." <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Uses first conditional, not second, and no reported speech. -->
- [ ] B) "The scientist said that if people recycled more, the planet would be cleaner." <!-- feedback: Correct. Reported speech (said that...) + second conditional (if people recycled...would be) combined naturally. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
Combining reported speech and conditionals shows advanced B1 proficiency. The structure: reporting verb (said) + that + second conditional clause. "The expert said that if Colombia invested more in renewable energy, it would reduce carbon emissions."
