# Bundle MASTERY: English Period 3 – Third Conditional, Advanced Reported Speech & Argumentative Writing

This bundle covers the third period of Grade 9 English at CEFR B1 level: third conditional for past unreal situations, advanced reported speech with mixed tenses, and argumentative writing with counterarguments.

---

## Question 1 [D3]

**ID:** `CO-ING-9-2026-P3-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v1`
**Bloom:** Remember
**ICFES:** Identificación de contenidos locales
**Context:** The teacher introduces the third conditional structure.

### Enunciado
What is the grammatical structure of the third conditional?

### Options
- [ ] A) If + past simple + would + base verb <!-- feedback: Incorrect. That's the second conditional (present unreal). -->
- [ ] B) If + present simple + will + base verb <!-- feedback: Incorrect. That's the first conditional (real future). -->
- [x] C) If + past perfect + would have + past participle <!-- feedback: Correct. The third conditional expresses unreal past situations: If + had + past participle, would have + past participle. Example: "If I had studied, I would have passed the exam." (I didn't study, and I didn't pass.) -->
- [ ] D) If + past perfect + would + base verb <!-- feedback: Incorrect. This is a mixed conditional (third + second), not the standard third. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
The third conditional expresses regret or speculation about the past. The if-clause describes the unreal condition, and the result clause describes the imagined outcome. Both parts are unreal because the past cannot be changed.

## Question 2 [D3]

**ID:** `CO-ING-9-2026-P3-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v2`
**Bloom:** Remember
**ICFES:** Identificación de contenidos locales
**Context:** Students review reported speech rules for different tenses.

### Enunciado
When the reporting verb is in the past tense (said), how does the present perfect tense shift in reported speech?

### Options
- [ ] A) It stays the same <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Tenses usually shift back. -->
- [ ] B) It shifts to past simple <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Present perfect shifts further back. -->
- [x] C) It shifts to past perfect <!-- feedback: Correct. Present perfect (have/has + past participle) shifts to past perfect (had + past participle) in reported speech. "She has finished" → She said she had finished. -->
- [ ] D) It shifts to present perfect continuous <!-- feedback: Incorrect. That's not the standard backshift. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
Backshift pattern: present simple → past simple, present continuous → past continuous, present perfect → past perfect, past simple → past perfect, will → would, can → could, must → had to.

## Question 3 [D4]

**ID:** `CO-ING-9-2026-P3-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v3`
**Bloom:** Understand
**ICFES:** Comprensión global
**Context:** Students discuss when to use the third conditional.

### Enunciado
What does the third conditional express?

### Options
- [ ] A) A possible future situation <!-- feedback: Incorrect. That's the first conditional. -->
- [ ] B) An unlikely present situation <!-- feedback: Incorrect. That's the second conditional. -->
- [x] C) A hypothetical past situation that did NOT happen, often expressing regret or criticism <!-- feedback: Correct. The third conditional is used for past unreal conditions. "If I had left earlier, I would have caught the bus" means: I did NOT leave earlier, and I did NOT catch the bus. It expresses regret about a missed opportunity. -->
- [ ] D) A scientific fact <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Scientific facts use the zero conditional (if + present, present). -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
The third conditional is the conditional of regret. It can also express criticism: "If you had told me, I would have helped you" (you didn't tell me, so I didn't help). The meaning is always negative — the condition wasn't met.

## Question 4 [D4]

**ID:** `CO-ING-9-2026-P3-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v4`
**Bloom:** Understand
**ICFES:** Comprensión global
**Context:** Students practice reporting questions with question words.

### Enunciado
How do you report: "Where did you buy that?" she asked.

### Options
- [ ] A) She asked where did you buy that <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Reported questions use statement word order. -->
- [ ] B) She asked where you bought that <!-- feedback: Incorrect. The pronoun needs to change (you → I/he/she). -->
- [x] C) She asked where I had bought that <!-- feedback: Correct. For wh-questions: 1) keep the question word (where), 2) change to statement word order (I had bought, not did I buy), 3) backshift: simple past (did buy) → past perfect (had bought), 4) change pronoun (you → I). -->
- [ ] D) She asked where I bought that <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Simple past should shift to past perfect in formal reported speech. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
Reporting wh-questions: question word + subject + verb (statement order). The auxiliary "do/does/did" disappears because it's no longer a question. Tense backshift also applies.

## Question 5 [D5]

**ID:** `CO-ING-9-2026-P3-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v5`
**Bloom:** Apply
**ICFES:** Identificación de estructuras
**Context:** Students complete third conditional sentences.

### Enunciado
If she _____ (know) about the traffic, she _____ (leave) earlier.

### Options
- [ ] A) knew / would leave <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Second conditional for present unreal, but the context suggests a past situation. -->
- [ ] B) knows / will leave <!-- feedback: Incorrect. First conditional for real future. -->
- [x] C) had known / would have left <!-- feedback: Correct. Third conditional: If + had known (past perfect), would have + left (would have + past participle). This expresses a past regret: she didn't know about the traffic, so she didn't leave earlier. -->
- [ ] D) would have known / had left <!-- feedback: Incorrect. The if-clause uses past perfect, not "would have." "Would have" only goes in the result clause. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
Never use "would have" in the if-clause. ✗ "If I would have known" is incorrect. ✓ "If I had known." "Would have" only appears in the result clause.

## Question 6 [D5]

**ID:** `CO-ING-9-2026-P3-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v6`
**Bloom:** Apply
**ICFES:** Identificación de estructuras
**Context:** Students transform direct speech with past tense verbs to reported speech.

### Enunciado
Direct: "I visited Cartagena last year," Pablo said.
Reported: ?

### Options
- [ ] A) Pablo said he visited Cartagena last year <!-- feedback: Partially correct. "Last year" should shift to "the previous year" in formal reported speech. -->
- [ ] B) Pablo said he has visited Cartagena the previous year <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Present perfect is not the correct backshift for past simple. -->
- [x] C) Pablo said he had visited Cartagena the previous year <!-- feedback: Correct. Simple past (visited) shifts to past perfect (had visited). "Last year" shifts to "the previous year" or "the year before." Both tense and time expression shifts are applied. -->
- [ ] D) Pablo said he had visited Cartagena last year <!-- feedback: Partially correct. The tense backshift is right, but "last year" should shift for consistency. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
When the direct speech already uses a past tense, the reported speech typically shifts further back to past perfect. This makes the sequence clear: the visit happened before Pablo spoke.

**ICFES:** Comprensión global
**Context:** Students analyze a third conditional sentence to understand its meaning.

"If the Spanish had not colonized the Americas, the language landscape would have been very different."

### Enunciado
What does this sentence imply about what actually happened?

### Options
- [ ] A) The Spanish colonized the Americas, and the language landscape is different as a result <!-- feedback: Correct, but I need the best answer. -->
- [x] B) The Spanish did colonize the Americas, and the language landscape did change. The speaker is imagining a counterfactual past to reflect on the actual consequences <!-- feedback: Correct. ## Question 9 [D6]

**ID:** `CO-ING-9-2026-P3-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v9`
**Bloom:** Analyze
**ICFES:** Identificación de estructuras
**Context:** Students analyze the use of reported speech in a news article.

**Text: "The mayor announced that the new park would be built in the center of the city. He added that construction would begin the following month. Residents had expressed concerns about traffic during the project."**

### Enunciado
Why does the text switch from reported speech ("would be built") to past perfect ("had expressed")?

### Options
- [ ] A) The journalist made a grammatical error <!-- feedback: Incorrect. The tense choice is deliberate and correct. -->
- [ ] B) "Would be built" reports a future-from-past perspective (the mayor's announcement), while "had expressed" provides background information that happened before the announcement <!-- feedback: Correct. "Would be built" and "would begin" are reported speech structures (will → would) that report what the mayor said about the future. "Had expressed" is past perfect showing residents' concerns occurred before the mayor's announcement. Different time relationships require different structures. -->
- [ ] C) "Had expressed" should be "expressed" <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Past perfect is appropriate to show the concerns came before the announcement. -->
- [ ] D) Both should be in simple past <!-- feedback: Incorrect. The different tenses serve different functions. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
Narrative texts with reported speech often use multiple tenses to show different time relationships: reported speech shifts (will → would) for future-from-past, and past perfect for actions before the reporting moment.

**Paragraph: "Some people argue that school uniforms limit students' freedom of expression. ### Enunciado
Identify the counterargument and the rebuttal in this paragraph.

### Options
- [ ] A) The whole paragraph is the author's opinion <!-- feedback: Incorrect. There are two voices. -->
- [x] B) Counterargument: "uniforms limit students' freedom of expression" (introduced by "Some people argue"). ## Question 12 [D7]

**ID:** `CO-ING-9-2026-P3-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v12`
**Bloom:** Apply
**ICFES:** Comprensión global
**Context:** Students practice writing a counterargument and rebuttal.

### Enunciado
Choose the best rebuttal to the following counterargument: "Online learning is not effective because students get easily distracted at home."

### Options
- [ ] A) "You are wrong." <!-- feedback: Incorrect. This is a simple rejection without reasoning, which is not a persuasive rebuttal. -->
- [x] B) "While distractions are a valid concern, online learning platforms now include engagement tools like interactive quizzes, video discussions, and progress tracking that help students stay focused. Moreover, online learning teaches self-discipline, a valuable skill for the future." <!-- feedback: Correct. This rebuttal: 1) concedes the point has some validity ("a valid concern"), 2) provides a specific counter-argument (engagement tools), and 3) adds another benefit (self-discipline). This is respectful, evidence-based, and persuasive. -->
- [ ] C) "Online learning is better." <!-- feedback: Incorrect. This is just a claim without evidence. -->
- [ ] D) "Some students study better at home." <!-- feedback: Too weak. It doesn't directly address the counterargument about distractions. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
A strong rebuttal: 1) acknowledges the opposing view (concession to show fairness), 2) presents evidence or reasons that weaken it, and 3) reinforces your own position. Use phrases like: "While it is true that...", "Although some argue that...", "This view overlooks..."

**Version A: "I think recycling is good. We should recycle more. It helps the environment."**
**Version B: "While some claim recycling is too expensive, the long-term environmental and economic benefits far outweigh the initial costs. Countries like Germany have shown that effective recycling programs reduce waste by up to 50%."**

### Enunciado
Which version is more persuasive and why?

### Options
- [ ] A) Version A is better because it is simpler <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Simpler is not always more persuasive. -->

## Question 15 [D9]

**ID:** `CO-ING-9-2026-P3-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v15`
**Bloom:** Evaluate
**ICFES:** Reflexión crítica
**Context:** A student evaluates the use of reported speech in an academic summary.

**Original: "The researcher said: 'Our findings prove that climate change is accelerating. We will see catastrophic effects within a decade if we don't act now.'"**
**Summary: "The researcher said that their findings proved that climate change was accelerating and that catastrophic effects would be seen within a decade if action was not taken."**

### Enunciado
What transformations were applied correctly in this summary?

### Options
- [ ] A) Only the pronouns changed <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Many changes were made. -->
- [ ] B) The tenses were kept the same to preserve the original meaning <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Tenses were shifted. -->
- [x] C) Multiple transformations were applied correctly: verb backshift (prove → proved, is → was, will → would), pronoun shift (our → their, we → they), time expression adjustment (now → not needed), direct command transformed (if we don't act → if action was not taken) <!-- feedback: Correct. The summary correctly applies: 1) Tense backshift: present → past (is → was, prove → proved), will → would. 2) Pronoun/deixis shift: our → their. 3) The imperative/conditional "if we don't act" becomes a passive construction. This demonstrates mastery of advanced reported speech. -->
- [ ] D) The summary changed the meaning of the original <!-- feedback: Incorrect. The summary accurately reports the original meaning. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
Good academic reporting requires accurate backshift. When the reported information is still true (climate change is still accelerating), some writers keep the present tense to emphasize ongoing truth. But in formal academic writing, backshift is the standard.
