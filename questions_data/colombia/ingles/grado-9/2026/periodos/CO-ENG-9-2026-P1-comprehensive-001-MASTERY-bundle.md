# Bundle Mastery: Past Perfect and Passive Voice (CEFR B1)

This bundle covers the past perfect tense and an introduction to the passive voice at the B1 level, with Colombian contexts.

---

## Question 1 [D3]

**ID:** `CO-ENG-9-2026-P1-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v1`
**Bloom:** Remember
**ICFES:** Comprensión de textos
**Context:** A student in Bogotá is learning about verb tenses in English class.

### Enunciado
Which auxiliary verb is used to form the Past Perfect tense?

### Options
- [ ] A) Have <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Have" is used for present perfect (I have seen). -->
- [ ] B) Was / Were <!-- feedback: Incorrect. These are used for past continuous (I was going). -->
- [x] C) Had <!-- feedback: Correct. Past Perfect is formed with "had" + past participle (I had seen). -->
- [ ] D) Did <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Did" is used for simple past questions (Did you see?). -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
The Past Perfect tense is formed with the auxiliary "had" followed by the past participle of the main verb. It describes an action that happened before another past action. Example: "She had finished her homework before dinner."

## Question 2 [D3]

**ID:** `CO-ENG-9-2026-P1-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v2`
**Bloom:** Remember
**ICFES:** Comprensión de textos
**Context:** A teacher explains the passive voice structure to students in Medellín.

### Enunciado
What is the basic structure of the passive voice in English?

### Options
- [ ] A) Subject + verb + object <!-- feedback: Incorrect. That's the active voice structure. -->
- [x] B) Subject + to be + past participle (+ by agent) <!-- feedback: Correct. Passive: "The cake was eaten (by Maria)." The object of the active sentence becomes the subject. -->
- [ ] C) Subject + had + past participle <!-- feedback: Incorrect. That's past perfect, not passive. -->
- [ ] D) Subject + will + verb <!-- feedback: Incorrect. That's future simple. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
The passive voice changes the focus from who does the action (agent) to the action itself. Structure: subject + form of "to be" + past participle. Example: "The letter was written by Juan." The agent (Juan) can be omitted.

## Question 3 [D4]

**ID:** `CO-ENG-9-2026-P1-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v3`
**Bloom:** Understand
**ICFES:** Comprensión de textos
**Context:** Read the sentence: "When the teacher arrived, the students had already finished the exam."

### Enunciado
What happened first according to this sentence?

### Options
- [ ] A) The teacher arrived <!-- feedback: Incorrect. The past perfect ("had finished") indicates the earlier action. -->
- [x] B) The students finished the exam <!-- feedback: Correct. Past perfect signals the action that happened first. The teacher's arrival (simple past) happened later. -->
- [ ] C) Both happened at the same time <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Past perfect indicates one action before another. -->
- [ ] D) The teacher arrived before the students finished <!-- feedback: Incorrect. That would use simple past for both actions. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
Past Perfect (had + past participle) is used to show which of two past actions happened first. The action in Past Perfect happened before the action in Simple Past. Think of it as "the past of the past."

## Question 4 [D4]

**ID:** `CO-ENG-9-2026-P1-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v4`
**Bloom:** Understand
**ICFES:** Comprensión de textos
**Context:** Read: "Coffee is grown in Colombia."

### Enunciado
What is the main focus of this passive sentence?

### Options
- [ ] A) The people who grow the coffee <!-- feedback: Incorrect. The agent is not mentioned. -->
- [x] B) The coffee itself (what happens to it) <!-- feedback: Correct. The passive voice focuses on the action and the receiver of the action, not the doer. -->
- [ ] C) The process of growing coffee <!-- feedback: Incorrect. The sentence states a fact, not a process. -->
- [ ] D) The farmers of Colombia <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Farmers are not mentioned. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
The passive voice is used when the agent (doer) is unknown, obvious, or unimportant. "Coffee is grown in Colombia" focuses on the product (coffee), not on who grows it. The agent "by farmers" could be added but is unnecessary.

### Enunciado
What does this sentence imply about the sequence of events?

### Options
- [ ] A) The rescue team found shelter first <!-- feedback: Incorrect. The team arrived later. -->
- [ ] B) The hikers were looking for shelter when the team arrived <!-- feedback: Incorrect. They had already found it. -->
- [x] C) The hikers found shelter, and THEN the rescue team arrived <!-- feedback: Correct. Past perfect (had found) indicates the earlier action. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
"By the time" is a common time expression used with Past Perfect. It shows that one action was completed before another action started. The earlier action (had found) uses past perfect; the later action (arrived) uses simple past.

## Question 10 [D6]

**ID:** `CO-ENG-9-2026-P1-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v10`
**Bloom:** Apply
**ICFES:** Competencia léxica y gramatical
**Context:** A news report about the Cartagena Film Festival.

### Enunciado
Change to passive voice: "The committee selected the winners last night."

### Options
- [ ] A) The winners selected the committee last night <!-- feedback: Incorrect. The meaning is reversed. -->
- [ ] B) The winners were select by the committee <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Select" is not the past participle; it's "selected." -->
- [ ] C) The winners were selected by the committee last night <!-- feedback: Correct. Object (winners) becomes subject, "were" + past participle (selected) + agent (by the committee). -->
- [ ] D) The committee was selected by the winners <!-- feedback: Incorrect. The agent and subject are reversed. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
To change active to passive: 1) Object becomes subject, 2) Add appropriate form of "to be," 3) Main verb in past participle, 4) Add "by + agent" if needed. Active: "The committee (subject) selected (verb) the winners (object)."

**ICFES:** Comprensión de textos
**Context:** A student reads: "He hadn't visited his hometown in ten years before he went back for the festival."

What does the negative Past Perfect form express here?

### Options
- [ ] A) He visited his hometown many times <!-- feedback: Incorrect. The sentence says he hadn't visited. -->
- [x] B) The last visit was more than ten years before the festival visit <!-- feedback: Correct. Past perfect negative shows an action that didn't happen before another past action. -->
- [ ] C) He never visited his hometown at all <!-- feedback: Incorrect. The sentence doesn't say "never" generally, just not in the ten years before the festival. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
Negative Past Perfect (hadn't + past participle) shows that an action was NOT completed before another past action. It's useful for expressing regret or surprise: "I hadn't realized how much the city had changed."

## Question 13 [D8]

**ID:** `CO-ENG-9-2026-P1-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v13`
**Bloom:** Evaluate
**ICFES:** Competencia léxica y gramatical
**Context:** A student must choose between two tenses for this sentence:
"Sofia ___ (visit) Cartagena before, so she knew the best restaurants."

### Enunciado
Which tense is correct and why?

### Options
- [ ] A) Visited - simple past, because the visit is in the past <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Simple past suggests the visit was recent but doesn't establish the sequence clearly. -->
- [x] B) Had visited - past perfect, because the visit happened before she knew the restaurants. The sentence shows a clear sequence of two past events. <!-- feedback: Correct. Past perfect clarifies which action happened first. -->
- [ ] C) Has visited - present perfect, because it's connected to the present <!-- feedback: Incorrect. The main clause is in simple past (knew). -->
- [ ] D) Was visiting - past continuous, because it was in progress <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Past continuous doesn't fit here. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
Choose Past Perfect when you need to make clear that an action happened before another past action. If the sequence is obvious from context (e.g., with "after" or "before"), simple past can sometimes work, but past perfect is more precise.

### Enunciado
Which paragraph correctly uses both structures?

### Options
- [ ] A) "The Gold Museum opens in 1939. Many tourists visit it every year." <!-- feedback: Incorrect. No past perfect or passive voice used. -->
