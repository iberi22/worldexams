# Bundle Mastery: Modal Verbs for Deduction and B1 Reading (CEFR B1)

This bundle covers modal verbs used for deduction and probability (must, might, can't, could) and B1-level reading comprehension.

---

## Question 1 [D3]

**ID:** `CO-ENG-9-2026-P4-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v1`
**Bloom:** Remember
**ICFES:** Comprensión de textos
**Context:** A student in Bogotá is learning about modal verbs.

### Enunciado
Which modal verb is used to express a strong certainty that something is true?

### Options
- [ ] A) Might <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Might" expresses possibility (50% or less certainty). -->
- [x] B) Must <!-- feedback: Correct. "Must" for deduction means "I am almost sure this is true" (90%+ certainty). -->
- [ ] C) Can't <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Can't" expresses certainty that something is NOT true. -->
- [ ] D) Could <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Could" expresses possibility, not strong certainty. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
Modal verbs for deduction (logical conclusions): must (very sure it's true), might/may/could (it's possible), can't (very sure it's NOT true). Examples: "He must be tired" (he worked all day). "He can't be tired" (he just woke up).

## Question 2 [D3]

**ID:** `CO-ENG-9-2026-P4-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v2`
**Bloom:** Remember
**ICFES:** Comprensión de textos
**Context:** A teacher explains skimming and scanning techniques for reading.

### Enunciado
What is "skimming" in reading?

### Options
- [ ] A) Reading every word carefully <!-- feedback: Incorrect. That's intensive reading. -->
- [x] B) Reading quickly to get the main idea or general meaning of a text <!-- feedback: Correct. Skimming is looking over a text to understand the general content without reading every word. -->
- [ ] C) Looking for specific information like a date or name <!-- feedback: Incorrect. That's scanning. -->
- [ ] D) Reading a text multiple times <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Repetition is not skimming. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
Skimming and scanning are two important reading strategies. Skimming = reading for the general idea (read titles, first sentences of paragraphs). Scanning = looking for specific information (scan for a date, name, or number). Both are tested in Saber 11 English.

## Question 3 [D4]

**ID:** `CO-ENG-9-2026-P4-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v3`
**Bloom:** Understand
**ICFES:** Comprensión de textos
**Context:** Read: "The ground is wet. It must have rained last night."

### Enunciado
What does the speaker express with "must have rained"?

### Options
- [ ] A) Certainty about a past event based on evidence <!-- feedback: Correct. "Must have + past participle" expresses a logical conclusion about the past. -->
- [ ] B) A future prediction about rain <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Must have" refers to the past. -->
- [ ] C) A possibility that it rained <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Might have" would express possibility. -->
- [ ] D) A suggestion to go out in the rain <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Not a suggestion. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
"Must have + past participle" is used for past deduction. The speaker sees evidence (wet ground) and draws a logical conclusion (rained last night). This is different from "must" for present deduction (must be).

## Question 8 [D6]

**ID:** `CO-ENG-9-2026-P4-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v8`
**Bloom:** Apply
**ICFES:** Competencia léxica y gramatical
**Context:** A student sees smoke coming from a building and hears sirens.

### Enunciado
Choose the correct deduction: "There's smoke. There ___ be a fire."

### Options
- [ ] A) can't <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Can't" would express impossibility. -->
- [x] B) must <!-- feedback: Correct. Smoke + sirens = logical conclusion: there must be a fire. -->
- [ ] C) might <!-- feedback: Incorrect. The evidence is strong, so "must" (high certainty) is better than "might" (possibility). -->
- [ ] D) should <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Should" expresses expectation or advice. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
The level of certainty should match the evidence. Strong evidence (smoke, sirens) = must. Some evidence (a person has a wet umbrella) = might. Contradictory evidence (the person has an umbrella but no rain) = can't.

## Question 15 [D9]

**ID:** `CO-ENG-9-2026-P4-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v15`
**Bloom:** Create
**ICFES:** Comprensión de textos
**Context:** A student needs to demonstrate understanding of a complete B1-level text and deduction modals.

### Enunciado
Read the text and choose the best inference:
"Carlos woke up late. His exam started at 8:00 AM. It was now 7:45 AM and he lived 30 minutes away. He ran out the door without eating breakfast."

What can Carlos be thinking?

### Options
- [ ] A) "I have plenty of time. I can walk slowly." <!-- feedback: Incorrect. He woke up late and has little time. -->
- [ ] B) "I must hurry. I can't arrive late to the exam." <!-- feedback: Correct. This is a logical thought: he must hurry (deduction about necessity) and can't arrive late (impossibility/prohibition). -->
- [ ] C) "I might have woken up early." <!-- feedback: Incorrect. He woke up late. -->
- [ ] D) "I can't have an exam today." <!-- feedback: Incorrect. He knows there is an exam. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
This question combines reading comprehension with modal verb use. Understanding the situation (woke up late, exam soon, 30 min away) leads to the logical conclusion: he must hurry and can't be late.
