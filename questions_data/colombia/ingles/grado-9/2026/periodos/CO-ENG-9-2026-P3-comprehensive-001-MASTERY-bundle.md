# Bundle Mastery: Phrasal Verbs and Narrative Writing (CEFR B1)

This bundle covers common phrasal verbs and the basics of narrative writing at the B1 level.

---

## Question 1 [D3]

**ID:** `CO-ENG-9-2026-P3-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v1`
**Bloom:** Remember
**ICFES:** Competencia léxica y gramatical
**Context:** A student in Bogotá is learning phrasal verbs.

### Enunciado
What is a phrasal verb?

### Options
- [ ] A) A verb that has no object <!-- feedback: Incorrect. That's an intransitive verb. -->
- [x] B) A verb combined with a preposition or adverb that creates a new meaning <!-- feedback: Correct. Phrasal verbs = verb + particle (up, off, out, in) with a different meaning from the original verb. -->
- [ ] C) A verb in past tense form <!-- feedback: Incorrect. That's not related to phrasal verbs. -->
- [ ] D) A verb that is always followed by "to" <!-- feedback: Incorrect. That's a verb followed by infinitive. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
Phrasal verbs are combinations of a verb + one or two particles (prepositions or adverbs). The meaning is often different from the original verb. Example: "give" (dar) vs "give up" (rendirse), "look" (mirar) vs "look for" (buscar).

**ICFES:** Competencia léxica y gramatical

What are the basic elements of a narrative text?

### Options
- [ ] A) Introduction, arguments, and conclusion <!-- feedback: Incorrect. That's an argumentative text. -->
- [x] B) Characters, setting, plot (beginning, middle, end), and conflict <!-- feedback: Correct. Narratives tell a story and include characters, a setting, a sequence of events, and often a conflict. -->

### Options
- [ ] A) To rotate something <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Turn" alone means rotate, but "turn off" is different. -->
- [x] B) To stop something from working (switch off) <!-- feedback: Correct. "Turn off" means to deactivate or stop the flow of electricity. -->

**ICFES:** Comprensión de textos

### Enunciado
What is the difference between "give up" and "give away"?

### Options
- [ ] A) They mean the same thing <!-- feedback: Incorrect. The particles change the meaning. -->
- [ ] B) "Give up" = stop trying; "give away" = donate or give for free <!-- feedback: Correct. Different particles create different meanings. -->
- [ ] C) "Give up" = donate; "give away" = stop trying <!-- feedback: Incorrect. The meanings are reversed. -->

The particle (up, away, in, out, off) significantly changes the meaning of a phrasal verb. This is why learning phrasal verbs as vocabulary units (verb + particle together) is more effective.

What does "look forward to" mean?

- [ ] B) To expect something bad to happen <!-- feedback: Incorrect. It's positive. -->
