**Context:** Asking to borrow something.

### Enunciado
You forgot your pencil. What do you say to your classmate?

### Options
- [ ] A) Give me your pencil now <!-- feedback: Incorrect. This is too direct and impolite. -->
- [x] B) Can I borrow your pencil, please? <!-- feedback: Correct. This is a polite request. "Can I borrow" + "please" is the correct form. -->
- [ ] C) Where is your pencil <!-- feedback: Incorrect. You want to ask for a pencil, not ask where theirs is. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
This question tests polite requests using school supplies context. "Can I borrow your pencil, please?" (¿Me prestas tu lápiz, por favor?) is the appropriate polite expression. Errors include being too direct or using incorrect phrases.

## Question 7 [D4]

**ID:** `CO-ING-4-2026-W06-school-supplies-001-MASTERY-v7`
**Bloom:** [Apply]
**ICFES:** [Comunicación y representación]
**Context:** Possessives with school supplies.

### Enunciado
Maria has a red pencil. How do you say this in English?

### Options
- [ ] A) The pencil red is Maria <!-- feedback: Incorrect. The word order is wrong. -->
- [ ] B) The Maria's pencil is red <!-- feedback: Incorrect. The possessive "Maria's" should come after the article, not before. -->
- [x] C) Maria's pencil is red <!-- feedback: Correct. "Maria's" shows possession: the pencil that belongs to Maria is red. -->
- [ ] D) The red pencil is of Maria <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Of Maria" is not the correct possessive form in English; use 's. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
This question tests the possessive form ('s) in English, applied to school supplies. "Maria's pencil" means "the pencil of Maria" (el lápiz de María). In English, we use 's for possession: noun + 's + object.
