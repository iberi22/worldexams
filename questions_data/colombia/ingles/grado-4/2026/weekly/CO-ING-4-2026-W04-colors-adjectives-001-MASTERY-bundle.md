# Weekly Pack W04: Colors and Adjectives (CEFR A1)

Este bundle evalúa el vocabulario de colores y adjetivos básicos en inglés, incluyendo descripciones de objetos, personas y lugares usando combinaciones de color + adjetivo. Nivel A1. Contexto colombiano.

## Question 1 [D1]

**ID:** `CO-ING-4-2026-W04-colors-adjectives-001-MASTERY-v1`
**Bloom:** [Remember]
**ICFES:** [Comunicación y representación]
**Context:** Identifying basic colors.

### Enunciado
What color is a **banana**?

### Options
- [ ] A) Red <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Bananas are not red. -->
- [ ] B) Green <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Some unripe bananas are green, but a ripe banana is not green. -->
- [x] C) Yellow <!-- feedback: Correct. A ripe banana is yellow (amarillo). -->
- [ ] D) Blue <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Bananas are never blue. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
This question tests basic color-object association. Students should know that a ripe banana is yellow. Common errors include confusing the color of a fruit with another fruit (apples are red, oranges are orange).

The opposite of **big** is:

### Options

## Question 4 [D2]

**ID:** `CO-ING-4-2026-W04-colors-adjectives-001-MASTERY-v4`
**Bloom:** [Understand]
**ICFES:** [Comunicación y representación]
**Context:** Understanding color + noun order.

### Enunciado
In English, which sentence is **correct**?

### Options
- [ ] A) A red beautiful flower <!-- feedback: Incorrect. In English, opinion adjectives (beautiful) come BEFORE color adjectives. -->
- [x] B) A beautiful red flower <!-- feedback: Correct. Opinion (beautiful) + color (red) + noun (flower) is the correct order. -->
- [ ] C) A flower red beautiful <!-- feedback: Incorrect. In English, adjectives come BEFORE the noun. -->
- [ ] D) Red beautiful a flower <!-- feedback: Incorrect. The word order is completely wrong. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
This question tests adjective order in English. The basic rule is: opinion + size + color + noun. "A beautiful red flower" follows this pattern. In Spanish, adjectives often come after the noun, so this is a common challenge for Colombian students.
