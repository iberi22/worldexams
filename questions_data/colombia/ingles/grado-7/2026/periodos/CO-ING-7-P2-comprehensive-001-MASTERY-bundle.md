# Bundle Mastery: English Grade 7 — Period 2

This bundle covers future (will and going to), present continuous, and expressing opinions (agreeing and disagreeing). Topics for the second period of Grade 7, CEFR A2 level.

---

## Question 1 (Difficulty D3)

**ID:** `CO-ING-7-P2-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v1`
**Bloom:** Remember
**ICFES:** Comunicación Lingüística

### Contexto
In English class at Colegio San José in Manizales, the teacher asks: "How do we express a future plan using 'going to'?"

### Enunciado
Which sentence correctly uses "going to" for a future plan?

### Options
- [ ] A) I go to visit my grandparents tomorrow. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. This is present simple, not future. -->
- [ ] B) I am going to visit my grandparents tomorrow. <!-- feedback: Correct! "Am going to" expresses a future plan. -->
- [ ] C) I will going to visit my grandparents tomorrow. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Don't mix "will" and "going to". -->
- [ ] D) I am going visit my grandparents tomorrow. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. You need "to" after "going". -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
"Going to" is used for planned future actions. Structure: subject + am/is/are + going to + base verb. Example: "She is going to study medicine."

## Question 2 (Difficulty D3)

**ID:** `CO-ING-7-P2-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v2`
**Bloom:** Remember
**ICFES:** Comunicación Lingüística

### Contexto
The teacher shows this sentence: "She _____ (read) a book right now."

### Enunciado
What is the correct present continuous form?

### Options
- [ ] A) She reads a book right now. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Present simple is for habits, not actions in progress. -->
- [ ] B) She is reading a book right now. <!-- feedback: Correct! Present continuous for actions happening now. -->
- [ ] C) She read a book right now. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. This is past or base form. -->
- [ ] D) She is read a book right now. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. You need the -ing form after "be". -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
Present continuous (am/is/are + verb-ing) is used for actions happening at the moment of speaking. "Right now" is a key time expression for this tense.

### Options
- [ ] A) It will rain. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Will" is for predictions without evidence. -->

## Question 12 (Difficulty D8)

**ID:** `CO-ING-7-P2-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v12`
**Bloom:** Evaluate
**ICFES:** Comunicación Lingüística

### Contexto
Read and find the error:
"I am go to the cinema with my friends tonight. We will to see the new superhero movie."

### Enunciado
How many grammar errors are in these sentences?

### Options
- [ ] A) 0 <!-- feedback: Incorrect. There are errors. -->
- [ ] B) 1 <!-- feedback: Incorrect. There are two errors. -->
- [x] C) 2 <!-- feedback: Correct! "I am go" → "I am going" (missing -ing). "We will to see" → "We will see" (remove "to"). -->
- [ ] D) 3 <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Exactly two errors. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
Two common errors: (1) Present continuous needs verb-ing: "I am going" not "I am go". (2) After "will" use base form: "will see" not "will to see". Will is always followed by the base verb.

## Question 15 (Difficulty D10)

**ID:** `CO-ING-7-P2-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v15`
**Bloom:** Evaluate
**ICFES:** Comunicación Lingüística

### Contexto
A student is writing about future plans but keeps changing between "will" and "going to". The teacher explains when to use each.

### Enunciado
When should you use "will" instead of "going to"?

### Options
- [ ] A) For pre-arranged plans. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Going to" is for plans. -->
- [ ] B) For predictions with evidence. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Going to" is for predictions with evidence. -->
- [ ] C) For spontaneous decisions and promises. <!-- feedback: Correct! "Will" is for decisions made at the moment of speaking. -->
- [ ] D) For actions happening right now. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. That's present continuous. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
"Will" is used for: (1) spontaneous decisions ("The phone is ringing. I'll answer it."), (2) promises ("I will help you"), (3) offers ("I'll carry that for you"), (4) predictions without evidence ("I think it will rain tomorrow").
