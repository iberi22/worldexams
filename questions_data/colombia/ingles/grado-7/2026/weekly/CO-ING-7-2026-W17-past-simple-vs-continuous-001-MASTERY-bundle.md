# MASTERY Bundle - Inglés: Past Simple vs Past Continuous (When/While) (W17)

This bundle focuses on the contrast between the Past Simple (interrupted action/short event) and the Past Continuous (background action/long event) using "when" and "while".

---

## Question 1 [D3]
**ID:** CO-ING-7-2026-W17-past-simple-vs-continuous-001-v1
**Bloom:** Remember
**ICFES:** Grammatical Knowledge
**Expected_Success:** 0.85
**Contexto:** Identifying the typical connector for interrupted actions.

### Enunciado
Which word is most commonly used to introduce the short action that interrupts a longer background action?

### Opciones
- [ ] A) While
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "While" is usually followed by the long action (Continuous). -->
- [x] B) When
  <!-- feedback: Correct! "When" is typically followed by the short action (Simple). -->
- [ ] C) Then
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. This is a sequence connector. -->
- [ ] D) If
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. This is a conditional connector. -->

### Explicacion Pedagogica
The student identifies the grammatical function of the connector "when" in past narratives.

### Enunciado
Complete: "I ________ (read) a book when the phone ________ (ring)."

### Opciones
- [ ] A) read / rang
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Both are simple past; doesn't show the background/interruption relationship. -->
- [x] B) was reading / rang
  <!-- feedback: Correct! "Was reading" is the background action, and "rang" is the interruption. -->
- [ ] C) reading / ringed
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Missing "was" and "ring" is irregular. -->
- [ ] D) readed / was ringing
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Read" is irregular and the tenses are reversed. -->

### Enunciado

### Opciones

  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "While" is normally followed by the continuous form. -->
