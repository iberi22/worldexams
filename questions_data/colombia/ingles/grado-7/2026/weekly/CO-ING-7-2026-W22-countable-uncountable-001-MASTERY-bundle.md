# MASTERY Bundle - Inglés: Countable vs Uncountable Nouns (W22)

This bundle focuses on distinguishing between countable and uncountable nouns, particularly in the context of food and shopping, and using "a/an" and "some" correctly.

---

## Question 1 [D3]
**ID:** CO-ING-7-2026-W22-countable-uncountable-001-v1
**Bloom:** Remember
**ICFES:** Lexical Knowledge
**Expected_Success:** 0.85
**Contexto:** Identifying a countable noun.

### Enunciado
Which of these nouns can be counted (1, 2, 3...)?

### Opciones
- [ ] A) Water
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Water is a liquid and generally uncountable. -->
- [ ] B) Rice
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Rice consists of many small grains and is uncountable. -->
- [x] C) Apple
  <!-- feedback: Correct! You can have one apple, two apples, etc. -->
- [ ] D) Sugar
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Sugar is a powder/granule substance and is uncountable. -->

### Explicacion Pedagogica
The student identifies basic countable nouns among uncountable substances.

**Contexto:** Using "a/an" with singular countable nouns.

### Enunciado
Complete the sentence: "I want to eat ________ orange."

### Opciones
- [ ] A) a
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Use "an" before a vowel sound. -->
- [x] B) an
  <!-- feedback: Correct! "Orange" starts with a vowel sound. -->

  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. While possible for plural or uncountable, for a single item "an" is more specific here. -->

  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Grammatically wrong combination. -->

**Expected_Success:** 0.75
**Contexto:** Using "some" with uncountable nouns.

### Enunciado
"Would you like ________ water?"

### Opciones
- [ ] A) a
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. You cannot use "a" with an uncountable noun like water. -->
- [ ] B) an
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. You cannot use "an" with uncountable nouns. -->
- [x] C) some
  <!-- feedback: Correct! "Some" is used with uncountable nouns in offers or affirmative sentences. -->

  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Any" is usually for questions or negatives, but "some" is better for polite offers. -->
