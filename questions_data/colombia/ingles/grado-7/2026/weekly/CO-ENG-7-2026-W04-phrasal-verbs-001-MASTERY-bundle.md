# Bundle Mastery: Phrasal Verbs - Grado 7
## Semana W04 — Common phrasal verbs and their meanings

## Pregunta 1 [D3]
**ID:** `CO-ENG-7-2026-W04-phrasal-verbs-001-MASTERY-v1`
**Bloom:** Remember
**ICFES:** Uso comprensivo del conocimiento científico
**Context:** In English class at Colegio San Jorge de Inglaterra in Bogotá, the teacher introduces phrasal verbs.
**Expected_Success:** 0.85

### Enunciado
What is a phrasal verb?

### Opciones
- [ ] A) A single verb that has only one meaning. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. -->
- [ ] B) A noun formed from a verb. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. -->
- [x] C) A verb combined with a preposition or adverb that creates a new meaning. <!-- feedback: Correct. Phrasal verbs combine a verb + particle. -->
- [ ] D) A verb that never changes form. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. -->

### Explicacion Pedagogica
A phrasal verb is a combination of a verb + one or two particles (prepositions or adverbs) that together have a different meaning from the original verb. For example: "look" (mirar) + "after" (después) = "look after" (cuidar). The meaning of "look after" cannot be guessed from the individual words. Phrasal verbs are very common in English, especially in spoken language.

**ICFES:** Explicación de fenómenos
**Context:** Carlos hears: "Please turn off the lights." He wants to know what "turn off" means in Cali.
**Expected_Success:** 0.80

### Enunciado
What does "turn off" mean?

### Opciones
- [x] A) To switch something on. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Turn on" is to switch on. -->
- [ ] B) To leave a place. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. -->
- [ ] C) To stop or deactivate an electronic device. <!-- feedback: Correct. "Turn off the TV" = apagar la TV. -->
- [ ] D) To change direction. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. That is "turn around". -->

### Explicacion Pedagogica
"Turn off" is a separable phrasal verb meaning to stop the operation of an electronic device or light. The opposite is "turn on" (encender). Example: "Please turn off your cell phones during class." It is separable: you can say "turn off the light" or "turn the light off." When using a pronoun, it must go in the middle: "turn it off" (NOT "turn off it").

What is the difference between "look for" and "look after"?

## Pregunta 7 [D6]
**ID:** `CO-ENG-7-2026-W04-phrasal-verbs-001-MASTERY-v7`
**Bloom:** Analyze
**ICFES:** Indagación
**Context:** Valentina compares separable and inseparable phrasal verbs in Pereira.
**Expected_Success:** 0.68

### Enunciado
Which sentence is grammatically INCORRECT?

### Opciones
- [ ] A) Please pick up your toys. <!-- feedback: Correct. Separable. -->
- [ ] B) Please pick your toys up. <!-- feedback: Correct. Separable with noun. -->
- [x] C) Please pick up them. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Pronoun must go between: "pick them up." -->
- [ ] D) Please pick them up. <!-- feedback: Correct. Pronoun in middle. -->
Phrasal verbs can be separable or inseparable. With separable phrasal verbs like "pick up": nouns can go between or after the particle ("pick the toys up" / "pick up the toys"). But PRONOUNS (it, them, him, her) MUST go between: "pick them up" (NOT "pick up them"). Other separable examples: turn on/off, put on, take off.

### Enunciado
Which sentence correctly uses an INSEPARABLE phrasal verb?

### Opciones
- [ ] A) She looks after her grandmother. <!-- feedback: Correct. "Look after" is inseparable. -->
- [ ] B) She looks her grandmother after. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Inseparable verbs cannot be split. -->
- [ ] C) She looks after her. <!-- feedback: Correct too, but with pronoun. -->
- [x] D) Both A and C. <!-- feedback: Correct. Both are correct because "look after" is inseparable. -->
Inseparable phrasal verbs always keep the verb and particle together. The object must go after the particle. Examples: look after (cuidar), look for (buscar), get over (superar), run into (encontrarse con). "She looks after her grandmother" ✓, "She looks after her" ✓. But NOT "She looks her grandmother after" ✗.

### Enunciado
What does "give up" mean and what form follows it?

### Opciones
- [ ] A) "Give up" means to continue; followed by infinitive. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. -->
- [ ] B) "Give up" means to start; followed by base verb. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. -->
- [x] C) "Give up" means to quit or stop doing something; followed by gerund (-ing). <!-- feedback: Correct. "Give up smoking" = dejar de fumar. -->

### Enunciado
Is "I look forward to hear from you" correct?

### Opciones
- [ ] A) Yes, it's perfect. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. -->
- [ ] B) No, it should be "I look forward to hearing from you." <!-- feedback: Correct. "To" here is a preposition, not part of infinitive. -->
- [ ] C) No, "look forward" doesn't exist. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. -->

Correct: "I look forward to hearing from you." ✗ Incorrect: "I look forward to hear from you." This is a very common mistake!
