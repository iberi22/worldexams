## Part 6: Reading Comprehension

### Text: Emergence and the Limits of Reductionism
For centuries, the scientific method has relied heavily on reductionism—the idea that a system can be understood by breaking it down into its smallest components. However, complexity theory argues that this approach fails to account for "emergent properties"—characteristics of a system that only appear when parts interact as a whole. A single water molecule is not "wet," but a collection of billions of them is. Similarly, a single neuron is not "conscious," but the interaction of billions of them gives rise to the human mind. This "bottom-up" complexity suggests that we cannot predict the behavior of a forest just by studying individual trees, or the stability of an economy just by studying individual consumers. The challenge for 21st-century science is to develop new mathematical tools that can capture the holistic dynamics of these emergent systems.
