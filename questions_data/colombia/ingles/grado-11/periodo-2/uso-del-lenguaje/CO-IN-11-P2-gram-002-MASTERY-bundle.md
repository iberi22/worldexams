### Text: Hydrothermal Vents and the Origin of Life
Deep on the ocean floor, along the boundaries of tectonic plates, lie hydrothermal vents—underwater volcanoes that spew mineral-rich water heated by the Earth's mantle. Unlike most ecosystems on Earth, which rely on sunlight for energy (photosynthesis), the life forms around these vents rely on chemicals (chemosynthesis). Some scientists believe that these vents may have been the "cradle of life" on Earth, providing the necessary heat and chemical energy for the first self-replicating molecules to emerge. However, these same mineral deposits that sustain life are now targeted for deep-sea mining. The conflict between scientific curiosity, environmental protection, and economic extraction is nowhere more apparent than in these extreme and alien environments.
