## Part 6: Reading Comprehension

### Text: The Inner Morality of Law
Lon L. Fuller, a prominent legal philosopher, rejected both strict natural law and extreme legal positivism. In his book *The Morality of Law*, he argued that for a legal system to be valid, it must possess an "inner morality"—a set of procedural requirements that make law possible. These requirements include that laws must be general, public, non-retroactive, clear, and consistent. For Fuller, a system that fails to meet these criteria is not merely "bad law"; it is not law at all. He famously used the example of King Rex, a well-meaning but incompetent ruler who fails to create a functioning legal system because his decrees are secret, contradictory, or constantly changing. Fuller's work suggests that justice is not just about the content of laws, but about the very structure of the legal process itself.
