## Part 6: Reading Comprehension

### Text: The Data-Driven City: Governance or Surveillance?
The promise of the smart city lies in its ability to use "big data" to optimize urban life. Sensors can detect leaks in water pipes, adjust street lighting based on pedestrian presence, and optimize waste collection routes. This data-driven governance promises to make cities more responsive and resource-efficient. However, critics like Evgeny Morozov warn of "solutionism"—the belief that all complex social problems can be solved with technical fixes. Furthermore, the constant collection of data from public spaces raises profound questions about the "right to the city" and the potential for a "digital panopticon," where citizens are constantly monitored without their explicit consent. The challenge is to ensure that the smart city serves the needs of all its inhabitants, rather than just the interests of the tech companies that build its infrastructure.
