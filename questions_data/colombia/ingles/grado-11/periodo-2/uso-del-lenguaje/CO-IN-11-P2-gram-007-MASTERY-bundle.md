## Part 6: Reading Comprehension

### Text: The "Humanity" of Genetic Modification
Philosopher Jürgen Habermas has expressed concern that germline editing could fundamentally alter the relationship between generations. In his view, a child who has been genetically "designed" is no longer the author of their own life in the same way as one born through the "contingency" of nature. The "designed" child may feel a sense of obligation or resentment toward their parents' choices, potentially undermining their sense of autonomy and equality. Habermas argues that our shared human identity depends on a certain degree of unpredictability and "givenness." By attempting to control the genetic makeup of our descendants, we may be inadvertently transforming ourselves into a different kind of being—one whose identity is a product of technical planning rather than natural growth.
