## Part 6: Reading Comprehension

### Text: Thomas Kuhn and the Structure of Scientific Revolutions
In contrast to Popper's gradualist view of scientific progress, Thomas Kuhn argued in his landmark book, *The Structure of Scientific Revolutions*, that science progresses through "paradigm shifts." For Kuhn, most science is "normal science"—work done within a stable framework of shared assumptions (the paradigm). However, over time, "anomalies" build up—observations that the current paradigm cannot explain. When these anomalies become too significant to ignore, a "crisis" occurs, leading to a revolutionary shift to a new paradigm. Kuhn famously argued that different paradigms are "incommensurable," meaning they cannot be directly compared because they represent fundamentally different ways of seeing the world.
