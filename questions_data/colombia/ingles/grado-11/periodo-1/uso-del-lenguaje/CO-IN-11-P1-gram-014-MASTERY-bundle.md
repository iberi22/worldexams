## Part 6: Reading Comprehension

### Text: Sovereignty and the Law of the Sea
The United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS) provides the primary legal framework for maritime boundaries and resource rights in the Arctic. Under UNCLOS, coastal states like Russia, Canada, and the United States have an Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ) extending 200 nautical miles from their shores. However, countries can claim rights to an even larger area if they can prove that their continental shelf extends beyond the 200-mile limit. This has led to overlapping and contentious claims, most notably regarding the Lomonosov Ridge, a massive underwater mountain range that both Russia and Denmark (via Greenland) claim as an extension of their territory. The resolution of these claims is a slow, technical process managed by the Commission on the Limits of the Continental Shelf (CLCS), but the political stakes—involving future oil and gas wealth—often override scientific diplomacy.
