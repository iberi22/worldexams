## Part 6: Reading Comprehension

### Text: The Architecture of Choice and Nudge Theory
In their influential book *Nudge*, Richard Thaler and Cass Sunstein argue that there is no such thing as a "neutral" choice architecture. Every environment, from the layout of a school cafeteria to the design of a retirement plan, inevitably steers individuals toward certain outcomes. "Nudging" involves designing these environments—choice architectures—to encourage better decisions while preserving the freedom to choose. A classic example is making "organ donation" the default option on driver's license applications, which significantly increases registration rates compared to an "opt-in" system. While proponents celebrate nudging as a tool for public good, critics warn that it can be a form of "libertarian paternalism" that undermines individual agency by manipulating subconscious biases without explicit consent.
