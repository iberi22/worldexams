## Part 6: Reading Comprehension

### Text: The Ethics of Public Aesthetics
Do we have a "right" to a beautiful city? This question lies at the heart of the debate over urban aesthetics. Some philosophers argue that because we are forced to inhabit public spaces, the design of these spaces has a profound impact on our psychological well-being. From this perspective, ugly or hostile architecture (such as "defensive design" intended to discourage loitering) is a form of civic harm. However, others contend that beauty is purely subjective and that prioritizing "aesthetics" often leads to gentrification and the displacement of marginalized communities. In this view, a city should prioritize "social affordances"—the ways in which a space allows for diverse human interactions—over its visual appearance. The challenge for modern planners is to reconcile these two demands: creating spaces that are both aesthetically enriching and socially inclusive.
