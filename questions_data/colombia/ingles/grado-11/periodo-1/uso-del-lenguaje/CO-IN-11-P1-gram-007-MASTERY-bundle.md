### Text: The Commodification of the Human Genome
The debate over gene editing often centers on the distinction between "therapy"—fixing a disease—and "enhancement"—improving a healthy trait. However, critics like Michael Sandel argue that this distinction is becoming increasingly fragile. When we treat genetic traits as consumer choices, we risk losing the sense of life as a "gift." In an era of neoliberal economics, the pressure to "optimize" ourselves and our children could turn human beings into biological products. Furthermore, if these enhancements are only available to those who can afford them, we might see the emergence of a permanent biological underclass, where inequality is no longer just socio-economic, but hard-coded into our DNA.
