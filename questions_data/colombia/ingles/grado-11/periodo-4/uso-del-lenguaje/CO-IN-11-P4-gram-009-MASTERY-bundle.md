## Part 6: Reading Comprehension

### Text: Heidegger and the Question Concerning Technology
Martin Heidegger's essay "The Question Concerning Technology" argues that the essence of technology is not something "technological." Instead, technology is a "challenging-forth" of nature—a process he calls *Enframing* (*Gestell*). In this state, the world is viewed purely as a "standing reserve"—a collection of resources waiting to be optimized and consumed. For Heidegger, the danger is not just that machines might fail, but that humans will lose their ability to experience any other way of revealing the world (such as through art or poetry). We risk becoming just another part of the standing reserve, valued only for our data or our productivity. The challenge is to find a "free relationship" with technology, where we use tools without allowing the technological mindset to dominate our entire existence.
