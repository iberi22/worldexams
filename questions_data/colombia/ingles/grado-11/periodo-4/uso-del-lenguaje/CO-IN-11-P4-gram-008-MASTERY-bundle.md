## Part 6: Reading Comprehension

### Text: Hayden White and the Metahistory of Narrative
In his influential work, *Metahistory*, Hayden White argued that historical writing is not a scientific mirrors of reality, but a "literary artifact." White contended that historians inevitably use "tropes"—metaphorical and rhetorical devices—to give meaning to the past. He identified four primary modes of "emplotment" (Romance, Tragedy, Comedy, and Satire) that historians use to structure their narratives. For White, there is no such thing as a "neutral" history; the choice of emplotment is itself an ideological and aesthetic decision. This "linguistic turn" in history suggests that the "truth" of a historical work is as much about its internal narrative consistency and rhetorical power as it is about its factual accuracy.
