## Part 6: Reading Comprehension

### Text: Roland Barthes and the Mythology of the Everyday
In his book *Mythologies*, Roland Barthes applied semiotic analysis to the "popular culture" of 1950s France—from professional wrestling to wine and advertising. Barthes argued that culture creates "myths"—second-order semiotic systems where a simple sign is transformed into a carrier of ideological meaning. For example, a picture of a French soldier saluting the flag is not just a sign of "soldier" and "flag"; at the mythical level, it becomes a signifier for "French imperialism" or "national duty." Barthes's goal was to "de-naturalize" these myths, showing that what we often take for granted as "natural" or "obvious" is actually a carefully constructed social product that serves the interests of a particular power structure.
