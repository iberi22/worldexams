## Part 6: Reading Comprehension

### Text: The Prisoner's Dilemma and the Logic of Reciprocity
The Prisoner's Dilemma is a fundamental concept in game theory that illustrates why two completely "rational" individuals might not cooperate, even if it is in their best interest to do so. In the classic scenario, two prisoners are interrogated separately. If both stay silent, they get a light sentence (cooperation). If one betrays the other, the defector goes free while the other gets a heavy sentence (defection). If both betray each other, both get a moderate sentence. The "rational" choice for an individual in a single encounter is to defect. However, Robert Axelrod's research on the *iterated* Prisoner's Dilemma showed that when the same players meet repeatedly, a strategy called "Tit-for-Tat" (starting with cooperation and then copying the opponent's last move) consistently wins. This suggests that the expectation of future interaction is a powerful catalyst for the evolution of trust and mutual aid.
