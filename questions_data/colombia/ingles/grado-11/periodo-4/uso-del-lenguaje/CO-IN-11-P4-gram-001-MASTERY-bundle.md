### Text: The Y2Q: Preparing for the Quantum Apocalypse
The "Quantum Apocalypse," often referred to as Y2Q (Years to Quantum), describes the moment when quantum computers become powerful enough to break the encryption that currently protects the world's digital data. This includes everything from bank transfers and private emails to government secrets. Unlike the Y2K bug, which was a relatively simple programming issue, Y2Q requires a fundamental redesign of the mathematical foundations of cryptography. Proponents of "Quantum-Resistant" systems argue that we must start the transition now, as it can take decades to update global infrastructure. However, the challenge is not just technical; it is also economic and political, as different nations race to develop their own quantum capabilities while trying to protect their own data from foreign rivals.
