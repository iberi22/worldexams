## Part 6: Reading Comprehension

### Text: De Novo Biology and the Ethics of Creation
One of the most ambitious goals of synthetic biology is "de novo" creation—the building of biological systems from scratch, using standardized genetic parts known as "BioBricks." This approach treats biology as a branch of engineering, where the goal is predictability and control. However, critics like philosopher Mary Midgley argue that this "mechanistic" view of life is fundamentally flawed. If we treat living beings as mere machines, we risk losing our sense of moral responsibility toward them. Furthermore, the possibility of creating entirely new life forms raises the question of "play God"—does humanity have the wisdom to manage the complex and unpredictable consequences of introducing artificial organisms into the global ecosystem?
