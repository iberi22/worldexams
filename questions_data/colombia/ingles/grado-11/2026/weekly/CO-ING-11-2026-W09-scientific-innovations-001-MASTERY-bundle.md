**ID:** CO-ING-11-2026-W09-scientific-innovations-001-MASTERY-bundle-v19
**Bloom:** Evaluate
**ICFES:** Pragmatic Competence
**Expected_Success:** 0.28
**Contexto:** An astronomer at an observatory in Villa de Leyva talks about a rare eclipse.

### Enunciado
Read what the astronomer says.

Astronomer: "A total solar eclipse visible from our town happens once in a blue moon, so the whole observatory team will be working that day."

What does the astronomer mean?

### Opciones
- [ ] A) The eclipse can only be seen when the Moon is blue.
  <!-- feedback: This is a literal reading. 'Once in a blue moon' does not refer to the colour of the Moon. -->
- [ ] B) The eclipse happens every month.
  <!-- feedback: If it happened every month, there would be no reason for the whole team to work that day. -->
- [ ] C) The observatory will be closed during the eclipse.
  <!-- feedback: The whole team will be working, so the observatory will be open and active. -->
- [x] D) The event is very rare.
  <!-- feedback: 'Once in a blue moon' means very rarely; the rarity explains why the whole team will work on that special day. -->

### Explicacion Pedagogica
Frequency idioms such as 'once in a blue moon' are useful in science contexts. The consequence ('so the whole team will be working') supports the meaning: something unusual deserves special attention.

## Question 20 [D9-D10]
**ID:** CO-ING-11-2026-W09-scientific-innovations-001-MASTERY-bundle-v20
**Bloom:** Evaluate
**ICFES:** Pragmatic Competence
**Expected_Success:** 0.25
**Contexto:** A researcher in Bucaramanga explains why her results are not final.

### Enunciado
Read what the researcher says.

Researcher: "We ran out of samples before we could repeat the experiment, so the results are still preliminary."

Why are the results preliminary?

### Opciones
- [ ] A) The samples ran away from the laboratory.
  <!-- feedback: This reads 'ran' literally. Samples cannot move; 'run out of' is a phrasal verb about quantity. -->
- [ ] B) The researchers had too many samples to analyse.
  <!-- feedback: This is the opposite: they had none left. -->
- [ ] C) The experiment was repeated many times.
  <!-- feedback: The researcher says they could not repeat the experiment. -->
- [x] D) There were no samples left to repeat the experiment.
  <!-- feedback: 'Run out of' means to use all of something; without samples, the experiment could not be repeated, so the results are not final. -->

### Explicacion Pedagogica
In science, results become reliable when experiments are repeated. The phrasal verb 'run out of' explains why this did not happen, and 'so' introduces the consequence: the results are still preliminary.
