### Enunciado
The same report points out that speakers use phrases like 'at odds with' to frame a disagreement. What kind of expression is 'at odds with'?

### Opciones
- [x] B) A complex preposition
  <!-- feedback: 'At odds with' behaves as a single prepositional unit, so it is a complex preposition, just like 'in the wake of' or 'on account of'. -->
- [ ] A) A phrasal verb
  <!-- feedback: A phrasal verb is a verb plus a particle with a meaning of its own, as in 'give up', and 'at odds with' has no verb in it. -->
- [ ] C) A gerund phrase
  <!-- feedback: A gerund phrase is built on an -ing verb, but 'at odds with' contains no verb form at all, only prepositions and a noun. -->
- [ ] D) A relative clause
  <!-- feedback: A relative clause would contain a subject and verb joined to a noun by 'who' or 'which', which is not the structure here. -->

### Explicacion Pedagogica
A complex preposition is a multiword unit such as 'at odds with' or 'in the wake of' that expresses one relation; it differs from a phrasal verb, which must contain a verb.
