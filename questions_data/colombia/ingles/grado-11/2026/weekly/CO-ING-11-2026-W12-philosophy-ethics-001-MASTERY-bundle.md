### Enunciado
Complete the following sentence about philosophy & ethics: 'Under no circumstances __________ a philosopher obliged to reach the conclusion the surrounding society finds convenient.' (Topic: fronted negative phrase with a modal of obligation)

### Opciones
- [x] A) is
  <!-- feedback: 'Under no circumstances' is a fronted negative phrase that calls for emphatic inversion, and the copula 'is' is the auxiliary that moves: 'under no circumstances is a philosopher obliged'. The structure is a link verb with 'obliged', not a modal. -->
- [ ] B) are
  <!-- feedback: 'Are' would not agree with the singular subject 'a philosopher'. In an inverted clause the auxiliary still has to match the subject that has moved to the right of it, and one philosopher takes 'is'. -->
- [ ] C) were
  <!-- feedback: 'Were' would either be a plural of 'are', which fails on agreement, or an inverted subjunctive copula used in formal conditionals such as 'were I to choose'. The sentence is a denial of an obligation, not a hypothetical condition. -->
- [ ] D) been
  <!-- feedback: 'Been' is a past participle and cannot open an inverted clause on its own. A fronted negative phrase needs a finite auxiliary in front of the subject, and the only one that agrees here is 'is'. -->

### Explicacion Pedagogica
Fronted negative phrases range in strength from 'by no means' to 'under no circumstances', and all of them invert. C1+ students should learn to identify the inversion trigger first and then supply the auxiliary that agrees in number with the displaced subject, which is what separates 'is' from 'are' in this item.

  <!-- feedback: 'Not until' is a negative fronted adverbial, and the main clause is inverted with 'did': 'not until... did the rest of the lecture make any sense'. The main verb returns to its base form 'make' because the auxiliary now carries the tense and the emphasis. -->
