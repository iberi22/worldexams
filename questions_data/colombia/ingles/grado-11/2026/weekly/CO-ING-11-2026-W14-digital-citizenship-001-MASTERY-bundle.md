### Enunciado
Complete the following sentence about digital citizenship: 'Had the platform logged every content removal and made the record public, none of the disputes in the last two years __________ to have been settled on private messages alone.' (Topic: inverted third conditional with a negative main clause)

### Opciones
- [x] A) would
  <!-- feedback: An inverted third conditional uses 'had' plus the past participle in the first clause and 'would have' plus the participle in the second. Here the second clause is negative, so the sentence becomes 'none of the disputes would have had to be settled on private messages alone'. -->
- [ ] B) will
  <!-- feedback: 'Will' belongs to the present or future, and the whole sentence is set in a counterfactual past. Using it would put the second clause in a different time world from the 'Had' clause that opens the sentence. -->
- [ ] C) must
  <!-- feedback: 'Must' expresses obligation in the present. A third conditional describes what would not have been necessary, and that reasoning is carried by 'would have' plus the infinitive, not by a modal of duty. -->
- [ ] D) had
  <!-- feedback: 'Had' already opens the sentence as the auxiliary of the first clause, and the second clause of a third conditional takes 'would have' rather than repeating the past perfect auxiliary. Two 'had' clauses would leave the main clause unfinished. -->

### Explicacion Pedagogica
Third conditionals are the reason 'had' and 'would have' exist as separate auxiliaries, and at C1+ they are also frequently inverted.
