### Opciones

In the context of digital citizenship, which word best completes the following sentence: 'The school blocked every site that was not on its list, and within a term the students had found ways to __________ the filter entirely without asking for permission'?

### Opciones
- [x] A) circumvent
  <!-- feedback: 'To circumvent' is to get round a rule or obstacle without defeating the obstacle itself. The filter stayed in place and the students found a path around it, which is precisely what circumvention means. -->
- [ ] B) dismantle
  <!-- feedback: 'To dismantle' is to take something apart, which would describe physically breaking the system. The sentence says the filter was bypassed rather than removed, so the verb has to express evasion rather than destruction. -->
- [ ] C) challenge
  <!-- feedback: 'To challenge' is to question the legitimacy of something, usually openly and formally. The students found secret routes around the block rather than disputing its right to exist, so this verb is too public and too direct. -->
- [ ] D) decode
  <!-- feedback: 'To decode' is to convert an encoded message into a readable form. No encoding is involved in a web filter, and the sentence is about evading a restriction rather than interpreting a cipher. -->

### Explicacion Pedagogica
The C1+ distinction tested here is between circumventing a rule and attacking the rule itself. To circumvent is to get past something that stays in place, which fits a filter that was never removed; dismantling and challenging both imply confronting it directly, which the sentence explicitly rules out.

## Question 11 [D7-D8]

**Contexto:** Discussing digital citizenship in Barranquilla.

### Enunciado
Complete the following sentence about digital citizenship: 'Had the platform logged every content removal and made the record public, none of the disputes in the last two years __________ to have been settled on private messages alone.' (Topic: inverted third conditional with a negative main clause)

### Opciones
- [x] A) would
  <!-- feedback: An inverted third conditional uses 'had' plus the past participle in the first clause and 'would have' plus the participle in the second. Here the second clause is negative, so the sentence becomes 'none of the disputes would have had to be settled on private messages alone'. -->
- [ ] B) will
  <!-- feedback: 'Will' belongs to the present or future, and the whole sentence is set in a counterfactual past. Using it would put the second clause in a different time world from the 'Had' clause that opens the sentence. -->
- [ ] C) must
  <!-- feedback: 'Must' expresses obligation in the present. A third conditional describes what would not have been necessary, and that reasoning is carried by 'would have' plus the infinitive, not by a modal of duty. -->
- [ ] D) had
  <!-- feedback: 'Had' already opens the sentence as the auxiliary of the first clause, and the second clause of a third conditional takes 'would have' rather than repeating the past perfect auxiliary. Two 'had' clauses would leave the main clause unfinished. -->

### Explicacion Pedagogica
Third conditionals are the reason 'had' and 'would have' exist as separate auxiliaries, and at C1+ they are also frequently inverted. The rule to internalise is a pair: one clause has 'had' plus participle, the other has 'would have' plus participle, and a negative subject only changes the wording, not the tense.
