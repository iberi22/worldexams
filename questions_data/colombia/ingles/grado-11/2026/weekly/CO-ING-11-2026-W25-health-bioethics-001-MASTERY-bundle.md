### Enunciado
Complete the following sentence about health & bioethics: 'Not until the trial protocol was registered publicly __________ the ethics board approve the recruitment of healthy volunteers.' (Topic: 'not until' with an inverted clause)

### Opciones
- [ ] A) had
  <!-- feedback: 'Not until' inverts the main clause, placing the auxiliary before the subject 'the ethics board'. 'Had the ethics board approved' would leave the subject first, which the pattern forbids. -->
- [ ] B) was
  <!-- feedback: 'Was' would suggest a passive or a singular subject. The subject is singular but the verb is active, so a passive form is not available here. -->
- [x] C) did
  <!-- feedback: 'Not until the protocol was registered did the ethics board approve'. The auxiliary is fronted to invert the clause, which is the defining feature of the structure. -->
- [ ] D) do
  <!-- feedback: 'Do' is present tense, and the clause reports a completed sequence in the past simple, so a present auxiliary cannot invert it. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
Inversion with 'not until' frontes the auxiliary so that the subject follows the verb. The auxiliary 'did' carries the tense of the main clause while 'the ethics board' is delayed, giving 'did the ethics board approve'. Choosing a perfect or passive auxiliary describes a relation of time or voice that the sentence does not express.

### Enunciado
Complete the following sentence about health & bioethics: 'Rarely __________ a review board approve a protocol that no independent statistician has examined.' (Topic: inversion with 'rarely' and a singular subject)

### Opciones
- [ ] A) do
  <!-- feedback: 'Do' agrees with plural subjects, and 'a review board' is singular here, so the base form with 'does' is required. -->
- [ ] B) have
  <!-- feedback: 'Have' would give the perfect aspect, but the bare inversion with 'rarely' and a singular subject takes 'does' before the base form. -->
- [x] C) does
  <!-- feedback: 'Rarely does a review board approve'. With a singular subject the inversion uses 'does', and the main verb reverts to the base form 'approve'. -->
- [ ] D) are
  <!-- feedback: 'Are' cannot invert with a singular subject and would require a plural or a collection for agreement. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
Negative adverbials such as 'rarely', 'seldom' and 'never' trigger subject-auxiliary inversion. When the subject is third person singular, as 'a review board' is, the auxiliary is 'does' and the following verb loses its inflection: 'does approve'. Using 'do' is the frequent error because the plural pattern dominates in the language.
