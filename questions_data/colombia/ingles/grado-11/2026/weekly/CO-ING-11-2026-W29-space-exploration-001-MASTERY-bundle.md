**ID:** CO-ING-11-2026-W29-space-exploration-001-MASTERY-bundle-v20
**Bloom:** Evaluate
**ICFES:** Pragmatic Competence
**Expected_Success:** 0.25
**Contexto:** A conversation between two engineers at a space company in Bogotá after a failed test.

### Enunciado
Two engineers in Bogotá talk after a satellite antenna failed a test.

Lucía: 'The antenna failed again. I think we should look into the material we used for the joints.'
Andrés: 'Good idea. I'll ask the lab to analyse a sample this afternoon.'

What does Lucía suggest?

### Opciones
- [ ] A) Looking through the antenna like a telescope.
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. This takes 'look into' literally; the phrasal verb here means to investigate a problem. -->
- [ ] B) Replacing the antenna without checking what went wrong.
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. She wants to examine the material, which is checking the cause, not replacing the antenna directly. -->
- [x] C) Investigating the material used in the joints to find the cause.
  <!-- feedback: Correct. 'Look into' means to investigate or examine; Andrés understands this, because he offers to have a sample analysed. -->
- [ ] D) Stopping the project because the antenna failed twice.
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. She suggests a next step for solving the problem, so she does not want to stop the project. -->

### Explicacion Pedagogica
'Look into' is a phrasal verb meaning to investigate or examine something carefully. The reply from Andrés, who decides to analyse a sample, confirms the meaning through the reaction of the listener. Using the other speaker's response as evidence is a reliable strategy for interpreting phrasal verbs in dialogues.
