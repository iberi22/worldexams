### Opciones

  <!-- feedback: 'One' can stand for a whole preceding clause when the repeated material is identical and no new information is added. Since the second and third statements repeat the first verbatim, substitution is exactly what the sentence reports. -->

  <!-- feedback: Addition means putting new material after a first mention, which is the opposite of what repetition of the same figures does. The third statement adds nothing, so it is not an addition. -->

  <!-- feedback: Modification implies the later statements alter the earlier ones, for instance by correcting a number. Here nothing changes between the three mentions, so the relation is pure substitution. -->

### Enunciado
Complete the following sentence about education systems: 'The inspection covered forty schools; it __________ only twelve, all of them rural.' (Topic: ellipsis of a repeated subject)

### Opciones
- [x] A) covered
  <!-- feedback: After a semicolon the repeated subject is normally left out, so 'it covered only twelve' is understood and 'covered' alone supplies the verb. The elliptical subject points back to the one named before the semicolon. -->
- [ ] B) was covering
  <!-- feedback: 'Was covering' would add a progressive reading that the sentence does not need, since the inspection of the first group is already complete and the second group is simply smaller. Ellipsis removes the subject, not the tense choice. -->
- [ ] C) has covered
  <!-- feedback: 'Has covered' would reopen the first action in the present perfect and link it to the second, which the semicolon is there to separate. Two separate completed passes are reported, not one action extended to the present. -->
- [ ] D) covers
  <!-- feedback: 'Covers' in the present simple would contradict the past tense established by 'covered' in the first clause. The tense of the repeated verb must match the one it stands in for, since nothing in the sentence moves to the present. -->

### Explicacion Pedagogica
Ellipsis of the subject after a semicolon or conjunction is standard English when the second clause repeats the first: the reader recovers the subject, and only the new element is stated. What the ellipsis never removes is agreement with the original tense and number, so the verb in the second clause still has to be 'covered' in the past.
