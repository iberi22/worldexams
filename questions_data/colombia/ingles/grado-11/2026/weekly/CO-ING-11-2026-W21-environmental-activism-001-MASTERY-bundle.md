### Enunciado
At a meeting about a campaign against air pollution in Medellín, Laura says:

Laura: 'Banning cars from the whole city next month? Let's not bite off more than we can chew. We are only twelve volunteers.'

What does Laura mean?

### Opciones
- [x] A) The group should not try to do something too big for its size and resources.
  <!-- feedback: Correct. 'Bite off more than you can chew' means to take on a task that is too large; her reason, 'We are only twelve volunteers', confirms she thinks the plan is too ambitious. -->
- [ ] B) The volunteers should stop eating during the meetings.
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. This is a literal reading of 'bite' and 'chew'; the idiom refers to taking on too much work, not to food. -->

  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Laura is warning against the plan, not asking to speed it up; her mention of only twelve volunteers shows she wants less, not more. -->

  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. 'Let's not...' introduces an objection, so she is disagreeing with the proposal, not supporting it. -->

### Explicacion Pedagogica
'Bite off more than you can chew' warns that a plan is bigger than the people doing it can manage. In the dialogue, the rhetorical question about banning cars and the reason 'We are only twelve volunteers' together show a polite objection. Recognising that 'Let's not' introduces disagreement is the pragmatic key.

## Question 19 [D9-D10]
**ID:** CO-ING-11-2026-W21-environmental-activism-001-MASTERY-bundle-v19
**Bloom:** Evaluate
**ICFES:** Pragmatic Competence
**Expected_Success:** 0.28
**Contexto:** A conversation between two volunteers after a beach clean-up in Santa Marta.

### Enunciado
Two volunteers talk after a beach clean-up in Santa Marta.

Andrés: 'We picked up two hundred kilos of plastic today!'
Camila: 'That's great, but honestly it's just a drop in the ocean. Ships throw tonnes into the sea every day.'

What does Camila mean?

### Opciones
- [ ] A) The plastic they collected fell back into the sea.
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. This reads 'drop' and 'ocean' literally; nothing in the dialogue says the plastic returned to the water. -->
- [ ] B) Andrés is lying about how much plastic they collected.
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Camila begins with 'That's great', which accepts his number; her point is about scale, not about honesty. -->
- [ ] C) The volunteers should start working on ships instead of beaches.
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. She mentions ships to show the size of the problem, but she makes no suggestion about where they should work. -->
- [x] D) Their work is positive but very small compared with the size of the problem.
  <!-- feedback: Correct. 'A drop in the ocean' means a very small amount compared with what is needed; she contrasts two hundred kilos with the tonnes thrown in every day. -->

### Explicacion Pedagogica
'A drop in the ocean' is an idiom of proportion: something good but tiny compared with the whole. Camila's structure 'That's great, but...' first values her friend's effort and then limits it, which is a common pragmatic pattern for disagreeing politely in English.

## Question 20 [D9-D10]
**ID:** CO-ING-11-2026-W21-environmental-activism-001-MASTERY-bundle-v20
**Bloom:** Evaluate

**Contexto:** Discussing environmental activism in Cali.

### Enunciado
In a high-level debate about environmental activism, Valentina says: 'Every tribunal case we bring names the same kind of harm and never a single emitter, and I put that to you as a rule of thumb rather than a demonstrated finding.' What is she doing?

### Opciones
- [ ] A) She is retracting a claim she had already proved.
  <!-- feedback: Nothing withdraws a proven claim; a retraction would say it proved false. She is qualifying a claim she never presented as established, so nothing is taken back. -->
- [ ] B) She is insisting the emitters should be named immediately.
  <!-- feedback: The final clause describes what she is doing with her own statement, not a demand aimed at anyone else. There is no imperative, no question and no instruction in the quotation. -->
- [ ] C) She is claiming that tribunals never accept naming a category rather than an emitter.
  <!-- feedback: The sentence describes what her own cases do, not what tribunals accept. Nothing in it reports any tribunal's practice, so the generalisation about tribunals has no support. -->
- [x] D) She is identifying a recurring weakness in the campaign's own cases while marking it as an observation rather than proven evidence.
  <!-- feedback: The final clause separates a rule she has noticed from a finding she can demonstrate. She asserts the pattern about her own strategy and limits its authority at the same time, which is what the two phrases together do. -->

### Explicacion Pedagogica
'As a rule of thumb rather than a demonstrated finding' is a standard C1+ hedge marking the epistemic status of a claim. This item asks the student to read that status rather than the content, and each distractor answers a question the sentence does not put.
