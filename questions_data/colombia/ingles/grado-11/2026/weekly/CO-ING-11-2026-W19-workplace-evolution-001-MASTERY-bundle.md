**Bloom:** Analyze
**ICFES:** Structural Cloze
**Expected_Success:** 0.49
**Contexto:** Discussing workplace evolution in Cartagena.

### Enunciado
Complete the following sentence about workplace evolution: 'Not until the redundancy package was announced __________ the unions agree to reopen talks.' (Topic: 'not until' with an inverted clause)

### Opciones
- [ ] A) had
  <!-- feedback: 'Not until' takes an inverted auxiliary before the subject. 'Had the unions agreed' would place the subject first, which is the pattern 'not until' forbids. -->
- [x] B) did
  <!-- feedback: 'Not until the package was announced did the unions agree'. The auxiliary is fronted to invert the clause, which is the defining feature of the structure. -->
- [ ] C) were
  <!-- feedback: 'Were' would form a subjunctive or a passive. Here the main clause is active and the subject is 'the unions', so a plural form is not used. -->
- [ ] D) do
  <!-- feedback: 'Do' is present tense and cannot invert a past sequence. The event described, the announcement, is already in the past simple. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
Inversion with 'not until' frontes the auxiliary so that the subject follows the verb: 'not until X did Y happen'. The auxiliary 'did' carries the tense of the main clause while the subject 'the unions' is delayed. Recognising that the subject must come after the auxiliary is what separates the correct form from the alternatives.

**Bloom:** Analyze

**Expected_Success:** 0.46
**Contexto:** Discussing workplace evolution in Cali.

### Enunciado
Complete the following sentence about workplace evolution: 'Rarely __________ a company that piloted a four-day week publish what it actually learned.' (Topic: inversion with 'rarely' and a singular subject)

### Opciones
- [ ] A) do
  <!-- feedback: 'Do' would be correct only if the subject were plural. 'A company' is singular, so the base form 'does' is required. -->
- [ ] B) has
  <!-- feedback: 'Has' gives the present perfect, but the bare inversion with 'rarely' and a singular subject takes 'does' before the base form. -->
- [x] C) does
  <!-- feedback: 'Rarely does a company publish'. With a singular subject the inversion uses 'does', and the main verb reverts to the base form 'publish'. -->
- [ ] D) are
  <!-- feedback: 'Are' cannot invert with a singular subject, and it would also require a plural or a collection for agreement. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
Negative adverbials such as 'rarely', 'seldom' and 'never' trigger subject-auxiliary inversion. When the subject is third person singular, as 'a company' is, the auxiliary is 'does' and the following verb loses its inflection: 'does publish'. Using 'do' is the most common error because the plural pattern is far more frequent.

### Enunciado
Complete the following sentence about workplace evolution: 'Only after the tribunal ruled on the case __________ the dismissed workers resume their claim.' (Topic: 'only when' with an inverted past clause)

### Opciones
- [ ] A) had
  <!-- feedback: 'Only after' frontes a subordinate clause and then inverts the main clause, so the auxiliary comes before the subject 'the dismissed workers', which makes 'had' impossible here. -->
- [ ] B) was
  <!-- feedback: 'Was' would require a passive or a singular subject. The subject is plural and the verb is active, so neither reading applies. -->
- [ ] C) could
  <!-- feedback: 'Could' is a modal, but the structure needed here is a simple inversion of the tense auxiliary that the sequence calls for. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
'Only after' and 'only when' invert the main clause that follows the subordinating expression. The auxiliary is placed before the subject and the main verb returns to its base form, so the sequence is 'did the dismissed workers resume'. Choosing an auxiliary that agrees with the subject without inversion is the trap in this item.
