### Enunciado
Complete the following sentence about historical turning points: 'Until the archives were opened in 1998, the 1919 __________ been documented in a single continuous narrative, and the two families involved gave entirely different accounts of the same afternoon.' (Topic: 'until' with a perfect in the historical present context)

### Opciones
- [x] D) had
  <!-- feedback: 'Until' marks a point before which something had not yet happened, so the perfect is needed: 'the 1919 had not been documented'. The negation of 'had been' is the standard way to describe a gap in the record that persisted until an archive opened. -->
- [ ] A) was
  <!-- feedback: 'Was' would give a simple past passive and assert that the 1919 was documented, which is the opposite of the sentence. 'Until the archives were opened' sets up an absence, and only a negated perfect can express it. -->
- [ ] B) has
  <!-- feedback: 'Has' would connect the undocumented year to the present, but the period at stake ended in 1998. What was missing up to that date is a past state of affairs, and the past perfect is what the timeframe requires. -->

### Explicacion Pedagogica

### Opciones
- [x] C) had
  <!-- feedback: 'No sooner' inverts the clause and takes the past perfect: 'no sooner had the assembly dissolved than...'. The 'than' clause then carries the ordinary word order and the past simple, so the two halves are deliberately mismatched in form. -->

### Opciones
- [x] A) has
  <!-- feedback: 'Not since' points forward to the present moment and denies any occurrence since the 1930s, so the present perfect is used: 'not since the 1930s has a single municipal archive managed'. The sentence is about the current state of the record. -->
- [ ] B) had
  <!-- feedback: 'Had' would place the failure in the past and close the period at some point before now. 'Not since' explicitly runs from the 1930s up to the present, so the perfect that reaches into now is the present one. -->

  <!-- feedback: 'Was' would give a simple past passive and would also fail to invert with the verb 'managed'. The clause needs a finite auxiliary in front of the subject, and only 'has' supplies one that reaches to the present. -->

  <!-- feedback: 'Only after' fronted inverts the main clause, and 'did' is the auxiliary that carries the past tense: 'only after the printing press arrived did the local chronicles become worth consulting'. The main verb returns to its base form 'become'. -->

  <!-- feedback: 'Had' would form a past perfect and need the participle 'become', but the sentence describes a single completed change in value at one point in the past, not a change completed before another past event. -->

  <!-- feedback: 'Was' would form a passive or a copula, and the chronicles are not being acted upon by anyone. An inverted clause with 'only' needs an auxiliary that inverts, which is 'did' in this past active sentence. -->
