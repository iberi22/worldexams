### Enunciado
In the context of media ethics, which word best completes the following sentence: 'A broadcaster that airs unverified footage and never corrects the record tends to __________ its own credibility with audiences who eventually notice'?

### Opciones
- [x] A) erode
  <!-- feedback: 'Erode' means to wear away gradually, which matches both the mechanism and the time scale: the loss happens little by little and only becomes visible once audiences notice. The word describes a slow decay, not a sudden collapse. -->
- [ ] B) bolster
  <!-- feedback: 'Bolster' means to strengthen or support, which is the opposite of what audiences experience. Nothing in the sentence suggests the airing reinforced trust; the clause about noticing points the other way. -->
- [ ] C) consolidate
  <!-- feedback: 'Consolidate' means to combine into something stronger, and the sentence describes an absence of correction that weakens a position. A word for reinforcement cannot fit a sentence about losing standing. -->
- [ ] D) circumvent
  <!-- feedback: 'Circumvent' means to go around a rule or obstacle, and the sentence describes broadcasting without correction, which is evading a standard rather than evading an obstacle. It says nothing about going around anything. -->

### Explicacion Pedagogica
C1+ vocabulary items reward reading the time scale of a process as carefully as its direction. Here 'gradually' and 'eventually notice' describe slow attrition, which is what separates 'erode' from the words that imply either strengthening or a single decisive event.

  <!-- feedback: 'Bury' is precisely what the sentence tells editors not to do, and the phrase 'not merely bury it' removes it as a possible answer. Choosing it would contradict the instruction the sentence gives. -->

  <!-- feedback: 'Retract' names the act of withdrawing the claim, and the sentence is about where a correction is placed rather than whether one is issued. The verb for withdrawing cannot occupy a slot describing presentation. -->

**Expected_Success:** 0.49
**Contexto:** Discussing media ethics in Cartagena.

### Enunciado
Compare 'The retraction appeared on Tuesday' with 'It was on Tuesday that the retraction appeared.' What does the second sentence add?

### Opciones
- [x] B) It places the day, rather than the action, in focus.
  <!-- feedback: The cleft moves 'on Tuesday' into the position after 'It was', which is exactly what the focus slot is for. The retraction and its date are unchanged; only which element is treated as given information differs. -->
- [ ] A) It establishes that the retraction was published by mistake.
  <!-- feedback: The cleft reorganises information structure and says nothing about intention or accident. Choosing it would read a grammatical device as evidence about the circumstances of publication. -->
- [ ] C) It changes the tense of the sentence from past to present.
  <!-- feedback: Both sentences keep 'appeared' in the past simple. The cleft never alters tense, and the comparison is about focus rather than about when the verb is situated. -->
- [ ] D) It proves that readers accepted the retraction.
  <!-- feedback: No reaction from any reader is mentioned or implied. A word order cannot supply evidence about an audience's response, and the sentence contains no such claim. -->

### Explicacion Pedagogica
A cleft keeps the propositional content intact and moves one element into focus, so C1+ students should test the options against that definition. Anything claiming the cleft adds an event, a tense or an audience reaction fails immediately.
