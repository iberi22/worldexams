**Expected_Success:** 0.49
**Contexto:** Discussing globalization in Barranquilla.

### Enunciado
Complete the following sentence about globalization: 'Not until the tariff was removed __________ local manufacturers start competing with imports on price.' (Topic: 'not until' with an inverted clause)

### Opciones
- [x] B) did
  <!-- feedback: 'Not until' is a fronted negative that inverts the main clause, and 'did' carries the simple past: 'not until the tariff was removed did local manufacturers start'. The main verb returns to its base form 'start'. -->
- [ ] A) had
  <!-- feedback: 'Had' would form a past perfect and need the participle 'started', placing the competing before another past point. The sentence describes the moment competing began, which is a single past event. -->
- [ ] C) could
  <!-- feedback: 'Could' would add a sense of ability or possibility rather than fixing the time of the event. The clause names the condition under which competing started, which is a temporal trigger and not a capability. -->
- [ ] D) were
  <!-- feedback: 'Were' would form a passive or a copula, and the manufacturers are the ones doing the competing. The inversion needs a finite auxiliary after the subject, and only 'did' provides one. -->

### Explicacion Pedagogica
'Not until' and 'not since' invert at C1+ and the auxiliary is decided by the main clause alone. The reliable order of work is to find the main clause, read off its tense and number, and only then let the fronted expression justify the inversion.

- [x] A) She is asserting a general pattern and marking it as an observation rather than proven evidence.
  <!-- feedback: The final clause explicitly separates what she has noticed from what she can demonstrate. Naming a common pattern while refusing to present it as proof is precisely what the two phrases do together, and that is what the item asks. -->
- [ ] B) She is withdrawing a claim she had already proved.
  <!-- feedback: Nothing in the sentence withdraws a proven claim; a retraction would say the opposite, that the claim turned out to be false. Here she is qualifying a claim she never presented as proved. -->
- [ ] C) She is demanding that the other speaker provide data.
  <!-- feedback: The final clause describes what she is doing with her own statement, not a request aimed at someone else. There is no imperative, no question, and no reference to anyone else's evidence. -->
- [ ] D) She is insisting the plant closures were beneficial.
  <!-- feedback: The sentence describes a loss and offers no defence of the closures. Reading it as praise reverses the evaluative direction of the statement, which is plainly negative. -->

### Explicacion Pedagogica
C1+ pragmatics includes reading how a speaker signals the strength of an assertion, and 'as a rule of thumb rather than a demonstrated finding' is a textbook hedge. The item tests whether the student tracks whose claim is being qualified and what kind of claim it is.
