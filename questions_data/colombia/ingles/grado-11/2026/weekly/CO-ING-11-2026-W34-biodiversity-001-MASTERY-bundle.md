### Enunciado
Complete the following sentence about biodiversity: 'Not until the pesticide ban took effect __________ the pollinator counts in the valley begin to recover.' (Topic: 'not until' with an inverted clause)

### Opciones
- [ ] A) had
  <!-- feedback: 'Not until' inverts the main clause, placing the auxiliary before the subject 'the pollinator counts'. 'Had the pollinator counts begun' would leave the subject first, which the pattern forbids. -->
- [x] B) did
  <!-- feedback: 'Not until the ban took effect did the counts begin to recover'. The auxiliary is fronted to invert the clause, which is the defining feature of the structure. -->
- [ ] C) was
  <!-- feedback: 'Was' would suggest a passive or a singular subject. The subject is plural but the verb is active, so a passive reading is not available. -->
- [ ] D) do
  <!-- feedback: 'Do' is present tense and cannot invert a past sequence in which the ban had already come into force. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
Inversion with 'not until' frontes the auxiliary so that the subject follows the verb: 'not until X did Y happen'. The auxiliary 'did' carries the tense of the main clause while the subject is delayed. Selecting a perfect or passive auxiliary would state a relation of time or voice that the sentence does not express.

### Enunciado
Complete the following sentence about biodiversity: 'Rarely __________ a restored marsh recover its full community of fish within a decade.' (Topic: inversion with 'rarely' and a singular subject)

### Opciones
- [ ] A) do
  <!-- feedback: 'Do' agrees with plural subjects, and 'a restored marsh' is singular, so the base form with 'does' is required. -->
- [x] B) does
  <!-- feedback: 'Rarely does a restored marsh recover'. With a singular subject the inversion uses 'does', and the main verb reverts to the base form 'recover'. -->
- [ ] C) have
  <!-- feedback: 'Have' would give the perfect aspect, but the bare inversion with 'rarely' and a singular subject takes 'does' before the base form. -->
- [ ] D) are
  <!-- feedback: 'Are' cannot invert with a singular subject and would require a plural or a collection for agreement. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica

Using 'do' is the frequent error because the plural pattern dominates in the language.
