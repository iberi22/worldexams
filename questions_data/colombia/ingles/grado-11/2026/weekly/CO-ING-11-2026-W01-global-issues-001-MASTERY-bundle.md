### Enunciado
In the context of global issues, which word best completes the following sentence: 'Aid agencies discovered that delivering food to a crisis zone by road could __________ the shortage they were trying to fix, because the trucks consumed fuel the region could no longer import'?

### Opciones
- [x] D) exacerbate
  <!-- feedback: 'Exacerbate' means to make a problem worse, and the sentence describes a well-meant intervention that deepened the very shortage it addressed. The clause about fuel and lost imports supplies the mechanism, which is the reasoning the item asks for. -->
- [ ] A) alleviate
  <!-- feedback: 'Alleviate' means to make something less severe, and nothing in the sentence indicates the shortage eased. The final clause describes consumption of scarce fuel, which works against relief. -->
- [ ] B) mitigate
  <!-- feedback: 'Mitigate' means to soften a problem without removing it, and the sentence presents no softening at all. It states the opposite, that the shortage grew worse because of the delivery itself. -->
- [ ] C) facilitate
  <!-- feedback: 'Facilitate' means to make an action easier, which would describe a delivery working well. Here the delivery drained fuel the region had lost the means to obtain, so no facilitation is described. -->

### Explicacion Pedagogica
C1+ lexical questions often require following a causal chain to its end rather than reacting to the first clause. Here the writer presents a good intention, then explains why the outcome was harmful, and the word in the blank has to match the outcome rather than the intention.

### Enunciado
In the context of global issues, which word best completes the following sentence: 'Climate negotiations stalled for so long that the countries most exposed to rising seas began to fear the talks had been __________ entirely'?

### Opciones
- [x] D) sidelined
  <!-- feedback: 'Sidelined' means moved to a position of lesser importance, which is what countries fear when negotiations are delayed: that their concerns have been taken off the table. The phrase 'began to fear' is what makes this a fear about status rather than a stated fact. -->
- [ ] A) ratified
  <!-- feedback: 'Ratified' means formally approved, and stalled talks have not reached approval. A country afraid of being sidelined is worried about exclusion from the process, not about a document being signed too late. -->
- [ ] B) accelerated
  <!-- feedback: 'Accelerated' means made faster, which contradicts the opening clause about the talks stalling. The sentence describes delay as the problem, so a word for increased speed cannot fit the gap. -->
- [ ] C) delegated
  <!-- feedback: 'Delegated' means handed over to others, and the countries in question are the ones who would be losing influence rather than handing their role away. Being removed from consideration is not the same as passing a responsibility along. -->

### Explicacion Pedagogica
This item turns on 'began to fear', which signals that the exclusion has not been stated as fact. At C1+ students need to notice whether a sentence asserts something or attributes a belief to someone, because the correct vocabulary depends on that distinction.

### Enunciado
Complete the following sentence about global issues: 'Not until the ceasefire was signed __________ the agencies resume the vaccination rounds they had suspended a year earlier.' (Topic: 'not until' with an inverted clause)

### Opciones
- [x] C) did
  <!-- feedback: 'Not until' is a fronted negative that inverts the main clause, and 'did' carries the simple past: 'not until the ceasefire was signed did the agencies resume'. The main verb returns to its base form 'resume'. -->
- [ ] A) had
  <!-- feedback: 'Had' would form a past perfect and need the participle 'resumed', placing the vaccination rounds before another past point. The sentence reports the moment work restarted, which is a single past event. -->
- [ ] B) were
  <!-- feedback: 'Were' would form a passive or a copula, and the agencies are the ones carrying out the rounds. The inverted clause needs a finite auxiliary, and only 'did' supplies one here. -->
- [ ] D) could
  <!-- feedback: 'Could' would add a sense of ability rather than marking the time the work began. The trigger is the signing of the ceasefire, and a modal for capability does not express that moment. -->

### Explicacion Pedagogica
At C1+ the fronted negative justifies the inversion but never dictates the tense. Reading the main clause first, then confirming the inversion from 'not until', is the sequence that keeps the two decisions separate and reliable.
