## Part 6: Reading Comprehension

### Text: The Perils of Predictive Policing
Predictive policing uses data on past crimes to forecast where future crimes are likely to occur. While proponents claim this allows for more efficient resource allocation, critics argue that it creates a self-fulfilling prophecy. If an algorithm is trained on data from over-policed neighborhoods, it will naturally predict that more crime will happen there. This leads to more police being sent to those areas, resulting in more arrests, which then feeds back into the algorithm as even more "data" supporting the original prediction. This feedback loop can lead to a state of "technological redlining," where certain communities are unfairly targeted by law enforcement based on biased historical patterns rather than individual behavior.
