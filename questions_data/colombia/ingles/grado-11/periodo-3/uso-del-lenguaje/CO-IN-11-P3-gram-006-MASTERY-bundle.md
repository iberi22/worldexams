## Part 6: Reading Comprehension

### Text: Digital Ontology and the Information Universe
Information theory suggests that at its most fundamental level, the universe may not be made of matter or energy, but of information. John Wheeler's "it from bit" philosophy posits that every physical quantity—every "it"—derives its ultimate significance from bits of information (binary choices). This perspective provides a theoretical backbone for the simulation hypothesis: if the universe is essentially information, then it is, in principle, computable. However, this raises profound ontological questions. If we are indeed living in a simulation, what does that mean for our concepts of "truth," "nature," and "being"? Some argue that the simulation is just as "real" as any other level of reality, as reality is defined by the internal consistency of its laws rather than its physical substrate. Others contend that a simulated existence is a "lesser" form of being, a digital shadow of a more fundamental "base reality."
