## Part 6: Reading Comprehension

### Text: The GDP Illusion
The reliance on GDP as the sole indicator of national health has been criticized for creating an "illusion" of progress. GDP measures the total value of goods and services produced, but it fails to account for the "negative externalities" of that production, such as pollution or the exhaustion of natural resources. For instance, a major oil spill actually increases GDP because of the money spent on the cleanup, even though it represents an environmental disaster. Furthermore, GDP ignores non-market activities like volunteer work or childcare, and it says nothing about the distribution of wealth. In response, some economists have proposed the "Genuine Progress Indicator" (GPI), which subtracts environmental costs and social harms from economic gains to provide a more holistic view of a society's well-being.
