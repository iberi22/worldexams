## Part 6: Reading Comprehension

### Text: The Ghost in Our Genes
One of the most remarkable findings in epigenetics is the concept of "transgenerational epigenetic inheritance." This suggests that the experiences of our ancestors—such as a period of famine or trauma—can leave molecular marks on their DNA that affect the health of their grandchildren. A famous study of the "Dutch Hunger Winter" of 1944 showed that children whose mothers were pregnant during the famine had higher rates of obesity and heart disease later in life. These health effects were linked to specific epigenetic changes in genes involved in metabolism. This finding challenges the "Weismann Barrier"—the traditional belief that acquired characteristics cannot be inherited. It suggests that our biological identity is not just a fixed blueprint we receive at birth, but a dynamic record of our environmental history.
