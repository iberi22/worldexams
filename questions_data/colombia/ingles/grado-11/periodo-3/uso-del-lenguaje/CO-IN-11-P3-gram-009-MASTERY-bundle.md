## Part 6: Reading Comprehension

### Text: Repression and the Return of the Repressed
A central tenet of psychoanalysis is "repression"—the unconscious mechanism by which the mind pushes distressing thoughts or memories out of conscious awareness. However, Freud argued that repressed material does not simply disappear; instead, it remains active in the unconscious and often seeks "return" through dreams, slips of the tongue (Freudian slips), or neurotic symptoms. This "return of the repressed" suggests that our mental health depends on the ability to bring these hidden conflicts into conscious awareness, a process Freud termed "making the unconscious conscious." Jacques Lacan later expanded on this, suggesting that the unconscious is "structured like a language," where repressed desires manifest as symbols and metaphors that must be deciphered through dialogue.
