## Part 6: Reading Comprehension

### Text: Color Perception and Linguistic Relativity
One of the most widely studied areas of linguistic relativity is color perception. Different languages divide the color spectrum in different ways. For example, some languages have only two basic color terms (light and dark), while others have dozens. A famous study of the Himba people in Namibia, whose language distinguishes between shades of green that look identical to English speakers but does not have separate words for "blue" and "green," showed that they were much faster at identifying the differing green shades than English speakers were. This suggests that the categories provided by our language can "tune" our perception, making us more sensitive to certain sensory inputs while blurring others. Language, then, is not just a tool for describing the world; it is a framework that helps organize our experience of it.
