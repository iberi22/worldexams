## Part 6: Reading Comprehension

### Text: The Ethics of Re-wilding
"Re-wilding" is the process of restoring natural processes and wilderness areas, often by reintroducing native species or allowing nature to take its course with minimal human intervention. When applied to cities, re-wilding challenges our traditional aesthetic of the "manicured" park—the perfectly mown lawn and the neatly trimmed hedge. Proponents argue that re-wilding increases "ecosystem services," such as flood mitigation and urban cooling, while providing vital habitats for pollinators. However, re-wilding also raises social and ethical concerns. For some, a "wild" space is seen as "unkempt" or even dangerous, potentially leading to increased crime or the presence of "pests." Furthermore, re-wilding projects must be managed carefully to ensure they do not become "green walls" that contribute to gentrification and exclude lower-income residents.
