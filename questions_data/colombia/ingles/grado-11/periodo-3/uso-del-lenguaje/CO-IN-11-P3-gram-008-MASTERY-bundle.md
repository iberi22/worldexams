## Part 6: Reading Comprehension

### Text: The Absurd and the Myth of Sisyphus
Albert Camus explored the concept of "the Absurd"—the conflict between the human desire to find inherent meaning in life and the "silent," meaningless universe. He famously used the Greek myth of Sisyphus, who is condemned to eternally roll a rock up a hill only for it to roll back down, as a metaphor for the human condition. For Camus, the realization of the absurd does not necessarily lead to nihilism or suicide. Instead, he argues that one must "imagine Sisyphus happy." By acknowledging the futility of his task and continuing anyway, Sisyphus achieves a form of revolt. He becomes the master of his own fate, creating his own meaning in a world that offers none. This "absurdist" stance requires a courageous acceptance of reality without resorting to the "philosophical suicide" of religious or metaphysical illusions.
