## Part 6: Reading Comprehension

### Text: The Trace and the Metaphysics of Presence
Derrida's critique of "logocentrism" centers on the idea that Western thought has always prioritized speech over writing, assuming that speech is a more direct and "authentic" expression of thought (the metaphysics of presence). He argued that writing, however, reveals the fundamental nature of all language: it is a system of "traces." A trace is the mark of a past presence that is no longer there, and a sign of a future meaning that has not yet arrived. Every word depends for its meaning on its relationship to other words that are *absent* from the current sentence. This means that meaning is never self-contained; it is always "contaminated" by the history and the future of the language itself. Deconstruction is the process of revealing these hidden traces and showing how they undermine the supposed stability of a text's "literal" meaning.
