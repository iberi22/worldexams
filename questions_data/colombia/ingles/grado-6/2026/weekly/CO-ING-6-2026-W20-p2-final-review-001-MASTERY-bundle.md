# Weekly Pack W20 — Period 2 Final Review

**Grade:** 6° | **Periodo:** 2 | **Semana:** W20 | **Year:** 2026 | **CEFR:** A2

## Question 1 [D3]

**ID:** CO-ING-6-2026-W20-p2-final-review-001-v1
**Bloom:** Remember
**ICFES:** Lexical Knowledge
**Expected_Success:** 0.85
**Contexto:** Naming the family relation between two people.

### Enunciado
My mother's sister is my ________.

### Opciones
- [ ] A) grandmother <!-- feedback: A grandmother is one generation above your parents, and a sister of your mother is your own generation. -->
- [ ] B) niece <!-- feedback: A niece is the daughter of your brother or sister, so she belongs to a generation below you. -->
- [x] C) aunt <!-- feedback: The sister of one of your parents is your aunt, and a mother is one of your parents. -->
- [ ] D) sister <!-- feedback: A sister shares your own parents, whereas the person described is the sibling of one of your parents. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
The correct answer is "aunt". Family vocabulary is a closed system of relationships, and naming the relation requires tracking generations as much as gender. This is the vocabulary the second period opens with and the one the descriptive weeks build on, since a student who can say how people are related can then describe how they look and how they behave.

## Question 2 [D3]

**ID:** CO-ING-6-2026-W20-p2-final-review-002-v1
**Bloom:** Remember
**ICFES:** Grammatical Knowledge
**Expected_Success:** 0.84
**Contexto:** Choosing the correct possessive for each member of a family.

### Enunciado
Complete the sentence: "That is my brother and this is ________ wife."

### Opciones
- [x] A) his <!-- feedback: His is the possessive for a male owner, and the wife named here belongs to the brother just mentioned. -->
- [ ] B) her <!-- feedback: Her would point to a female owner other than the sister, but the sentence names the brother's wife. -->
- [ ] C) he <!-- feedback: He is the subject pronoun, and a possessive is needed to show that the wife belongs to him. -->
- [ ] D) she <!-- feedback: She is the subject pronoun, and it cannot be used in front of a noun to show ownership. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
The correct answer is "his". Referring back to a person already mentioned and marking the relationship with a possessive is the structure the family vocabulary is normally taught inside, so the two weeks reinforce each other. Tracking the gender and the number of the antecedent is a real comprehension task as well as a grammar one, and both have to succeed for the sentence to work.

## Question 3 [D3-D4]

**ID:** CO-ING-6-2026-W20-p2-final-review-003-v1
**Bloom:** Understand
**ICFES:** Reading Comprehension
**Expected_Success:** 0.83
**Contexto:** Working out a relationship from a short family text.

### Enunciado
Read: "Camila is my sister. Her son is my nephew." How old is Camila's son?

### Opciones
- [ ] A) He is an adult. <!-- feedback: A nephew belongs to the generation below the speaker, and the text gives no reason to treat him as an adult. -->
- [x] B) He is a child. <!-- feedback: A son of a sister is one generation below, so the text places him in childhood rather than adulthood. -->
- [ ] C) He is Camila's brother. <!-- feedback: Her son is her child, and her brother would be Camila's own sibling rather than her son. -->
- [ ] D) The text does not say. <!-- feedback: The relationship nephew is given explicitly, and that relation tells the reader the generation he belongs to. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
The correct answer is "He is a child". The text never states an age, so the answer has to be inferred from the kinship term itself, and that inference is the actual reading task. Students who know what nephew means can answer without any other information, and those who have only memorised the word as a label cannot, which is exactly the distinction worth testing.

## Question 4 [D3-D4]

**ID:** CO-ING-6-2026-W20-p2-final-review-004-v1
**Bloom:** Apply
**ICFES:** Grammatical Knowledge
**Expected_Success:** 0.82
**Contexto:** Using the verb to be to describe a family member.

### Enunciado
Which sentence describes a grandmother correctly?

### Opciones
- [ ] A) My grandmother are my father's fathers. <!-- feedback: This has two faults at once, the plural verb and the plural noun after a singular possessive word. -->
- [ ] B) My grandmother are my father's mother. <!-- feedback: The subject is singular, so it takes is rather than the plural are. -->
- [ ] C) My grandmother is my father's father. <!-- feedback: The father of one's father is one's grandfather, so the gender of the parent has been changed here. -->
- [x] D) My grandmother is my father's mother. <!-- feedback: The mother of one's father is one's grandmother, and the verb to be agrees with the singular subject. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
The correct answer is "My grandmother is my father's mother". The relationship has to be worked out and the sentence has to be built correctly at the same time, and each distractor fails on one of those two fronts. Combining vocabulary and agreement in a single item is what the final review is for, since it is the kind of combined demand the period exam will set.

## Question 5 [D4]

**ID:** CO-ING-6-2026-W20-p2-final-review-005-v1
**Bloom:** Apply
**ICFES:** Grammatical Knowledge
**Expected_Success:** 0.81
**Contexto:** Placing a preposition to say where a person is in relation to a place.

### Enunciado
Where is Andrés? Choose the correct sentence.

### Opciones
- [ ] A) Andrés is in school. <!-- feedback: In school describes being inside the building, which is a stronger claim than the sentence intends. -->
- [x] B) Andrés is at school. <!-- feedback: At is the preposition used for a specific place or point in English, and school is such a point. -->
- [ ] C) Andrés is on school. <!-- feedback: On would place him on top of the school rather than at it, and it is not the preposition for a place. -->
- [ ] D) Andrés is to school. <!-- feedback: To is the preposition of direction towards a place, not of being located at one. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
The correct answer is "Andrés is at school". In, at and on divide places into containers, points and surfaces, and Spanish uses a single preposition where English needs three. The three distractors are the three prepositions a student reaches for in this position, so choosing correctly means knowing the rule rather than guessing the commonest one.

## Question 6 [D4]

**ID:** CO-ING-6-2026-W20-p2-final-review-006-v1
**Bloom:** Apply
**ICFES:** Lexical Knowledge
**Expected_Success:** 0.80
**Contexto:** Choosing the correct word to describe a person in a short text.

### Enunciado
A person who is very short of money is called ________.

### Opciones
- [x] A) poor <!-- feedback: Poor is the ordinary English word for someone who does not have much money. -->
- [ ] B) rich <!-- feedback: Rich is the opposite of poor, and it names someone who has a lot of money rather than very little. -->
- [ ] C) tall <!-- feedback: Tall describes height rather than wealth, so it has nothing to do with how much money somebody has. -->
- [ ] D) strong <!-- feedback: Strong describes physical power or stamina, and it does not describe a person's finances. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
The correct answer is "poor". Describing a person in English requires the right category of word, and money words are a category students meet in reading texts long before they feel confident using them. The distractors are drawn from the appearance vocabulary of the previous weeks, which is exactly the confusion that appears when a student reaches for the most familiar adjective instead of the accurate one.

## Question 7 [D4]

**ID:** CO-ING-6-2026-W20-p2-final-review-007-v1
**Bloom:** Apply
**ICFES:** Pragmatic Competence
**Expected_Success:** 0.79
**Contexto:** Introducing a relative to a teacher at school.

### Enunciado
You want to say to a teacher: "This is my grandmother." Which sentence do you use?

### Opciones
- [ ] A) This is a grandmother. <!-- feedback: The indefinite article would suggest one grandmother among others rather than the particular person being introduced. -->
- [ ] B) This is the grandmother of me. <!-- feedback: The phrase of me is not how English marks a relation to the speaker, so this sounds wrong throughout. -->
- [x] C) This is my grandmother. <!-- feedback: This is the standard introduction, with the possessive marking the relation and the word naming the person. -->
- [ ] D) She is my grandmother's she. <!-- feedback: The pronoun and the possessive are in the wrong order, and the extra she has nothing to refer to. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
The correct answer is "This is my grandmother". Introducing a family member is a fixed routine in English and the possessive does the work of naming the relationship. The distractor about the indefinite article is worth noticing, because a student who has only practised with a may produce a form that loses the particular person the introduction depends on.

## Question 8 [D4]

**ID:** CO-ING-6-2026-W20-p2-final-review-008-v1
**Bloom:** Analyze
**ICFES:** Reading Comprehension
**Expected_Success:** 0.77
**Contexto:** Comparing what two short family texts say.

### Enunciado
Read: "A: My brother is 16. B: My sister is 13." Which statement about the two is true?

### Opciones
- [x] A) The brother is 3 years older than the sister. <!-- feedback: 16 minus 13 is 3, so the brother is three years older than the sister. -->
- [ ] B) The sister is older than the brother. <!-- feedback: The brother is 16 and the sister is 13, so the brother is the older of the two. -->
- [ ] C) The brother and the sister are the same age. <!-- feedback: The two ages given are 16 and 13, which are three years apart rather than equal. -->
- [ ] D) The text does not give enough information to compare them. <!-- feedback: Both ages are stated explicitly, so the comparison can be made directly from the numbers given. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
The correct answer is "The brother is 3 years older than the sister". A family text almost always carries ages, and comparing two of them is the first step towards the comparisons the reading section of the exam demands. Two of the distractors contradict the numbers and one claims the text is insufficient when it is not, so the student has to actually use the data rather than judge by its appearance.

## Question 9 [D4]

**ID:** CO-ING-6-2026-W20-p2-final-review-009-v1
**Bloom:** Analyze
**ICFES:** Grammatical Knowledge
**Expected_Success:** 0.74
**Contexto:** Repairing a sentence in which the relationship is expressed wrongly.

### Enunciado
A student writes: "My uncle is my mother's brother." The teacher says there is one error. What is it?

### Opciones
- [ ] A) The verb is missing. <!-- feedback: There is no verb in this sentence in the original either, and the relationship noun phrase stands correctly without one. -->
- [ ] B) Uncle should be aunt. <!-- feedback: Uncle is the correct word, since the person described is a brother rather than a sister of the mother. -->
- [ ] C) The possessive should be a plural. <!-- feedback: My refers to a single mother, and the singular possessive is the correct form here. -->
- [x] D) There is no error in the sentence. <!-- feedback: The brother of one's mother is one's uncle, so the relationship named on both sides of the sentence is accurate. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
The correct answer is "There is no error in the sentence". Accepting a correct sentence is a real skill, and this period's topics are exactly where students over-correct and start inventing faults. Each distractor names a plausible-sounding defect that is simply not present, so the student has to verify rather than assume, which is the evaluative habit the hardest items in an exam require.

## Question 10 [D4]

**ID:** CO-ING-6-2026-W20-p2-final-review-010-v1
**Bloom:** Evaluate
**ICFES:** Pragmatic Competence
**Expected_Success:** 0.70
**Contexto:** Choosing the best way to present a family to a new class.

### Enunciado
You have to present your family to the class. Which introduction is best?

### Opciones
- [ ] A) My family is big and I like it. <!-- feedback: This is an opinion rather than a presentation, and it names none of the family members. -->
- [x] B) In my family there are four people: my father, my mother, my brother and me. <!-- feedback: This gives the number, the order from oldest to youngest and the relations, so the class can picture the family clearly. -->
- [ ] C) My family is four, and it is very nice. <!-- feedback: The number is given but no relation is named, so the class learns nothing about the people. -->
- [ ] D) There are my family and my house. <!-- feedback: This is not a grammatical presentation of the family and it names no individual person. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
The correct answer is the one that names each member and their relation. A presentation is judged by whether the listener can reconstruct the family afterwards, and only the full version supplies what is needed. Choosing detail over evaluation is a judgement the exam rewards, and it also consolidates the period's core skill of moving between a relation and the person who holds it.
