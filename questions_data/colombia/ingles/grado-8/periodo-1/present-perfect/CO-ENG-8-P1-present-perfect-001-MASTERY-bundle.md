# Bundle Mastery: Present Perfect - Grade 8

This bundle covers the Present Perfect tense, including its form, use with already/yet/just, for/since, and the contrast with Past Simple, in Colombian contexts at CEFR A2-B1 level.

---

## Question 1 [D3]

**ID:** `CO-ENG-8-P1-present-perfect-001-MASTERY-v1`
**Bloom:** Remember
**ICFES:** Comunicación y Representación
**Context:** A student in Bogotá is learning the structure of the present perfect tense.

### Enunciado
Choose the correct structure for the present perfect tense in English.

### Options
- [ ] A) Subject + past simple + complement <!-- feedback: Incorrect. This is the structure for the past simple, not present perfect. -->
- [ ] B) Subject + verb "to be" + past participle <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Present perfect uses "have/has", not "to be". -->
- [x] C) Subject + have/has + past participle <!-- feedback: Correct. The present perfect is formed with have/has + past participle. -->
- [ ] D) Subject + will + past participle <!-- feedback: Incorrect. This is a future perfect structure. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
The present perfect tense is formed with the auxiliary verb "have" (have/has) followed by the past participle of the main verb. Example: "I have visited Cartagena."

**Bloom:** Remember
**ICFES:** Comunicación y Representación
**Context:** A teacher in Medellín asks students to identify the auxiliary verb in present perfect.

### Enunciado
Which auxiliary verb do we use with "she" in the present perfect tense?

### Options
- [ ] A) Have <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Have" is used with I, you, we, they. -->
- [x] B) Has <!-- feedback: Correct. "Has" is used with he, she, it. -->
- [ ] C) Do <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Do" is an auxiliary for simple present questions. -->
- [ ] D) Did <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Did" is the auxiliary for simple past. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
The auxiliary "has" is used for third person singular (he, she, it), while "have" is used for all other persons (I, you, we, they).

**Bloom:** Understand
**ICFES:** Comunicación y Representación
**Context:** A student in Cali needs to choose the correct form of the verb.

### Enunciado
Choose the correct sentence in present perfect:

### Options
- [ ] A) I have went to Bogotá. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Went" is the past simple form. The past participle of "go" is "gone". -->
- [x] B) She has visited the Gold Museum. <!-- feedback: Correct. "Has visited" is the correct present perfect form. -->
- [ ] C) They have seen that movie yesterday. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Yesterday" is a past simple time marker and doesn't work with present perfect. -->
- [ ] D) He have eaten arepas. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "He" requires "has", not "have". -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
The present perfect is used for experiences without specifying when they happened. "She has visited the Gold Museum" means she had this experience at some point in her life.

### Options
- [ ] A) I already have eaten lunch. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. The correct word order is "have already eaten". -->
- [x] B) I have already finished my homework. <!-- feedback: Correct. "Already" goes between "have" and the past participle in affirmative sentences. -->

"Already" is placed between the auxiliary (have/has) and the past participle in affirmative sentences: "Subject + have/has + already + past participle."

Choose the correct sentence with "yet":

### Options

- [ ] B) He hasn't yet arrived. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Although grammatically possible, the most common position for "yet" is at the end of the sentence. -->

"Yet" is used in negative sentences and questions, usually at the end of the sentence. It means "up to now" and implies expectation.

What does "just" mean in this sentence?

### Options
- [ ] A) She arrived in a fair way. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Just" as "fair" is an adjective, but here it's an adverb. -->
- [ ] B) She arrived a long time ago. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Just" indicates recency. -->
- [x] C) She arrived a very short time ago. <!-- feedback: Correct. "Just" means "a moment ago" or "very recently". -->

"Just" in present perfect indicates that something happened a very short time ago. It goes between the auxiliary and the past participle: "have/has + just + past participle."

**ID:** `CO-ENG-8-P1-present-perfect-001-MASTERY-v9`
**Bloom:** Analyze
**ICFES:** Comprensión Lectora
**Context:** A student writes: "I have studied English since three years."

### Enunciado
Is this sentence correct?

### Options
- [ ] A) Yes, it's perfect. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. There is a mistake with "since". -->
- [x] B) No, it should be "I have studied English for three years." <!-- feedback: Correct. "Since" is used with a specific point in time; "for" is used with a duration. -->
- [ ] C) No, it should be "I studied English since three years." <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Since" with past simple doesn't work either. -->
- [ ] D) No, it should be "I have study English for three years." <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Study" is not the past participle. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
"For" is used with periods of time (for three years, for a week, for two hours). "Since" is used with specific points in time (since 2022, since Monday, since I was a child).

**Bloom:** Analyze
**ICFES:** Comprensión Lectora
**Context:** A student needs to choose between past simple and present perfect.

### Enunciado
Which sentence is correct?

### Options
- [ ] A) I have visited the Museum of Antioquia last week. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Last week" is a specific past time, requiring past simple. -->
- [x] B) I visited the Museum of Antioquia last week. <!-- feedback: Correct. "Last week" is a finished time in the past. -->
- [ ] C) I have visited the Museum of Antioquia yesterday. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Yesterday" requires past simple. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
Use past simple for completed actions at a specific time in the past (yesterday, last week, in 2023). Use present perfect for experiences when the time is not specified.

### Enunciado
What is the difference between the use of "since" and "for" in this dialogue?

### Options
- [ ] A) "Since" is used with a duration; "for" with a specific point. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. It's the opposite. -->
- [x] B) "Since 2020" refers to a specific starting point; "for five years" refers to a duration. <!-- feedback: Correct. "Since" + point in time, "for" + duration of time. -->

"Since" indicates the starting point of an action (since 2020, since Monday). "For" indicates the duration of an action (for five years, for two hours).
