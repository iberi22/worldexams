# Bundle Mastery: Modal Verbs - Grade 8

This bundle covers modal verbs: can, must, should, ought to, have to, may, might. It includes ability, obligation, permission, advice, and possibility, in Colombian contexts at CEFR A2-B1 level.

---

## Question 1 [D3]

**ID:** `CO-ENG-8-P1-modal-verbs-001-MASTERY-v1`
**Bloom:** Remember
**ICFES:** Comunicación y Representación
**Context:** A student in Bogotá is learning about modal verbs for the first time.

### Enunciado
Which of the following is a modal verb?

### Options
- [ ] A) Play <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Play" is a main verb, not a modal verb. -->
- [ ] B) Eating <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Eating" is a gerund form. -->
- [x] C) Must <!-- feedback: Correct. "Must" is a modal verb used to express obligation or necessity. -->
- [ ] D) Walked <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Walked" is a past simple verb. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
Modal verbs (can, must, should, may, might, etc.) are auxiliary verbs that express necessity, possibility, permission, or ability. They are always followed by the base form of the main verb.

## Question 2 [D3]

**ID:** `CO-ENG-8-P1-modal-verbs-001-MASTERY-v2`
**Bloom:** Remember
**ICFES:** Comunicación y Representación
**Context:** A teacher in Medellín asks about the structure of modal verbs.

### Enunciado
What is the correct structure after a modal verb?

### Options
- [ ] A) Modal verb + to + infinitive <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Most modal verbs are NOT followed by "to" (except "ought to"). -->
- [x] B) Modal verb + base form of the verb (infinitive without "to") <!-- feedback: Correct. Example: "You must study" (not "must to study"). -->
- [ ] C) Modal verb + -ing form <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Modals are followed by the base form, not -ing. -->
- [ ] D) Modal verb + past participle <!-- feedback: Incorrect. That's for perfect modals or passive voice. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
Modal verbs are followed by the base form of the verb (infinitive without "to"). The only exception is "ought to" which includes "to".

**Bloom:** Understand
**ICFES:** Comunicación y Representación
**Context:** A student in Cali wants to express ability.

### Enunciado
Choose the correct sentence to express that someone knows how to play the accordion:

### Options
- [ ] A) She must play the accordion. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Must" expresses obligation, not ability. -->
- [x] B) She can play the accordion. <!-- feedback: Correct. "Can" expresses ability (knowing how to do something). -->
- [ ] C) She should play the accordion. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Should" gives advice. -->
- [ ] D) She may play the accordion. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "May" expresses possibility or permission. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
"Can" is the modal verb used to express ability in the present. It means someone knows how to do something or is able to do it.

**Bloom:** Understand
**ICFES:** Comunicación y Representación
**Context:** A student in Barranquilla needs to express obligation.

### Enunciado
Which sentence expresses a strong obligation (necessity)?

### Options
- [ ] A) You can arrive on time. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Can" expresses ability or possibility. -->
- [x] B) You must arrive on time. <!-- feedback: Correct. "Must" expresses a strong obligation or necessity. -->

Which modal verb best completes this rule?

### Options

**ICFES:** Comprensión Lectora
**Context:** Read: "It may rain in the afternoon in Bogotá."

### Enunciado
What does "may" express in this sentence?

### Options
- [ ] A) Certainty <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "May" does not express certainty, only possibility. -->
- [x] B) Possibility (maybe 50% chance) <!-- feedback: Correct. "May" expresses that something is possible but not certain. -->

"May" can express two things: possibility (it may rain = it's possible) and permission (you may go = you are allowed to go). Context determines the meaning.

- [ ] B) "Can" expresses obligation, "must" expresses ability. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. It's the opposite. -->

## Question 14 [D8]

**ID:** `CO-ENG-8-P1-modal-verbs-001-MASTERY-v14`
**Bloom:** Evaluate
**ICFES:** Comprensión Lectora
**Context:** A student writes: "You mustn't study for the exam because it's easy."

### Enunciado
Is this sentence logical? Why or why not?

### Options
- [ ] A) Yes, it's logical. You don't need to study for an easy exam. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Mustn't" means prohibition (not allowed), not lack of necessity. -->
- [x] B) No, it should be "You don't have to study." <!-- feedback: Correct. "Mustn't" = prohibited. "Don't have to" = not necessary. -->
- [ ] C) Yes, "mustn't" means "it's not necessary". <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Mustn't" means prohibition, not lack of necessity. -->
- [ ] D) No, it should be "You may not study." <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "May not" expresses permission not given, not lack of necessity. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
"Mustn't" (must not) = prohibition (it is forbidden). "Don't have to" = lack of necessity (it is not required). This is a very common confusion.

- [x] C) Sentence (c) - "should to visit" is wrong. It should be "should visit" (modal + base form without "to"). <!-- feedback: Correct. "Should" is followed by the base form, not "to + infinitive". -->
- [ ] D) Sentence (d) - "may take" is wrong. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "May take" is correct. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
Most modal verbs (can, must, should, may, might) are followed by the base form of the verb WITHOUT "to". The exception is "ought to" which includes "to".
