# Weekly Pack W15: Travel and Tourism — transport, documents, and the present perfect with "ever", "never", "been to" and "gone to" (B2)

This pack develops travel and tourism vocabulary, asks for and gives information about journeys, and practises the present perfect with "ever" and "never" and the difference between "been to" and "gone to".

---

## Question 1 [D3-D4]
**ID:** CO-ING-8-2026-W15-travel-and-tourism-001-MASTERY-bundle-v1
**Bloom:** Remember
**ICFES:** Lingüística
**Expected_Success:** 0.85
**Contexto:** A family is looking at a map of a trip they finished last year and keeps talking about the city they visited.

### Enunciado
Look at the map on the wall: last year we ________ to Toronto and we still talk about it. We are home now. Use "been".

### Opciones
- [ ] A) have gone
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Have gone to" says the visit is still in progress, which contradicts the sentence "We are home now". -->
- [x] B) have been
  <!-- feedback: Correct. "Have been to" describes a completed visit that has finished, which agrees with the fact that the family is back home. -->
- [ ] C) have being
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Being" is not a past participle, so "have being" does not exist as a form of the verb. -->
- [ ] D) had went
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. The past simple "went" cannot follow "have", and "went" is not a participle; the correct participle is "been". -->

### Explicacion Pedagogica
The present perfect is formed with "have" plus a past participle, and the participle of "to be" is "been". "Have been to" says the visit is finished, while "have gone to" says the person is still away. The last clause of the sentence is the clue that decides which one is correct.

## Question 2 [D3-D4]
**ID:** CO-ING-8-2026-W15-travel-and-tourism-001-MASTERY-bundle-v2
**Bloom:** Remember
**ICFES:** Structural Cloze
**Expected_Success:** 0.80
**Contexto:** A student is preparing a school presentation about the archaeological sites of the Colombian Caribbean.

### Enunciado
"________ you ever been to the Ciudad Perdida?" Use "Have".

### Opciones
- [ ] A) Do
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Do" builds the present simple, which needs a finished time expression such as "last year". -->
- [ ] B) Did
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Did ... go" is possible, but this question is about experience at any time, so the present perfect is the required tense. -->
- [x] C) Have
  <!-- feedback: Correct. The present perfect is made with "have" + participle, so the question begins with "Have" and is answered with "Yes, I have". -->
- [ ] D) Are
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Are" cannot be used with "been" in a question about past experience, because it belongs to the verb "to be". -->

### Explicacion Pedagogica
Questions about experience use the present perfect: "Have you ever been to ...?" Here the present perfect has a second function, which is to ask whether something has happened at any time in a person's life, and that function requires "have" as the auxiliary.

## Question 4 [D3-D4]

**Contexto:** A short text about transport options between a coastal town and the capital is used in a reading lesson.

### Enunciado
The coastal town is connected to the capital by two options. The bus takes four hours and costs a small amount of money; the flight takes one hour but the airport is sixty kilometres from the town centre and the ticket is much more expensive. According to the text, what is the main disadvantage of the flight?

### Opciones
- [ ] A) It takes four hours instead of one.
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. It is the bus that takes four hours; the flight takes only one hour according to the text. -->
- [x] B) It is more expensive and the airport is far away.
  <!-- feedback: Correct. The text says the ticket is much more expensive and that the airport is sixty kilometres from the town centre. -->
- [ ] C) The bus does not reach the capital.
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. The text says the town is connected to the capital by two options, one of which is the bus. -->
- [ ] D) There is no flight on weekends.

### Explicacion Pedagogica
A disadvantage is a negative detail given by the text. Here two are stated: the price is much higher and the airport is sixty kilometres away. Any option that adds a new fact, such as a lack of weekend flights, cannot be answered from the passage and is therefore wrong.

## Question 5 [D5-D6]
**ID:** CO-ING-8-2026-W15-travel-and-tourism-001-MASTERY-bundle-v5
**Bloom:** Apply
**ICFES:** Grammatical Knowledge
**Expected_Success:** 0.75
**Contexto:** Two friends are comparing how long they have lived in their neighbourhoods.

### Enunciado
We ________ in this neighbourhood since 2019, but we travel abroad at least twice a year.

### Opciones
- [ ] A) live
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. The simple present "live" needs a present time expression, and it cannot be used with "since 2019". -->
- [x] B) have lived
  <!-- feedback: Correct. "Since 2019" marks a starting point that continues up to now, so the structure is have + past participle: have lived. -->
- [ ] C) are living since
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. The present continuous cannot be combined with "since" followed by a finished year such as 2019. -->
- [ ] D) lived for
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "For" needs a duration such as "for six years", and "lived" alone is not the present perfect. -->

### Explicacion Pedagogica
"Since" points to a starting moment in the past and requires the present perfect, because the situation continues now. "For" measures a length of time instead. Mixing "since" with the present continuous, or with the simple present, breaks the tense rule of English.

## Question 6 [D5-D6]
**ID:** CO-ING-8-2026-W15-travel-and-tourism-001-MASTERY-bundle-v6
**Bloom:** Apply
**ICFES:** Structural Cloze
**Expected_Success:** 0.75
**Contexto:** A student is talking about the countries he has and has not visited.

### Enunciado
My cousins have travelled a lot, but I ________ been to another country. I only know Bogotá well. Use "haven't".

### Opciones
- [ ] A) have
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "I have been" states the opposite of what the sentence says, because it confirms that the speaker has been abroad. -->
- [ ] B) am not
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Am not been" does not exist; the negative of the present perfect is built with "have not", never with "be". -->
- [ ] C) didn't
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Didn't been" is not grammatical; a negative past form would be "didn't go", which changes the structure. -->
- [x] D) haven't
  <!-- feedback: Correct. The present perfect with "never" or its short form "haven't" says the experience has not happened up to now. -->

### Explicacion Pedagogica
To deny past experience that extends up to now, English uses the present perfect with "never" or "haven't". The negative form always contains the auxiliary "have", never "am" or "did", so options B and C cannot be correct forms of the present perfect.

## Question 7 [D5-D6]

**Expected_Success:** 0.75
**Contexto:** A group of students is visiting Cartagena and needs to find a well-known historical building.

### Enunciado
You are a tourist in Cartagena and you need to reach the Castillo de San Felipe. Which question is the most appropriate?

### Opciones
- [ ] A) "Where is the Castillo? And hurry up, I am late."
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "And hurry up" turns a request into an order, which is not appropriate when asking a stranger for help. -->
- [ ] B) "You know where the Castillo is, don't you?"
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. A tag question like this sounds impatient when it is directed at a stranger who has just been met. -->
- [x] C) "Excuse me, could you tell me how to get to the Castillo de San Felipe, please?"
  <!-- feedback: Correct. "Excuse me" and "could you" make the request polite, and the question is clear enough to be answered. -->

### Explicacion Pedagogica
Asking for directions in a foreign city follows a simple polite pattern: apologise briefly, use a modal question such as "could you", and name the destination. Commands and impatient tag questions break the politeness conventions that make a stranger willing to help.

## Question 9 [D7-D8]
**ID:** CO-ING-8-2026-W15-travel-and-tourism-001-MASTERY-bundle-v9
**Bloom:** Analyze
**ICFES:** Grammatical Knowledge
**Expected_Success:** 0.70
**Contexto:** An advertisement for domestic flights compares the time spent travelling by bus and by plane.

### Enunciado
Flying is much ________ than travelling by bus.

### Opciones
- [ ] A) fast
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. The base form "fast" cannot follow "much" in a comparison, because the comparative form is required here. -->
- [x] B) faster
  <!-- feedback: Correct. Short adjectives of one syllable form the comparative with "-er", so fast becomes faster, and it is followed by "than". -->
- [ ] C) fastest
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Fastest" is the superlative form, and a superlative needs "the" and a group, never "than". -->
- [ ] D) the fastest
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. The definite superlative cannot be used with "than", which only appears with the comparative. -->

### Explicacion Pedagogica
The comparative ends in "-er" for short adjectives and is followed by "than": "faster than". The superlative ends in "-est" and requires "the". Recognising which form a sentence needs is what separates the comparative from the superlative.

Tourism creates work for local guides and cooks, but in the last five years rents in the historic centre have gone up by thirty per cent. Residents say that families who were born there can no longer afford to live in the houses where they grew up. What does the text mainly show?

## Question 12 [D7-D8]
**ID:** CO-ING-8-2026-W15-travel-and-tourism-001-MASTERY-bundle-v12
**Bloom:** Evaluate
**ICFES:** Pragmatic Competence
**Expected_Success:** 0.70
**Contexto:** A group of students is organising a visit to a city museum during a school trip.

### Enunciado
The city museum opens from Tuesday to Sunday, from nine in the morning to four in the afternoon. On Mondays it is closed. The guided visit lasts forty-five minutes and must be booked at the entrance. A group arrives on Monday morning and wants the guided visit. What should the organiser do?

### Opciones
- [ ] A) Arrive at ten on Monday and ask for the visit, because the museum opens on Mondays.
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. This contradicts the text, which says the museum is closed on Mondays. -->
- [ ] B) Come on Monday and explain that the museum should be open.
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Asking politely does not change the opening days, so the visit still cannot take place on Monday. -->
- [x] C) Arrive on Tuesday morning and book the guided visit at the entrance.
  <!-- feedback: Correct. Since the museum is closed on Mondays, the organiser must arrive on Tuesday and make the booking in person. -->
- [ ] D) Book the visit on Monday by phone, since the museum is open in the mornings.
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. A Monday visit is impossible because of the closing day, and the text does not mention telephone booking. -->

### Explicacion Pedagogica
Planning requires checking every constraint: the day, the hours and the way the booking is made. Monday is excluded by the closing day, so the only workable plan is to arrive on Tuesday and book at the entrance. Options that ignore the closing day or invent a telephone service cannot succeed.
