# Weekly Pack W08: Conditions and Possibilities (B1)

This bundle focuses on Zero and First Conditionals to talk about facts and future possibilities, aligned with CEFR B1 level for Grade 8.

---

## Question 1 [D3-D4]
**ID:** `CO-ING-8-2026-W08-conditions-possibilities-001-MASTERY-v1`
**Bloom:** Remember
**ICFES:** Lingüística
**Expected_Success:** 0.85
**Contexto:** Expressing a general truth (Zero Conditional).

### Enunciado
If you ________ water to 100 degrees Celsius, it ________.

### Opciones
- [x] A) heat / boils <!-- feedback: Correct! Zero conditional uses Present Simple in both clauses for facts. -->
- [ ] B) heat / will boil <!-- feedback: Incorrect. This sounds like a specific prediction, but Zero is for general facts. -->
- [ ] C) heats / boil <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Subject-verb agreement errors. -->
- [ ] D) heated / boiled <!-- feedback: Incorrect. This is past tense. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
El Condicional Cero se usa para hechos científicos o verdades universales. La estructura es: If + Presente Simple, Presente Simple.

## Question 2 [D3-D4]
**ID:** `CO-ING-8-2026-W08-conditions-possibilities-001-MASTERY-v2`
**Bloom:** Remember
**ICFES:** Lingüística
**Expected_Success:** 0.80
**Contexto:** Making a future plan dependent on a condition (First Conditional).

### Enunciado
If I ________ time this weekend, I ________ to the movies with you.

### Opciones
- [ ] A) will have / go <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Will" never goes in the "if" clause. -->
- [x] B) have / will go <!-- feedback: Correct! First conditional: If + Present Simple, Will + base form. -->
- [ ] C) had / would go <!-- feedback: Incorrect. This is Second Conditional (imaginary). -->
- [ ] D) have / go <!-- feedback: Incorrect. This would be a general habit, not a specific plan. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
El Primer Condicional se usa para situaciones reales o muy probables en el futuro. Usamos el Presente Simple después de "if" y "will" en la otra parte.

**ICFES:** Lingüística

### Enunciado
If you ________ your homework, the teacher ________ angry.

### Opciones
- [ ] A) not do / is <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Needs auxiliary verbs. -->

## Question 7 [D7-D8]
**ID:** `CO-ING-8-2026-W08-conditions-possibilities-001-MASTERY-v7`
**Bloom:** Analyze
**ICFES:** Lingüística
**Expected_Success:** 0.55
**Contexto:** Swapping clause order.

### Enunciado
Which sentence is grammatically correct?

### Opciones
- [ ] A) If you will study hard, you will pass. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. No "will" in the "if" clause. -->
- [x] B) You will pass the exam if you study hard. <!-- feedback: Correct! The order can be result + if + condition (no comma needed). -->
- [ ] C) You pass the exam if you will study hard. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. -->
- [ ] D) If you study hard you will pass. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Needs a comma when "if" starts the sentence. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
Se puede cambiar el orden de las cláusulas: [Condition], [Result] (con coma) o [Result] [Condition] (sin coma). Nunca se pone "will" en la parte del "if".

## Question 8 [D7-D8]
**ID:** `CO-ING-8-2026-W08-conditions-possibilities-001-MASTERY-v8`
**Bloom:** Analyze
**ICFES:** Pragmática
**Expected_Success:** 0.50
**Contexto:** Distinguishing Zero and First conditional by meaning.

### Enunciado
**Sentence A:** If I eat peanuts, I get sick.
**Sentence B:** If I eat those peanuts, I will get sick.

What is the difference?

### Opciones
- [ ] A) There is no difference in meaning. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. -->
- [x] B) A is a general allergy (always true); B is a prediction for this specific moment. <!-- feedback: Correct! A is Zero Conditional, B is First Conditional. -->
- [ ] C) A is about the past; B is about the future. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Both are in present/future context. -->
- [ ] D) B is more polite than A. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
El Condicional Cero (A) describe una ley general o hábito. El Primer Condicional (B) describe lo que pasará en un caso particular en el futuro.

## Question 10 [D9-D10]
**ID:** `CO-ING-8-2026-W08-conditions-possibilities-001-MASTERY-v10`
**Bloom:** Evaluate
**ICFES:** Lingüística
**Expected_Success:** 0.40
**Contexto:** Identifying errors in complex conditions.

### Enunciado
Identify the error:
"I **will go (A)** to the university **if (B)** I **will pass (C)** the Saber 11 **exam (D)**."

### Opciones
- [ ] A) will go <!-- feedback: Incorrect. This is the correct result form. -->
- [ ] B) if <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Correct conjunction. -->
- [x] C) will pass <!-- feedback: Correct! Error found. We must use Present Simple "pass" after "if". -->
- [ ] D) exam <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Correct noun. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
Uno de los errores más persistentes en B1 es intentar usar el futuro (will) en ambas partes de la oración. La regla gramatical prohíbe el uso de "will" inmediatamente después de "if" en este tipo de oraciones.

**Bloom:** Evaluate
**ICFES:** Sociolingüística
**Expected_Success:** 0.30
**Contexto:** Selecting the best conclusion for a conditional argument.

### Enunciado
"Governments are discussing new taxes on sugar. If these taxes are approved, the price of soda will rise. Consequently..."

### Opciones
- [x] A) people might consume less sugar and improve their health. <!-- feedback: Correct! Follows the logic of cause and effect with a modal of possibility. -->
- [ ] B) everyone will become a millionaire. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Illogical conclusion. -->
- [ ] C) sugar was very popular in the past. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Irrelevant to the conditional argument. -->
- [ ] D) the taxes didn't work because they are expensive. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Contradicts the "if ... are approved" future focus. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
Para completar un argumento condicional, el estudiante debe identificar una consecuencia que sea tanto gramaticalmente posible como lógicamente coherente con las premisas dadas.
