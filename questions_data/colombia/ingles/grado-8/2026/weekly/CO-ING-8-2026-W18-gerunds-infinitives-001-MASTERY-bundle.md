# Weekly Pack W18: Gerunds and Infinitives (B1)

This bundle explores when to use the -ing form (gerund) and when to use the "to" form (infinitive) after specific verbs, aligned with CEFR B1 level.

---

## Question 1 [D3-D4]
**ID:** `CO-ING-8-2026-W18-gerunds-infinitives-001-MASTERY-v1`
**Bloom:** Remember
**ICFES:** Lingüística
**Expected_Success:** 0.85
**Contexto:** Verbs of preference.

### Enunciado
I really enjoy ________ to vallenato music during the weekends.

### Opciones
- [ ] A) to listen <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Enjoy" is followed by -ing. -->
- [x] B) listening <!-- feedback: Correct! After "enjoy", we must use the gerund (-ing). -->
- [ ] C) listen <!-- feedback: Incorrect. -->
- [ ] D) listened <!-- feedback: Incorrect. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
En inglés, ciertos verbos como "enjoy", "finish" y "mind" siempre van seguidos de un gerundio (forma -ing).

### Opciones
- [x] A) Swimming <!-- feedback: Correct! When an activity is the subject of the sentence, we use the gerund. -->

### Enunciado

### Opciones
- [ ] A) to eat <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Stop to eat" means you paused another activity to eat. -->

## Question 8 [D7-D8]
**ID:** `CO-ING-8-2026-W18-gerunds-infinitives-001-MASTERY-v8`
**Bloom:** Analyze
**ICFES:** Pragmática
**Expected_Success:** 0.50
**Contexto:** Understanding preference nuance.

### Enunciado
"I like **dancing**" vs. "I'd like **to dance**."
What is the difference?

### Opciones
- [ ] A) They mean exactly the same. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. -->
- [x] B) One is a general habit, the other is a specific desire for now. <!-- feedback: Correct! "Like + ing" is for general tastes; "Would like + to" is a polite request/desire. -->
- [ ] C) Both are about the past. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. -->
- [ ] D) The second one is rude. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
"I like + -ing" describe un gusto general. "I would like (I'd like) + to" se usa para pedir algo o expresar un deseo específico en un momento dado.
