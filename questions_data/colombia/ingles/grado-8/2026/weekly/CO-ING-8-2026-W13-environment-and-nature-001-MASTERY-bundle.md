## Question 1 [D3-D4]
**ID:** CO-ING-8-2026-W13-environment-and-nature-001-MASTERY-bundle-v1
**Bloom:** Remember
**ICFES:** Lexico
**Expected_Success:** 0.85
**Contexto:** A recycling campaign in the municipality of Chía explains the three words that describe what households should do with their rubbish.

### Enunciado
What does "to recycle" mean?

### Opciones
- [ ] A) to use something again without throwing it away
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. That describes the general idea of reusing materials, but recycling means treating used material so it can be made into something new. -->
- [ ] B) to put rubbish in a bag for the collectors
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Putting rubbish in a bag is disposal, the opposite of recycling, which keeps materials out of the rubbish. -->
- [x] C) to treat used materials so they can be used again
  <!-- feedback: Correct. Recycling means processing used material such as glass, metal or plastic so that it can be used again in new products. -->
- [ ] D) to make a river dirty with waste
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Making a river dirty is polluting, which is the environmental problem that recycling tries to solve. -->

### Explicacion Pedagogica
The three "R" words are different: "reduce" means to buy less, "reuse" means to use an object again, and "recycle" means to treat used material so it can be remade. Knowing the exact difference is what allows a student to explain a recycling campaign correctly in English.

**Contexto:** An environmental poster in a school in Villavicencio invites families to buy less disposable plastic.

### Enunciado
If you ________ the amount of plastic you buy, you will throw away much less waste. Use "reduce".

### Opciones
- [ ] A) redoing
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Redo" means to do something again, so it cannot mean making a quantity smaller. -->
- [ ] B) reusing
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Reuse" means using a thing again, but it does not reduce how much new plastic you buy. -->

  <!-- feedback: Correct. "To reduce something" means to make it smaller or less, and it is the only option that fits "the amount of plastic you buy". -->

## Question 5 [D5-D6]
**ID:** CO-ING-8-2026-W13-environment-and-nature-001-MASTERY-bundle-v5
**Bloom:** Apply
**ICFES:** Grammatical Knowledge
**Expected_Success:** 0.75
**Contexto:** A neighbourhood in Bogotá installed sorting bins in 2021 and the city published the results afterwards.

### Enunciado
Since the recycling bins were installed, the amount of waste in the neighbourhood ________ every week. Use the passive perfect.

### Opciones
- [ ] A) reduced
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. The simple past "reduced" closes the process in the past, but the text says the reduction continues week after week. -->
- [x] B) has been reduced
  <!-- feedback: Correct. "Since" points from a past event to now, so the passive present perfect "has been reduced" is the required form. -->
- [ ] C) is reducing
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. The passive of the present continuous would be "is being reduced"; "is reducing" cannot be used passively like this. -->
- [ ] D) reduces to
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Reduces to" needs a final value after it, and it is not compatible with the ongoing meaning of "every week". -->

### Explicacion Pedagogica
"Since" connects a past event with the present, so it goes with the present perfect, and because the focus is on the waste (the thing being reduced), the structure is passive: has been reduced. The present continuous passive would need "is being reduced", which describes an action happening at this moment.

### Enunciado
In some areas of La Guajira, ________ has turned fertile land into sand because the trees were removed. Use "desertification".

### Opciones
- [ ] A) erosion
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Erosion is the wearing away of soil by water or wind; it is one step, not the final result described in the sentence. -->

## Question 9 [D7-D8]
**ID:** CO-ING-8-2026-W13-environment-and-nature-001-MASTERY-bundle-v9
**Bloom:** Analyze
**ICFES:** Grammatical Knowledge
**Expected_Success:** 0.70
**Contexto:** An infographic compares national statistics on plastic waste and recycling rates.

### Enunciado
Colombia produces more plastic waste per person than most countries, ________ our recycling rate is still very low.

### Opciones
- [ ] A) however
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "However" is an adverb, and as a connector it needs a full stop before it; here it would have to stand between the subject and the verb. -->
- [x] B) even though
  <!-- feedback: Correct. "Even though" introduces a concession between two true facts: high production and a low recycling rate. -->
- [ ] C) because
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Because" makes the first clause the cause of the second, but the two facts are a contrast, not a cause and effect. -->
- [ ] D) otherwise
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Otherwise" expresses the consequence of not doing something, which is not the relationship described here. -->

### Explicacion Pedagogica
Linkers must match the logical relationship. A contrast between two facts that are both true needs "even though"; "because" would claim a cause that does not exist, and "however" is an adverb that cannot be placed inside this clause. Choosing the linker by the meaning of the two halves is the reliable method.

## Question 11 [D7-D8]
**ID:** CO-ING-8-2026-W13-environment-and-nature-001-MASTERY-bundle-v11
**Bloom:** Analyze
**ICFES:** Structural Cloze
**Expected_Success:** 0.70
**Contexto:** A city council document recommends long-term measures for cleaner air in urban corridors.

### Enunciado
Cities ________ build more parks and cycle lanes, because traffic and pollution keep getting worse.

### Opciones
- [x] A) ought to
  <!-- feedback: Correct. "Ought to" gives advice about future actions and takes the base form of the verb: ought to + build. -->
- [ ] B) ought
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Ought" on its own is not a complete structure for advice; it needs "to" before the verb. -->
- [ ] C) ought to built
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Ought to" is followed by the bare infinitive, never by the past participle. -->
- [ ] D) ought being to
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Ought being to" mixes the continuous and the infinitive, which is not a form in English. -->

### Explicacion Pedagogica
"Ought to" belongs to the modal family, so it behaves exactly like "should" and "must": modal + to + base form of the verb. It cannot take "-ing" or a past participle, and it cannot stand alone. Recognising that it is a modal is what makes the pattern predictable.
