# Weekly Pack W17: Urban Life and Transport — the city, public transport, "used to" against "now", and comparison with "than" (B2)

This pack works with the vocabulary of city life and public transport, practises the contrast between "used to" and present habits, and compares two options with "than".

## Question 1 [D3-D4]
**ID:** CO-ING-8-2026-W17-urban-life-and-transport-001-MASTERY-bundle-v1
**Bloom:** Remember
**ICFES:** Lingüística
**Expected_Success:** 0.85
**Contexto:** A grandfather in Tunja describes how he travelled to school when he was a boy, compared with today.

### Enunciado
When my grandfather was a boy, he used to ________ to school every day. Now he takes the bus. Use "walk".

### Opciones
- [ ] A) walked
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Used to" is followed by the base form of the verb, so adding the past form "walked" breaks the structure. -->
- [x] B) walk
  <!-- feedback: Correct. After "used to" we always use the base form of the verb: used to + walk. -->
- [ ] C) walking
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Used to" is never followed by the "-ing" form; that belongs to "be used to walking". -->
- [ ] D) walks
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. The third person form belongs to the present simple, but "used to" describes a past habit. -->

### Explicacion Pedagogica
"Used to" describes a habit or state that existed in the past and no longer does, and its grammar is completely regular: it is always followed by the base form of the verb. The contrast with "now he takes the bus" confirms that the past situation has changed.

### Enunciado
Which word means the place where people wait for the bus?

### Opciones
- [ ] A) station
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. A station is a large building used for trains or long-distance buses, not a place in the street. -->
- [ ] B) platform
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. A platform is the raised surface next to railway tracks where passengers wait for a train. -->
- [x] C) bus stop
  <!-- feedback: Correct. A "bus stop" is the place, usually in the street, where buses stop to pick up passengers. -->

**Contexto:** A student explains why she has adapted to her new neighbourhood in Bogotá.

### Enunciado
I ________ living in a small flat near the river, so I am not used to the noise of the traffic.

### Opciones
- [ ] A) used to
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Used to living" describes a habit in the past, but the sentence is about a present state and a present reaction. -->
- [ ] B) was used to
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Was used to" is the past of being accustomed; the sentence needs the present form "am used to". -->
- [ ] C) used to being
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Used to" is never followed by "being"; the structure that requires "-ing" is "be used to being". -->

## Question 6 [D5-D6]
**ID:** CO-ING-8-2026-W17-urban-life-and-transport-001-MASTERY-bundle-v6
**Bloom:** Apply
**ICFES:** Structural Cloze
**Expected_Success:** 0.75
**Contexto:** Two commuters discuss why they prefer different ways of moving through the city.

### Enunciado
Cycling through the city is ________ than driving, because you do not have to look for a place to park.

### Opciones
- [ ] A) healthy
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. The base form "healthy" cannot be compared with "than"; a comparative form is required. -->
- [ ] B) healthiest
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Healthiest" is the superlative and needs "the" plus a group, never the word "than". -->
- [ ] C) more healthy
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. For short adjectives we add "-ier", so we say "healthier" and not "more healthy". -->
- [x] D) healthier
  <!-- feedback: Correct. Short adjectives take "-ier" to form the comparative, so healthy becomes healthier, and it is followed by "than". -->

### Explicacion Pedagogica
Adjectives of one syllable form the comparative by adding "-er" or "-ier", while longer adjectives use "more". The superlative uses "-est" or "most" and requires "the". Comparing forms correctly is what makes the sentence grammatically complete with "than".

## Question 8 [D5-D6]

**Bloom:** Apply
**ICFES:** Uso de la lengua
**Expected_Success:** 0.75
**Contexto:** A writing exercise focuses on the articles and prepositions used to talk about transport.

### Enunciado
Choose the correct sentence about urban transport.

### Opciones
- [ ] A) "I go to work by the TransMilenio every day."
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Proper names of specific services take no article, so it must be "by TransMilenio" without "the". -->
- [ ] B) "I travel public transport every morning."
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. The verb "travel" needs a preposition such as "by" or "on" before the means of transport. -->
- [x] C) "I travel on public transport to avoid traffic."
  <!-- feedback: Correct. "Travel on" is the natural collocation, and "public transport" used in a general sense takes no article. -->
- [ ] D) "I go home by the bus every morning, and it is always full."
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. In the general sense the noun takes no article: "by bus"; "by the bus" would refer to one specific vehicle. -->

### Explicacion Pedagogica
Two rules govern articles with transport: proper names of services and general uses of the noun "bus" take no article, and the verbs of movement are followed by "by" or "on". Applying both rules removes the article error and the missing preposition in the same step.

### Enunciado
My neighbourhood ________ have a small market on the corner; now there is a large supermarket two streets away. Use "used to".

### Opciones

  <!-- feedback: Correct. "Used to have" describes a situation that existed in the past and no longer does, which is the contrast the sentence makes. -->

  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Was used to" means being accustomed to, and it would need a noun or an "-ing" form after it. -->
