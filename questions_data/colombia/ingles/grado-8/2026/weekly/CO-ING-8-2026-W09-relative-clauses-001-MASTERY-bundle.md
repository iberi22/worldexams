# Weekly Pack W09: Connecting Ideas - Relative Clauses (B1)

This bundle focuses on defining relative clauses using "who", "which", "that", and "where" to provide essential information about people, things, and places, aligned with CEFR B1 level for Grade 8.

---

## Question 1 [D3-D4]
**ID:** `CO-ING-8-2026-W09-relative-clauses-001-MASTERY-v1`
**Bloom:** Remember
**ICFES:** Lingüística
**Expected_Success:** 0.85
**Contexto:** Identifying people.

### Enunciado
A teacher is a person ________ helps students to learn.

### Opciones
- [x] A) who <!-- feedback: Correct! We use "who" to refer to people. -->
- [ ] B) which <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Which" is for things or animals. -->
- [ ] C) where <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Where" is for places. -->
- [ ] D) when <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "When" is for time. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
Las oraciones de relativo (relative clauses) nos permiten dar información extra sobre alguien o algo. Usamos "who" específicamente para personas.

## Question 7 [D7-D8]
**ID:** `CO-ING-8-2026-W09-relative-clauses-001-MASTERY-v7`
**Bloom:** Analyze
**ICFES:** Lingüística
**Expected_Success:** 0.55
**Contexto:** Omitting the relative pronoun.

### Enunciado
In which sentence can the relative pronoun be **omitted** (deleted)?

### Opciones
- [ ] A) The man who lives next door is a musician. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Who" is the subject of "lives". -->
- [x] B) The movie (that) we watched yesterday was boring. <!-- feedback: Correct! "That" is the object (we watched it), so it can be omitted. -->
- [ ] C) The girl who won the prize is my sister. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Who" is the subject. -->
- [ ] D) A hospital is a building where people go when they are sick. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Where" is rarely omitted this way. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
En inglés, podemos omitir "who", "which" o "that" solo cuando funcionan como el **objeto** de la oración de relativo (cuando hay otro sujeto después del pronombre).

## Question 8 [D7-D8]
**ID:** `CO-ING-8-2026-W09-relative-clauses-001-MASTERY-v8`
**Bloom:** Analyze
**ICFES:** Pragmática
**Expected_Success:** 0.50
**Contexto:** Understanding definitions.

### Enunciado
"An orphan is a child whose parents are dead."
What information does the relative clause provide?

### Opciones
- [ ] A) It describes where the child lives. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. -->
- [x] B) It gives essential information to define what an orphan is. <!-- feedback: Correct! Without this clause, we don't know which child we mean. -->
- [ ] C) It is extra information that we can delete without changing the meaning. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. It's a defining clause. -->
- [ ] D) It tells us the name of the child. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
Las "defining relative clauses" (oraciones de relativo especificativas) son esenciales para el significado de la oración; sin ellas, el sustantivo principal no queda bien definido.

## Question 9 [D7-D8]
**ID:** `CO-ING-8-2026-W09-relative-clauses-001-MASTERY-v9`
**Bloom:** Analyze
**ICFES:** Lingüística
**Expected_Success:** 0.45
**Contexto:** Combining sentences.

### Enunciado
Combine these two sentences:
"I lost the watch. My grandmother gave it to me."

### Opciones
- [ ] A) I lost the watch who my grandmother gave to me. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Watch is not a person. -->
- [x] B) I lost the watch that my grandmother gave to me. <!-- feedback: Correct! "That" connects the object to its origin. -->
- [ ] C) I lost the watch where my grandmother gave to me. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. -->
- [ ] D) I lost the watch whose my grandmother gave to me. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
Para combinar oraciones, reemplazamos el objeto repetido ("it") por un pronombre relativo ("that" o "which") que conecte ambas ideas.

### Opciones

## Question 12 [D9-D10]
**ID:** `CO-ING-8-2026-W09-relative-clauses-001-MASTERY-v12`
**Bloom:** Evaluate
**ICFES:** Sociolingüística
**Expected_Success:** 0.30
**Contexto:** Writing a descriptive sentence about a Colombian landmark.

### Enunciado
Choose the best way to describe "The Gold Museum" (El Museo del Oro) in Bogotá:

### Opciones
- [x] A) It is a world-famous museum which houses the largest collection of pre-Hispanic gold work. <!-- feedback: Correct! Uses a relative clause to provide essential and impressive information. -->
- [ ] B) It is a place who has many golden things for people. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Who" is for people, not places. -->
- [ ] C) It is the building where gold was invented long ago. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Gold is a natural element, not "invented" in a museum. -->
- [ ] D) It is a museum that it is near the city center. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Doubling the subject (that it is) is a common error. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
La creación de descripciones complejas requiere el uso de pronombres relativos adecuados y evitar errores comunes como la redundancia del sujeto ("that it is").
