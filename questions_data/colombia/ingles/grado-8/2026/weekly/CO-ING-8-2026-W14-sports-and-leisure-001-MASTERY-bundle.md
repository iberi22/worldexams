# Weekly Pack W14: Sports and Leisure — sport and hobby vocabulary, and "would like to" for making plans (B2)

This pack works with vocabulary of sports, pastimes and hobbies, the structure "would like to" for making plans, and the language of preference and invitation around free time.

## Question 1 [D3-D4]
**ID:** CO-ING-8-2026-W14-sports-and-leisure-001-MASTERY-bundle-v1
**Bloom:** Remember
**ICFES:** Lexico
**Expected_Success:** 0.85
**Contexto:** A coach in Cali explains to young players what they must do before the match starts.

### Enunciado
Before the match, the players ________ for ten minutes so that their muscles do not get cold.

### Opciones
- [ ] A) warm down
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Warming down is the opposite action: it is done after training to cool the body down gradually. -->
- [x] B) warm up
  <!-- feedback: Correct. "To warm up" is the standard expression for preparing the body before exercising so the muscles do not get cold. -->
- [ ] C) warm off
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Warm off" is not an expression that exists in English, so no meaning can be attached to it here. -->
- [ ] D) warm round
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Warm round" does not exist either; the correct phrasal verb in this context is "warm up". -->

### Explicacion Pedagogica
Sports English depends on fixed phrases: "warm up" before the activity and "cool down" after it. Phrasal verbs cannot be improvised, because changing the particle changes the meaning or makes the phrase non-existent, as with "warm off" and "warm round".

## Question 2 [D3-D4]
**ID:** CO-ING-8-2026-W14-sports-and-leisure-001-MASTERY-bundle-v2
**Bloom:** Remember
**ICFES:** Structural Cloze
**Expected_Success:** 0.80
**Contexto:** Two classmates are talking in the school yard while rain starts to fall in Pasto.

### Enunciado
It's raining heavily. We ________ cancel the football match and play indoors instead. Use "would like to".

### Opciones
- [ ] A) would liking to
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. After a modal we never use the "-ing" form, and "would like" is already a complete structure. -->
- [ ] B) would like
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. The infinitive needs its "to": "would like to cancel" is the correct form, not "would like cancel". -->
- [x] C) would like to
  <!-- feedback: Correct. "Would like to" expresses a polite preference, and the infinitive "cancel" completes the structure correctly. -->
- [ ] D) would likes to
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Would" is a modal, so the following verb never takes the third person ending "-s". -->

### Explicacion Pedagogica
"Would like to" is a polite way to say what a person wants to do, and it follows the same rule as every modal: modal + to + base form of the verb. It is never used with "-ing" and never with a verb in the "-s" form.

### Enunciado
Laura is learning to swim, but she would ________ go to the local club on Saturdays. Use "prefer".

### Opciones

  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Would preferring" is wrong because modals are always followed by the bare infinitive of the verb. -->

### Enunciado
On Sundays my father and I ________ our bikes along the river and then have lunch in the town. Use the correct verb form.

### Opciones
- [x] A) ride
  <!-- feedback: Correct. The subject "my father and I" is plural, so the simple present takes the base form "ride" with two subjects. -->
- [ ] B) rides
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Rides" is the third person singular form, which does not apply when the subject is "my father and I". -->

  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Riding" is not a complete verb in this clause, because there is no auxiliary before it. -->

**ICFES:** Pragmatic Competence

**Contexto:** A classmate has a free Sunday afternoon and a group is playing basketball at the court in the neighbourhood.

### Enunciado
You want to invite a friend to play basketball on Sunday. Which invitation is the most appropriate?

### Opciones
- [ ] A) "You must come to the court at ten, no excuses."
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "No excuses" turns a friendly invitation into a command and sounds aggressive between friends. -->

  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. It applies emotional pressure by threatening to inform other people instead of inviting sincerely. -->
- [x] C) "Would you like to play basketball on Sunday? We usually play at ten."
  <!-- feedback: Correct. It uses a polite question, adds useful information about the time and leaves the decision to the listener. -->

## Question 8 [D5-D6]
**ID:** CO-ING-8-2026-W14-sports-and-leisure-001-MASTERY-bundle-v8
**Bloom:** Apply
**ICFES:** Lexical Knowledge
**Expected_Success:** 0.75
**Contexto:** A sports newspaper reports the result of the match between two national teams.

### Enunciado
Choose the correct sentence for a sports report.

### Opciones
- [ ] A) "Colombia beat Brazil two goals to one in the last match."
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Beat" does not take a score after the opponent, because the score belongs to the verb "won". -->
- [x] B) "Colombia won Brazil two goals to one in the last match."
  <!-- feedback: Correct. "To win a match" takes the opponent as the object and can be followed by the score, in the order verb + opponent + score. -->
- [ ] C) "Colombia won two goals to one against Brazil's team, which was the Brazil of the match."
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. The relative clause repeats "Brazil" and loses the verb that shows the result, so the sentence does not report the outcome clearly. -->
- [ ] D) "Colombia beat two goals to one Brazil in the last match."
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. The score cannot be placed between the verb and its object; the correct order is verb + opponent + score. -->

### Explicacion Pedagogica
Two verbs describe the result of a match: "to win" and "to beat", and they do not behave in the same way. "Win" takes the opponent and allows a score after it, while "beat" only takes the opponent and never a score. This difference in structure is what makes one sentence correct and the others wrong.

The gym is closed for renovation, so I ________ play football in the park instead.

### Enunciado
"At forty, Carlos decided to ________ playing table tennis, and now he plays every weekend."

### Opciones

  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Get up" means to leave the bed, so it has nothing to do with starting a regular activity. -->

  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Give up" means to stop doing something, which is the opposite of the decision described. -->

The sports school of the neighbourhood announces new swimming courses for teenagers. The first session is on Tuesday at four in the afternoon and there are twelve places. Students who want a place must sign up before Friday. Diego wants to join but he is not free on Tuesday. What should he do?

### Opciones
- [ ] A) Arrive at ten on Tuesday and join late.
  <!-- feedback: Incorrect. The first session starts on Tuesday and there is no mention of accepting late arrivals, so he would miss the beginning of the course. -->

The text fixes the first session on Tuesday, so the only useful question is whether another group or another session exists. The other options either ignore the information or act against the purpose of the course.
