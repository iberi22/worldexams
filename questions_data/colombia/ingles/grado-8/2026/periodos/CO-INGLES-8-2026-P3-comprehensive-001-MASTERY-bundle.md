# Bundle Mastery: Past Perfect, Conditional Type 1, and Future Continuous

This bundle covers the past perfect simple, first conditional (if + present simple, will + base verb), and future continuous tense.

---

## Question 1 [D3]

**ID:** `CO-INGLES-8-2026-P3-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v1`
**Bloom:** Remember
**ICFES:** Comunicación
**Context:** In Bogotá, a teacher explains the past perfect tense.

### Enunciado
How do we form the past perfect tense?

### Options
- [ ] A) Have + past participle <!-- feedback: Incorrect. That's present perfect. -->
- [x] B) Had + past participle <!-- feedback: Correct. Past perfect: had + past participle. -->
- [ ] C) Was/were + past participle <!-- feedback: Incorrect. That's passive voice. -->
- [ ] D) Had + base verb <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Need past participle, not base. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
El past perfect se forma con had + past participle. Se usa para hablar de una acción que ocurrió antes de otra acción en el pasado. Ej: "When I arrived, the movie had already started."

### Options

- [ ] B) Will be + past participle <!-- feedback: Incorrect. That's future passive. -->

**ID:** `CO-INGLES-8-2026-P3-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v6`
**Bloom:** Apply
**ICFES:** Indagación
**Context:** In Pereira, a student makes a first conditional sentence.

### Enunciado
Complete: "If she _____ (study), she _____ (pass) the exam."

### Options
- [ ] A) studies / passes <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Both in present simple - this is zero conditional (general truth). -->
- [x] B) studies / will pass <!-- feedback: Correct. First conditional: if + present, will + base verb. -->
- [ ] C) studied / would pass <!-- feedback: Incorrect. That's second conditional (unlikely situation). -->
- [ ] D) will study / passes <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Will" cannot go in the if-clause. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
Estructura del first conditional: If + present simple (condition), will + base verb (result). "If she studies, she will pass." La condición está en presente, no en futuro. "If she will study" es incorrecto.

### Enunciado
When do we use the past perfect?

### Options
- [ ] A) To describe a single past action. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Simple past is for that. -->
- [x] B) To show that one past action happened before another past action. <!-- feedback: Correct. Past perfect establishes the order of past events. -->
- [ ] C) To talk about future plans in the past. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. That's "was going to." -->

**Bloom:** Analyze
**ICFES:** Razonamiento
**Context:** A student says: "If I will pass the exam, I will be happy."

### Enunciado
Is this sentence correct?

### Options
- [ ] A) Yes, it's perfect. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Will" cannot go in the if-clause. -->
- [x] B) No, it should be: "If I pass the exam, I will be happy." The if-clause takes present simple, not future. <!-- feedback: Correct. Never use "will" in the if-clause of first conditional. -->

## Question 15 [D9]

**ID:** `CO-INGLES-8-2026-P3-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v15`
**Bloom:** Evaluate
**ICFES:** Pensamiento Reflexivo
**Context:** A teacher says: "If you practice English every day, you will be improving your skills. By the end of the year, you will have learned many new structures."

### Enunciado
Why does the teacher use three different future/conditional structures?

### Options
- [ ] A) The teacher is showing off grammar knowledge. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Each structure has a specific purpose. -->
- [x] B) Each structure communicates a different time/aspect: first conditional (practice → result), future continuous (gradual process), future perfect (completed by a point in the future). <!-- feedback: Correct. Different tenses for different meanings. -->
- [ ] C) All three structures mean the same thing. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. They express different aspects. -->
- [ ] D) The teacher is confused about tenses. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. The teacher uses them correctly. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
Cada estructura temporal comunica un matiz diferente: first conditional (condición y resultado posible), future continuous (proceso gradual en el futuro), future perfect (acción completada en un punto futuro). Elegir la estructura correcta permite expresar significados precisos.
