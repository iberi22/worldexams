# Bundle Mastery: Passive Voice, Second Conditional, and Narrative Writing

This bundle covers the passive voice (simple present and simple past), the second conditional, and narrative writing connectors.

---

## Question 1 [D3]

**ID:** `CO-INGLES-8-2026-P4-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v1`
**Bloom:** Remember
**ICFES:** Comunicación
**Context:** In Bogotá, a teacher introduces the passive voice.

### Enunciado
How do we form the passive voice in simple present?

### Options
- [ ] A) Subject + verb + object <!-- feedback: Incorrect. That's active voice. -->
- [ ] B) Subject + will be + past participle <!-- feedback: Incorrect. That's future passive. -->
- [x] C) Subject + am/is/are + past participle <!-- feedback: Correct. Simple present passive: am/is/are + past participle. -->
- [ ] D) Subject + was/were + past participle <!-- feedback: Incorrect. That's simple past passive. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
La voz pasiva en simple presente se forma con am/is/are + past participle. El sujeto recibe la acción, no la realiza. Ej: "Coffee is grown in Colombia." (El café es cultivado en Colombia.)

**ID:** `CO-INGLES-8-2026-P4-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v2`
**Bloom:** Remember
**ICFES:** Comunicación
**Context:** In Medellín, students learn about the second conditional.

### Enunciado
What does the second conditional express?

### Options
- [ ] A) A real/possible situation in the future. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. That's first conditional. -->
- [x] B) An unreal/unlikely situation in the present or future. <!-- feedback: Correct. If + past simple, would + base verb. -->
- [ ] C) A past situation that didn't happen. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. That's third conditional. -->
- [ ] D) A general truth. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. That's zero conditional. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
El second conditional expresa situaciones hipotéticas o improbables en el presente/futuro. Estructura: If + past simple, would + base verb. Ej: "If I had a million dollars, I would travel the world." (No tengo un millón, es hipotético.)

## Question 3 [D4]

**ID:** `CO-INGLES-8-2026-P4-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v3`
**Bloom:** Understand
**ICFES:** Comunicación
**Context:** In Cali, a teacher explains when to use passive voice.

### Enunciado
When do we use the passive voice instead of active?

### Options
- [ ] A) When we always want to name the agent. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Passive is used when the agent is unknown, obvious, or unimportant. -->
- [x] B) When the action is more important than who performed it. <!-- feedback: Correct. Passive emphasizes the action or the receiver. -->
- [ ] C) Only in formal writing. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. It's used in many contexts. -->

**Bloom:** Apply
**ICFES:** Indagación
**Context:** A student in Bucaramanga writes about coffee production in Colombia.

### Enunciado
Change to passive: "Farmers grow coffee in Colombia."

### Options
- [ ] A) Coffee grows in Colombia. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. This is active, not passive. -->
- [ ] B) Farmers are grown in Colombia. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Wrong meaning. -->
- [x] C) Coffee is grown in Colombia (by farmers). <!-- feedback: Correct. Is + grown (past participle). -->
- [ ] D) Coffee was grown in Colombia. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Was" is past passive. "Is grown" is present passive, matching the active. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
Para cambiar de activa a pasiva: 1) el objeto (coffee) pasa a ser sujeto, 2) agregamos am/is/are según el sujeto, 3) el verbo va en past participle. "Farmers grow coffee" → "Coffee is grown (by farmers)."

## Question 6 [D5]

**ID:** `CO-INGLES-8-2026-P4-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v6`
**Bloom:** Apply
**ICFES:** Indagación
**Context:** In Pereira, a student uses second conditional.

### Enunciado
Complete: "If I _____ (be) the president, I _____ (improve) education."

### Options
- [ ] A) am / will improve <!-- feedback: Incorrect. That's first conditional (real). -->
- [ ] B) was / improved <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Improved" is past; need "would" + base. -->
- [x] C) were / would improve <!-- feedback: Correct. Second conditional: "were" (subjunctive, not "was") + would + base. -->
- [ ] D) would be / improve <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Wrong structure. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
En second conditional, usamos "were" para todas las personas (no "was"), es el subjuntivo. "If I were president (but I'm not), I would improve education." Estructura: If + past simple, would + base verb.

## Question 7 [D5]

**ID:** `CO-INGLES-8-2026-P4-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v7`
**Bloom:** Understand
**ICFES:** Razonamiento
**Context:** A teacher explains active vs. passive.

### Enunciado
Which sentence is in passive voice?

### Options
- [ ] A) The chef cooked the meal. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Active: subject (chef) does the action. -->
- [ ] B) The meal cooked quickly. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Cooked" is intransitive here. -->
- [x] C) The meal was cooked by the chef. <!-- feedback: Correct. Passive: subject (meal) receives the action. -->
- [ ] D) The chef is cooking. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Active, present continuous. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
Para identificar voz pasiva: buscar "be + past participle". "The meal was cooked" = was (be) + cooked (past participle). El sujeto (meal) no hace la acción, la recibe. El agente puede ir con "by" (by the chef).

## Question 9 [D6]

**ID:** `CO-INGLES-8-2026-P4-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v9`
**Bloom:** Analyze
**ICFES:** Razonamiento
**Context:** A student compares: "If it rains, I will stay home" vs. "If it rained, I would stay home."

### Enunciado
What is the difference between these two sentences?

### Options
- [ ] A) They mean exactly the same. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. They express different degrees of possibility. -->
- [x] B) First sentence (first conditional) = possible situation. Second sentence (second conditional) = less likely or imaginary situation. <!-- feedback: Correct. First = real possibility; second = hypothetical. -->
- [ ] C) The first is correct, the second is wrong. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Both can be correct. -->
- [ ] D) The first is past, the second is present. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Both refer to present/future. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
Primer condicional = situación posible/real (if it rains, I will stay). Segundo condicional = situación hipotética/improbable (if it rained, I would stay — pero no parece que vaya a llover). La diferencia está en la probabilidad.

## Question 11 [D7]

**ID:** `CO-INGLES-8-2026-P4-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v11`
**Bloom:** Analyze
**ICFES:** Razonamiento
**Context:** A student says: "If I would be rich, I would buy a house."

### Enunciado
Is this sentence correct?

### Options
- [ ] A) Yes, it's perfect. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Would" cannot go in the if-clause. -->
- [x] B) No, it should be: "If I were rich, I would buy a house." In second conditional, if-clause uses past simple (were), not "would." <!-- feedback: Correct. Never use "would" in the if-clause. -->
- [ ] C) No, it should be: "If I am rich, I will buy a house." <!-- feedback: Incorrect. That changes to first conditional (real possibility). -->
- [ ] D) Yes, because both "if" and "would" can be together. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. They cannot. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
Error común: poner "would" en la if-clause del condicional. Regla: if-clause usa past simple, result clause usa "would" + base verb. "If I were rich (past simple), I would buy a house (would + base)." Nunca "If I would be."

## Question 13 [D8]

**ID:** `CO-INGLES-8-2026-P4-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v13`
**Bloom:** Analyze
**ICFES:** Razonamiento
**Context:** A student writes: "First, I woke up. However, I had breakfast. Then, I went to school. Finally, I brushed my teeth."

### Enunciado
What is wrong with this narrative?

### Options
- [ ] A) The connectors are correct. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "However" is wrong here. -->
- [x] B) "However" is a contrast connector but the sentence doesn't show contrast. Also, the order of events is illogical (brushing teeth after going to school). <!-- feedback: Correct. "However" should be replaced with "Then" and the sequence should be logical. -->
- [ ] C) "Finally" is in the wrong position. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Finally" is at the end, which is correct. -->
- [ ] D) There are too many connectors. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. The problem is wrong connector and illogical order. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
En una narrativa: 1) usar conectores de secuencia apropiados (no "however" que es de contraste), 2) los eventos deben tener orden lógico. Corregido: "First, I woke up. Then, I brushed my teeth. After that, I had breakfast. Finally, I went to school."

## Question 14 [D8]

**ID:** `CO-INGLES-8-2026-P4-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v14`
**Bloom:** Evaluate
**ICFES:** Razonamiento
**Context:** A student says: "Passive voice is unnecessary. We should always use active voice."

### Enunciado
Is this statement correct?

### Options
- [ ] A) Yes, active voice is always better. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Passive has important uses. -->
- [ ] B) No, passive voice is always better. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Both have their place. -->
- [x] C) No. While active voice is often clearer, passive voice is useful when the agent is unknown, obvious, or when we want to emphasize the receiver of the action. <!-- feedback: Correct. Each voice has its purpose. -->
- [ ] D) Yes, because passive is grammatically incorrect. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Passive is grammatically correct. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
La voz activa es más directa y suele preferirse, pero la pasiva tiene usos importantes: informes académicos (The experiment was conducted), noticias (The president was elected), procesos (Coffee is grown here). Saber ambas voces permite expresarse con precisión.

## Question 15 [D9]

**ID:** `CO-INGLES-8-2026-P4-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v15`
**Bloom:** Evaluate
**ICFES:** Pensamiento Reflexivo
**Context:** A language expert says: "If English learners only studied grammar rules, they would not develop real communication skills. They also need to practice listening, speaking, and writing in context."

### Enunciado
Why does the expert use the second conditional here?

### Options
- [ ] A) To describe a real situation that happened. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Second conditional is for unreal situations. -->
- [ ] B) The expert is making a grammar mistake. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. It's used correctly. -->
- [x] C) To express a hypothetical situation: (if they only studied grammar) the result is imaginary (they would not develop real skills). The expert implies that in reality, learners SHOULD do more than just grammar. <!-- feedback: Correct. Second conditional presents the hypothetical contrast. -->
- [ ] D) Because the expert is unsure about the facts. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. The expert is certain, but uses hypothetical structure to make a point. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
El segundo condicional se usa para contrastar una situación hipotética/imaginaria con la realidad. "If learners only studied grammar (they don't), they would not develop real skills (imaginary result)." La implicación es que los estudiantes deben hacer más que solo gramática.
