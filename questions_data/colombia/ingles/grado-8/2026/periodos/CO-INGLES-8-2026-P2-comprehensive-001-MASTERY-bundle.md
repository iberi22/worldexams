# Bundle Mastery: Present Perfect (Irregular), Might/Could/May, Jobs and Professions

This bundle covers present perfect with irregular verbs, modal verbs (might, could, may) for possibility, and vocabulary related to jobs and professions.

---

## Question 1 [D3]

**ID:** `CO-INGLES-8-2026-P2-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v1`
**Bloom:** Remember
**ICFES:** Comunicación
**Context:** In Bogotá, a teacher asks about irregular past participles.

### Enunciado
What is the past participle of "write"?

### Options
- [ ] A) Wrote <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Wrote" is simple past. -->
- [x] B) Written <!-- feedback: Correct. Write-wrote-written. -->
- [ ] C) Writed <!-- feedback: Incorrect. This form doesn't exist. -->
- [ ] D) Writing <!-- feedback: Incorrect. This is gerund/present participle. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
Verbos irregulares comunes y sus past participles: write-wrote-written, speak-spoke-spoken, eat-ate-eaten, take-took-taken, break-broke-broken, see-saw-seen, go-went-gone.

## Question 3 [D4]

**ID:** `CO-INGLES-8-2026-P2-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v3`
**Bloom:** Understand
**ICFES:** Comunicación
**Context:** In Cali, a teacher explains "might."

### Enunciado
What does "might" express?

### Options
- [ ] A) Obligation <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Must" expresses obligation. -->
- [ ] B) Ability <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Can" expresses ability. -->
- [x] C) Possibility (maybe) <!-- feedback: Correct. "Might" expresses uncertain possibility. -->
- [ ] D) Permission <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "May" can express permission. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
"Might" expresa posibilidad en el presente o futuro, pero con más incertidumbre que "may". "It might rain" = quizás llueva (menos seguro). "It may rain" = puede que llueva (más probable). "Could" también expresa posibilidad.

**ID:** `CO-INGLES-8-2026-P2-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v4`
**Bloom:** Understand
**ICFES:** Comunicación

### Enunciado
Complete: "She _____ never _____ (eat) Colombian food."

### Options
- [ ] A) have never ate <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Have" is for I/you/we/they, and "ate" is simple past. -->
- [x] B) has never eaten <!-- feedback: Correct. "Has" for she + "eaten" (past participle of eat). -->
- [ ] C) has never eated <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Eated" doesn't exist. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
El past participle de "eat" es "eaten" (no "eated", no "ate"). Con "she/he/it" usamos "has". "She has never eaten Colombian food" = Ella nunca ha comido comida colombiana.

## Question 5 [D5]

**ID:** `CO-INGLES-8-2026-P2-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v5`
**Bloom:** Apply
**ICFES:** Indagación
**Context:** A student in Bucaramanga uses "might" to talk about future plans.

### Enunciado
Choose the correct sentence:

### Options
- [ ] A) I might to go to the cinema tonight. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. After modals, use base verb without "to." -->
- [ ] B) I might go to the cinema tonight. <!-- feedback: Correct. "Might" + base verb. -->
- [ ] C) I might going to the cinema tonight. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Going" is not base form. -->
- [ ] D) I might went to the cinema tonight. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Past tense not used after modals. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
Después de modals (might, could, may, can, must, should) siempre usamos el verbo en forma base sin "to". "I might go" = quizás vaya. "Might" indica menos probabilidad que "will" pero más que "might not".

## Question 6 [D5]

**ID:** `CO-INGLES-8-2026-P2-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v6`
**Bloom:** Apply
**ICFES:** Indagación
**Context:** In Pereira, a student talks about what she has done in her career.

### Enunciado
Complete: "I _____ (be) a teacher for ten years."

### Options
- [ ] A) have been <!-- feedback: Correct. "Be" → "been". "Have been" = present perfect. -->
- [ ] B) have be <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Be" is base form, not past participle. -->
- [ ] C) has been <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Has" is for he/she/it. -->
- [ ] D) am being <!-- feedback: Incorrect. This is present continuous. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
El past participle de "be" es "been". "I have been a teacher for ten years" = he sido profesor por 10 años (y aún lo soy). "For" + período de tiempo. "Since" + punto de inicio.

## Question 9 [D6]

**ID:** `CO-INGLES-8-2026-P2-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v9`
**Bloom:** Analyze
**ICFES:** Razonamiento
**Context:** A student compares two sentences: "He could be at home" and "He must be at home."

### Enunciado
What is the difference in meaning?

### Options
- [ ] A) They mean exactly the same. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. They express different levels of certainty. -->
- [x] B) "Could" expresses possibility (maybe), while "must" expresses logical certainty (probably). <!-- feedback: Correct. "Must" for deduction is stronger. -->
- [ ] C) "Could" is past tense, "must" is present. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Both refer to present possibility. -->
- [ ] D) "Must" expresses ability. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Can" expresses ability. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
"Could" y "must" también se usan para deducción: "could be" = podría ser (30-50% seguro), "must be" = debe ser (90-95% seguro). Ej: "He could be sick" (quizás), "He must be sick" (seguramente está enfermo).

## Question 11 [D7]

**ID:** `CO-INGLES-8-2026-P2-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v11`
**Bloom:** Analyze
**ICFES:** Razonamiento
**Context:** A student says: "I have saw that movie."

### Enunciado
Is this sentence correct?

### Options
- [ ] A) Yes, it's perfect. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "Saw" is simple past. -->
- [x] B) No, it should be "I have seen that movie." The past participle of "see" is "seen." <!-- feedback: Correct. See-saw-seen. -->
- [ ] C) No, it should be "I have see that movie." <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "See" is base form. -->
- [ ] D) Yes, because "saw" is the past form. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Present perfect needs past participle, not simple past. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
Error común: confundir simple past con past participle en present perfect. "See" → "saw" (simple past) → "seen" (past participle). "I have seen" (correcto), "I have saw" (incorrecto).

## Question 13 [D8]

**ID:** `CO-INGLES-8-2026-P2-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v13`
**Bloom:** Analyze
**ICFES:** Razonamiento
**Context:** A student reads: "She has broken her arm" and "She broke her arm yesterday."

### Enunciado
Why would a speaker choose the first sentence over the second?

### Options
- [ ] A) The speaker doesn't know when it happened. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Present perfect is chosen when time is not specified or relevant. -->
- [x] B) The speaker wants to emphasize the present result (her arm is still broken now), not when it happened. <!-- feedback: Correct. Present perfect connects past action to present result. -->
- [ ] C) Both sentences are incorrect. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Both can be correct in different contexts. -->
- [ ] D) There is no difference, they are interchangeable. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. They emphasize different things. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
Present perfect enfatiza el resultado presente: "She has broken her arm" → el brazo sigue roto ahora. Simple past enfatiza cuándo ocurrió: "She broke her arm yesterday" → ayer, sin importar su estado actual.

## Question 14 [D8]

**ID:** `CO-INGLES-8-2026-P2-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v14`
**Bloom:** Evaluate
**ICFES:** Razonamiento
**Context:** A student says: "I could to become a doctor."

### Enunciado
Is this sentence correct?

### Options
- [ ] A) Yes, it's correct. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. "To" is incorrect after "could." -->
- [ ] B) No, it should be: "I could become a doctor." Modals are followed by base form without "to." <!-- feedback: Correct. Could + base verb. -->
- [ ] C) No, it should be: "I could became a doctor." <!-- feedback: Incorrect. Past tense not used after modals. -->
- [ ] D) Yes, because "could" is special. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. All modals follow the same rule. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
Todos los modals (could, might, may, can, must, should, will, would) van seguidos de base verb sin "to". Error común: "could to do" → correcto: "could do". "I could become a doctor" = podría convertirme en doctor.

## Question 15 [D9]

**ID:** `CO-INGLES-8-2026-P2-comprehensive-001-MASTERY-v15`
**Bloom:** Evaluate
**ICFES:** Pensamiento Reflexivo
**Context:** A career counselor says: "You might become an engineer, but you must study hard, and you should also gain practical experience."

### Enunciado
Why does the counselor use three different modals in this sentence?

### Options
- [ ] A) The counselor is confused about modals. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. The counselor uses them correctly. -->
- [x] B) Each modal communicates a different level of necessity: "might" indicates possibility (uncertain), "must" indicates obligation (essential), "should" indicates recommendation (advisable). <!-- feedback: Correct. Each modal has a distinct function. -->
- [ ] C) All three modals mean the same thing. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. They express different levels of certainty/necessity. -->
- [ ] D) The counselor uses them randomly. <!-- feedback: Incorrect. The choice is deliberate. -->

### Explicación Pedagógica
Los modals permiten matizar el mensaje: "might" (posibilidad incierta), "must" (obligación/necesidad), "should" (recomendación). Usarlos correctamente permite comunicar con precisión diferentes niveles de certeza, obligación y consejo.
