## Question 1 [D3]
**ID:** CR-ING-11-2026-W05-repaso-p1-v1
**Bloom:** Apply
**EJE:** Linguistic Competence
**Expected_Success:** 0.85
**Contexto:** Last Saturday, Diego visited his grandparents in Heredia and stayed until Sunday evening.

### Enunciado
Choose the sentence with the correct verb form.

### Opciones
- [x] D) Last Saturday Diego visited his grandparents and he stayed there until Sunday evening.
  <!-- feedback: A finished action in the past needs the past simple in both clauses, so 'visited' and 'stayed' are right. -->
- [ ] A) Last Saturday Diego visit his grandparents and he stay there until Sunday evening.
  <!-- feedback: The past simple is formed with the verb alone in the affirmative, so the bare 'visit' and 'stay' are wrong. -->
- [ ] B) Last Saturday Diego visited his grandparents and he stay there until Sunday evening.
  <!-- feedback: The second clause has its own subject, 'he', so it also requires the past simple 'stayed'. -->
- [ ] C) Last Saturday Diego visited his grandparents and he staying there until Sunday evening.
  <!-- feedback: 'Staying' is a gerund and cannot serve as the verb of the main clause. -->

### Explicacion Pedagogica
Review exercises often return to the same principle from different angles. Here the point is that past simple is a single conjugated form for every person, which is why 'visit' without an ending can never appear after 'Last Saturday'.

### Enunciado

**Bloom:** Understand
**EJE:** Linguistic Competence
**Expected_Success:** 0.8
**Contexto:** A journalist writes: 'The volcano has erupted three times since scientists began monitoring it.'

### Enunciado
Which verb form correctly links a past starting point to a situation that continues now?

### Opciones
- [x] A) has erupted
  <!-- feedback: The present perfect links a past action to the present with 'since', which is the time expression in the sentence. -->
- [ ] B) erupted
  <!-- feedback: The past simple would place the eruptions entirely in the past and would not work with 'since' plus a continuing period. -->
- [ ] C) will erupt
  <!-- feedback: 'Will' refers to the future and contradicts the fact that the eruptions already happened. -->

Time expressions act as clues for the tense: 'since' and 'for' call for the present perfect, 'yesterday' and 'last year' for the past simple, and 'tomorrow' for a future form. Choosing the tense from the time marker is the most reliable exam strategy.
