## Question 1 [D3]
**ID:** CR-ING-11-2026-W20-reading-multimodal-texts-v1
**Bloom:** Apply
**EJE:** Reading Comprehension
**Expected_Success:** 0.85
**Contexto:** A page shows a bar chart of rainfall by month, a photograph of the reservoir, and a caption reading: 'Levels reached their lowest point in March.'

### Enunciado
Which element of the page most directly supports the claim in the caption?

### Opciones
- [x] B) The bar chart, because it shows the monthly distribution of rainfall
  <!-- feedback: A month-by-month distribution identifies March as a distinct point, which is exactly what the caption asserts. -->
- [ ] C) The photograph, because it shows the appearance of the water
  <!-- feedback: A photograph shows a moment visually but cannot establish which month the level was lowest. -->
- [ ] D) The title of the page, because it names the subject
  <!-- feedback: A title names the subject and carries no month-by-month data. -->
- [ ] A) The footnote, because it explains the sources
  <!-- feedback: A footnote explains provenance and does not supply evidence about levels. -->

### Explicacion Pedagogica
A multimodal page is read by asking what each element can and cannot show. Matching a claim to the element that has the resolution to support it is the core skill.

**EJE:** Reading Comprehension
**Expected_Success:** 0.8
**Contexto:** An infographic shows a map with districts shaded in five tones, and the legend labels each tone from lowest to highest.

### Enunciado
What must a reader check before comparing two districts on the map?

### Opciones
- [x] A) That the two districts are shaded with tones that are adjacent in the legend
  <!-- feedback: Five tones classify values into bands, so a difference between two districts means different amounts only within a band. -->
- [ ] B) That both districts appear in the legend
  <!-- feedback: Every tone on a classified map corresponds to a legend entry, and membership is not the variable question. -->
- [ ] C) That the map was printed in colour
  <!-- feedback: The tone can be read in monochrome as distinct patterns, so printing is not the issue. -->
- [ ] D) That the two districts have the same population
  <!-- feedback: Nothing in the map classification depends on population size. -->

### Explicacion Pedagogica
Classified maps carry a hidden quantisation: they group values into bands. A reader who treats a difference between tones as a measured difference is reading more precision than the map provides.
