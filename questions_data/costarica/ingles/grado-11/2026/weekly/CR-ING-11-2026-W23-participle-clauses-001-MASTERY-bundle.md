## Question 1 [D3]
**ID:** CR-ING-11-2026-W23-participle-clauses-001-MASTERY-bundle-v1
**Bloom:** Remember
**EJE:** Linguistic Competence
**Expected_Success:** 0.85
**Contexto:** Sofia, a student in Heredia, waits for the bus to campus.

### Enunciado
Which sentence contains a present participle clause?

### Opciones
- [x] A) Waiting for the bus, Sofia checked her phone again.
  <!-- feedback: Correct. 'Waiting for the bus' is a present participle phrase with an understood subject that shares Sofia with the main clause. -->
- [ ] B) The bus waited while Sofia checked her phone again.
  <!-- feedback: This uses 'while' to build a full subordinate clause, not an -ing clause, so no participle is present. -->
- [ ] C) Sofia was waited for the bus at the corner.
  <!-- feedback: Here 'was waited for' is a broken passive; the subject Sofia cannot be waited for, she waits for the bus. -->
- [ ] D) Sofia checked her phone again and waited for the bus.
  <!-- feedback: Two finite verbs joined by 'and' form a compound clause, and no participle phrase appears here. -->

### Explicacion Pedagogica
A present participle clause is a verb group beginning with -ing that has its own understood subject. It normally shares the subject of the main clause and adds a circumstance, not a second action.

**Contexto:** A student in Alajuela explains ocean currents at a school fair.

### Enunciado
In 'Counting on her fingers, she explained the currents', what does the -ing clause do?

### Opciones
- [ ] A) It tells when she started the explanation.
  <!-- feedback: An -ing phrase placed before the main clause shows something simultaneous with it, not the moment it began. -->
- [ ] B) It explains why she needed a whiteboard.
  <!-- feedback: No cause is stated anywhere in the sentence, so the phrase cannot be the reason a whiteboard was needed. -->
- [x] C) It adds a detail about how she explained the currents.
  <!-- feedback: Correct. A fronted -ing phrase with the same subject describes the manner or accompanying action of the main verb. -->

  <!-- feedback: The listeners are never named, and the phrase attaches to 'she' as its subject rather than to any people. -->

### Explicacion Pedagogica
When an -ing clause stands before the main clause, its implied subject is the subject of the main verb. It then adds manner, accompaniment or cause, and it never introduces a time.

### Enunciado
In 'the photographs taken in Cartago', the participle phrase is what?

### Opciones
- [ ] A) an active clause meaning the photographs took something
  <!-- feedback: 'Taken' is past participle form, so it is passive; the photographs cannot take anything in this context. -->
- [x] B) a reduced relative clause with passive meaning
  <!-- feedback: Correct. A past participle after a noun is a reduced relative clause in the passive: someone else took the photographs. -->
- [ ] C) a gerund clause serving as the subject of the sentence
  <!-- feedback: The noun 'photographs' is the subject of the sentence, not a gerund phrase, and no -ing form appears at all. -->

A past participle placed after a noun is a reduced relative clause in the passive voice. The noun is the object of the participle, so the doer stays unnamed unless a 'by' phrase restores it.

### Enunciado
Which sentence shows the exams happening before the form is sent?

### Opciones

  <!-- feedback: Correct. 'Having finished' is the perfect participle, so it marks an action completed before the main verb began. -->

The perfect participle 'having' plus past participle places one action strictly before another in time. It is required when the earlier action must be shown as complete before the main clause starts.
