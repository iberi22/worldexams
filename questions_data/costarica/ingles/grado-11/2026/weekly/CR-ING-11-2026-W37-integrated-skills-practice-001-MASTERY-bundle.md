  <!-- feedback: Skimming gives the general idea of a text, but it can easily miss a small figure. -->

**Bloom:** Apply
**EJE:** Linguistic Competence
**Expected_Success:** 0.85
**Contexto:** Sofia must join two facts from a report into one sentence for her presentation.

### Enunciado
Which sentence correctly combines the two facts with the right connector?

### Opciones
- [x] A) Recycling saves raw materials, so less waste is sent to the rivers.
  <!-- feedback: Correct: 'so' links a cause to its result, which is the real relation between these two facts. -->
- [ ] B) Recycling saves raw materials, because less waste is sent to the rivers.
  <!-- feedback: 'Because' makes the second clause the reason, but less waste is the effect, not the reason. -->
- [ ] C) Recycling saves raw materials, so less waste was sent to the rivers.
  <!-- feedback: 'So' needs the present simple for a general truth, and the shift to the past breaks the link. -->
- [ ] D) Recycling saves raw materials, and less waste is sent to the rivers.
  <!-- feedback: 'And' only lists two facts and signals no cause, so the connection the speaker wants disappears. -->

### Explicacion Pedagogica
Connectors must match the real logical relation. A result follows a cause and takes 'so', a reason comes before its effect and takes 'because', and plain 'and' hides any link at all.
